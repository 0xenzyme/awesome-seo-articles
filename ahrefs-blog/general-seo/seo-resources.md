# 75 SEO Resources I (Probably) Can’t Live Without

I’ve worked in SEO for just over 12 years.

In that time, I’ve built up a trusty vault of tried-and-tested SEO resources that I use, which I’ll share with you today. Hopefully, you’ll find them useful (…and maybe even bookmark this page).

## Free SEO tools

When you start your SEO career, it’s natural to gravitate to free tools—they’re usually the first SEO resource you search for. But the truth is that not all [free SEO tools](https://ahrefs.com/free-seo-tools) are created equal – some are great, and some are *not* so great. So you need to be careful.

Here are the ones I use:

1. [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) – For many people Google Search Console, or “GSC” is the first tool you connect to your website to help diagnose technical issues and assess performance from Google’s perspective. It’s where a lot of my analysis has started off over the years.

![Google Search Console Screenshot](https://ahrefs.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/google-search-console-screenshot.png)

2. [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/analytics/) – Although GA4 is not nearly as popular as its predecessors, Google Analytics is a necessary part of [SEO reporting](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-reporting/), and it’s a useful tool for seeing what is going on outside of organic search.

3. [Ahrefs Webmaster Tools](https://ahrefs.com/webmaster-tools) – Ahrefs WMT is the best (free) way to get a taste of Ahrefs. Setup is fast if you already have access to GSC, and in a couple of clicks, you can start improving your website’s SEO performance. I used it while working in an agency to run a quick audit of clients’ sites.

4. [Screaming Frog](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/) – If you want to audit a website, Screaming Frog is one of the best tools for small sites, as you can use it in list mode for free or up to 500 URLs in crawling mode.

5. [Bing WMT](https://www.bing.com/webmaster/tools/contentremovalform/) – Bing’s version of GSC is one of the best ways to gain insights into how your website performs and diagnose website issues.

6. [Ahrefs’ Free SEO Tools](https://ahrefs.com/free-seo-tools) – Ahrefs has eight tools that you can check out completely for free. Covering everything from keyword research and link building to SERP and ranking checkers.

7. [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/) – Allows you to understand trends of certain keywords. This type of data is especially interesting if you work in [ecommerce SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ecommerce-seo/), as it can help you track product category trends and compare them against each other.

8. [Pinterest Trends](https://trends.pinterest.com/) – If you are working with a company that gets traction on Pinterest then Pinterest Trends might be a more useful tool than Google Trends.

9. [Google Collab](https://colab.google/) – A [Jupyter notebook](https://jupyter.org/)-style platform that requires no setup and is the easiest way to run a Python script. Google Collab is great if you work in an agency, as you can run small Python scripts without asking the IT department for admin access every five minutes.

10. [Chrome DevTools](https://www.google.com/intl/en_uk/chrome/dev/)—DevTools is built into the Chrome browser and is one of the best free tools for diagnosing technical or performance issues with websites.

11. [Page Speed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) – Helps you diagnose issues with [Core Web Vitals](https://ahrefs.com/blog/core-web-vitals/) and other SEO performance issues.

12. [Google Looker Studio](https://lookerstudio.google.com/) – Formerly known as Google Data Studio, this is a dashboarding platform for creating free SEO dashboards.

13. [Google Keyword Planner](https://ads.google.com/intl/en_uk/home/tools/keyword-planner/) – Google’s tool to help you identify the search volume of keywords.

14. [Webpagetest.org](https://webpagetest.org) – In years gone by, I’ve seen this used by the team at Google. It helps measure the speed of your website and create a loading animation video to show the speed difference between competitors.

15. [Dentsu’s technical SEO tools](https://technicalseo.com/tools/) – Dentsu has a suite of 17 free SEO tools that you can use for free, covering everything from hreflang tag testers, [XML sitemap](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-create-a-sitemap/) generators, [schema markup](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-markup/) generators, and much more. Formerly run by Merkle this has always been a useful resource for free SEO tools.

16. [Keyword Shitter](https://keywordshitter.com/) – As the name implies, if you need to mass-generate keywords quickly, then this is the tool to do it.

17. [Hreflang Checker](https://hreflangchecker.com/) – Useful free tool created by [Dan Taylor](https://x.com/taylordanrw/) for checking, yes, you guessed it, hreflang implementation.

## WordPress Plugins

WordPress is one of the [most popular CMSs on the internet](https://trends.builtwith.com/cms), So it makes sense if you’re working in SEO to know your way around some of the most popular SEO plugins.

These are the ones I use most regularly.

18. [Yoast SEO](https://yoast.com/wordpress/plugins/seo/) – Yoast has been considered the number one SEO plugin for many years now. Its strength lies in its ability to simplify a lot of SEO optimization.

19. [RankMath](https://rankmath.com/) – RankMath is another popular WordPress SEO plugin. Like Yoast, it simplifies SEO optimization.

20. [Ahrefs WP Plugin](https://ahrefs.com/wordpress-seo-plugin) – Our tool allows you to run a full content audit on your website and suggests different improvement actions. It integrates with GA and GSC to help provide you with better-targeted keywords and content recommendations.

21. [TablePress](https://tablepress.org/) – If you work with data, you’ll need to use a table in WordPress. One of the most SEO-friendly ways to do this is using a plugin like TablePress.

## Browser Extensions

I am a bit of a browser extension junkie, so this list may be longer than you were expecting. If you are a beginner, I suggest you start with [Ahrefs SEO Toolbar](https://ahrefs.com/seo-toolbar).

I’ve included some of the lesser-known plugins I’ve used over the years as well.

30. [Hreflang Tag Checker](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hreflang-tag-checker/hjgdcecfiohgajnhilmjhebdganpaomk?hl=en) – If you’re working with an international site, then this is a useful extension to help you check your hreflang tags are correct

31. [Ahrefs SEO Toolbar](https://ahrefs.com/seo-toolbar) – Our toolbar is now one of the most full-featured toolbars out there. When I joined Ahrefs, I had about 20 extensions that I would use. As the toolbar has improved over the last two years, I’ve deleted a lot of the other Chrome extensions I used to use—as the Ahrefs’ toolbar does it all.

32. [Wappalyzer](https://www.wappalyzer.com/) – Useful for understanding the technology behind websites. Doing so is useful for [technical SEO](https://ahrefs.com/seo/technical-seo) as it lets you understand what SEO issues you could encounter.

33. [Linkclump](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/linkclump/lfpjkncokllnfokkgpkobnkbkmelfefj) is a great tool for copying links quickly. I use it to scrape the search results. (Don’t tell Google!)

34. [SEO Render Insight Tool](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/seo-render-insight-tool/ignachbibbeengfepmkeogegpfkigljc?hl=en) – This tool highlights server-side rendered (SSR) vs client-side rendered (CSR) content on websites, I find it useful for spot checks.

35. [FatRank](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/FATRANK/jcnfkjjanbdfabigknbedgkfjkljhbdn?hl=en) – A quick way to check the ranking for any keyword.

36. [Detailed SEO](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/detailed-seo-extension/pfjdepjjfjjahkjfpkcgfmfhmnakjfba?hl=en) – Created by [Glen Allsopp](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/glen-allsopp-63084025), this extension has seven tabs on on-page factors you can use to analyze any website.

37. [Robots Exclusion Checker](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/robots-exclusion-checker/lnadekhdikcpjfnlhnbingbkhkfkddkl?hl=en) – Checks the website’s robots.txt file to ensure specific pages are excluded from search engine indexing.

38. [Word Counter Plus](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/word-counter-plus/fpjegfbcdijjfkceenlfoehpcakfgldj?hl=en) – A browser extension that counts words, characters, and sentences in text. If you write content, then this can be a useful way to check your word count quickly.

39. [SEO Meta in 1 CLICK](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/seo-meta-in-1-click/bjogjfinolnhfhkbipphpdlldadpnmhc) – With over 600k users, this is a popular extension for diagnosing on-page issues.

40. [Scraper](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/scraper/mbigbapnjcgaffohmbkdlecaccepngjd) – A simple but fast way to get data out of websites and into your spreadsheets. I’ve found this useful for assisting with data studies.

41. [Keywords Everywhere](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/keywords-everywhere-keywo/hbapdpeemoojbophdfndmlgdhppljgmp) – A browser extension that provides keyword metrics such as search volume and CPC directly in search results, helping users identify valuable keywords for SEO strategies.

42. [SEO Minion](https://keywordseverywhere.com/seominion/) – Created by Keywords Everywhere, this extension can analyze on-page SEO, check broken links, compare differences between HTML and DOM (rendered HTML), analyze structured data, and more.

43. [SEO Schema Visualizer](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/seo-schema-visualizer/obabcjddknfnjjeblajgnlflppnpgdhi?hl=en) – Allows you to visualize JSON-LD schema markup in a click.

51. [SEO: The Complete Guide For Beginners Guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo) – The Ahrefs’ Beginner’s Guide to SEO is the first place to start your SEO journey if you are new to SEO.

## Books

It may sound old-fashioned in our online world, but sometimes, the best way to get an in-depth view of a particular topic is to pick up a book and read it.

Here are the books I recommend.

53. [The Art of SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#1-the-art-of-seo) – This is a very detailed guide to SEO. When I learned SEO, there weren’t many SEO books around, but this was one that I enjoyed reading.

54. [Product-Led SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#2-product-led-seo) – This must-read book by [Eli Schwartz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/schwartze) is for anyone interested in taking their SEO strategy to the next level.

55. [Feck Perfection](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#4-feck-perfection) – A book that helps you tackle communicating more complex SEO projects and shift your mindset to create marketing that resonates more with your audience.

56. [Information Architecture for the World Wide Web](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#8-information-architecture-for-the-world-wide-web) – This book goes deep into the science behind structuring content on a site. It will most likely appear to [enterprise SEOs](https://ahrefs.com/enterprise) or anyone working on a large website.

57. [Data-Driven SEO With Python](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#3-data-driven-seo-with-python) – This book is useful for SEOs who want to expand their knowledge further and incorporate tools like Python into their SEO and data research projects.

58. [The Link Building Book](https://aira.net/link-building-book/) – I think every SEO should read this book as it offers practical ways to build links that anyone can do.

59. [Entity SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-books/#12-entity-seo) – Written by [Dixon Jones](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/dixonjones), it’s an interesting deep dive into Entity SEO. This will be of more interest to more experienced SEO professionals.

60. [Ahrefs SEO Book For Beginners](https://ahrefs.com/seo/book) – Our beginner’s book to SEO is the perfect way to start learning about SEO in my opinion. It’s also useful if you are an SEO team lead and you want to quickly train new starters on the basics of SEO and how to use Ahrefs effectively.

## News

Over the past 10 or so years, outside of social media, three websites have been the main sources of information for SEO news and insights.

65. [SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/) – Founded by [Barry Schwartz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rustybrick) in 2003, Search Engine Roundtable reports regularly on SEO, Google’s algorithm updates, [SEO techniques](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-techniques/), and more. It is the OG of SEO reporting.

66. [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/) – Search Engine Journal was founded by Loren Baker in 2003, it covers topics related to SEO, content marketing, and social media.

67. [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/) – Founded by [Danny Sullivan](https://twitter.com/dannysullivan?lang=en) and Chris Sherman in 2006, SEL has been one of the first places I’ve checked to see what’s happening in SEO.
