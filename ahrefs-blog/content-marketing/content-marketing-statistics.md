# 105 Hand-Picked Content Marketing Statistics for 2026 Planning

Welcome to the 2026 edition of our curated list of content marketing statistics.

Each year, we pick, vet, and categorize a list of up-to-date statistics to give you insight from hundreds of marketers on trends, best practices, or simply things that work in content marketing.

This article draws exclusively from original data sources, featuring some of this year’s latest statistics alongside a few older but still relevant findings.

This year’s collection shows a clear trend: content marketing is changing faster than ever. Short videos are now the top choice for consumers, long articles are losing attention, AI content is everywhere, and new tools that once took years to catch on are being adopted by marketers almost instantly.

## Top content marketing statistics

AI has officially gone mainstream—no turning back. Most marketers now rely on it to create content, and it’s showing up across nearly all top-ranking pages. At the same time, consumer tastes have shifted dramatically—short videos have become the go-to way people consume content.

1. 86.5% of top-ranking pages contain some amount of AI-generated content.

2. The top investment area for marketers in 2026 will be AI-powered marketing tools ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/b2b-research/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research), 2025).

## Career satisfaction and salaries

Content marketers are earning more than ever, but job security has slipped, with many experiencing layoffs over the past year. And despite the pay increases, money isn’t what makes people happiest in these roles. Creative freedom and the quality of the work matter far more, which is why only about half of marketers feel satisfied with their compensation.

24. The most influential factors of whether or not someone is satisfied with their career are the quality of the work they’re producing and whether it allows them to produce creatively fulfilling work. ([The Content Studio](https://www.thecontentstudio.com/the-state-of-discontent/), 2024)

25. The average total annual income of a content marketer is $111,891. This is up 0.48% over last year. ([Superpath](https://www.superpath.co/blog/content-marketing-salary-report), 2025)
26. Full-time content marketers make more on average than freelancers ($113,276 vs. $108,319). A switch in places since last year. ([Superpath](https://www.superpath.co/blog/content-marketing-salary-report), 2025)
27. 13% of content marketers were laid off in 2024. ([Superpath](https://www.superpath.co/blog/content-marketing-salary-report), 2025)
28. 49.24% of the content marketing community reports being “satisfied” or “very satisfied” with their salary. Meanwhile, 26.46% of people report feeling neutral, and 24.30% feel dissatisfied. ([The Content Studio](https://www.thecontentstudio.com/the-state-of-discontent/), 2024)

And a large portion of searches are for brands people already recognize, making it even tougher for new content to stand out.

## Final thoughts

Some of the data reveals an interesting paradox: we know exactly what works, yet most marketers still won’t do it. Long-form content with visuals, influencer collaboration, original research, and consistent analytics are proven winners, yet they remain the least commonly used tactics. Meanwhile, AI adoption hit 87% in under two years because it makes things easier, not necessarily better.
