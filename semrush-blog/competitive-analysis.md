# What is competitive analysis? How to do one (+ template)

A competitive analysis tells you how rivals attract, convince, and keep the customers you're trying to win — but the surfaces where that competition plays out have expanded.

Prospects are forming opinions about your brand inside AI search platforms before they ever visit your site. Standard competitive analyses miss this. They cover competitors' websites, marketing, and reputation, but stop short of AI visibility, which increasingly drives buyer decisions.

In this article, you’ll learn what competitive analysis is, how to run one across all three surfaces of a competitor's brand, and how to spot where competitors are winning in AI search. You'll also get a free template to organize your findings.

## What is competitive analysis?

Competitive analysis is the process of researching and documenting competitors’ business strategies (like their product, marketing, and sales strategies) so you can use those insights to sharpen your own approach. It's also called competition analysis or competitive market analysis.

In 2026, a complete competitive analysis covers three surfaces:

- What the brand says about itself through its site’s marketing and sales content
- What third parties say about the brand through user reviews, press coverage, and community discussions
- What AI search platforms say about the brand, including if and how often it’s mentioned, the sentiment of those mentions, and which prompts it shows up in

The first two surfaces are familiar territory. The third is newer, and it shapes which brands prospects consider before they ever reach a website.

## Types of competitors

To get the most valuable insights from competitive analysis, you need to identify and analyze the right competitors.

There are three types of competitors: direct, indirect, and aspirational. Each plays a different role in your strategy, and shows up differently across the three surfaces of competitor analysis.

### Direct competitors

Direct competitors target similar audiences and offer similar products or services. Analyzing them should be your top priority because they’re the easiest to learn from and compare against.

Say you run a food delivery app targeting urban professionals. DoorDash is a direct competitor because it delivers restaurant meals to the same audience.

### Indirect competitors

Indirect competitors offer different products or services but solve the same core need. They're worth analyzing after direct competitors, as changes in their strategies often signal broader market shifts that affect demand.

For a food delivery app, meal kit services like HelloFresh are indirect competitors — they reach busy people who want home-cooked meals, but with ingredient kits rather than restaurant meals delivered ready to eat.

### Aspirational competitors

Aspirational competitors are industry leaders you admire and model your strategy after, even if you're not competing for the same customers. Study them when you're planning to grow beyond your current business model.

For a food delivery app, Uber is an aspirational competitor. It targets a similar audience and offers a similar service, but its platform also includes ride-hailing and package delivery. These are services the app could add as it scales.

## Benefits of competitor analysis

The benefits of competitor analysis include:

- **Benchmarking your performance**: Comparing your traffic, rankings, conversions, and brand presence against direct rivals shows you where you stand and where you're falling behind
- **Learning from others’ successes and failures**: Studying what worked and didn't work for competitors accelerates your own path forward
- **Identifying AI visibility gaps**: Knowing where competitors appear (and don't appear) in AI-generated answers shows you which on- and off-site signals to strengthen
- **Discovering untapped opportunities**: Identifying market gaps reveals where competitors are overlooking customer segments, marketing channels, or key features
- **Sharpening your positioning**: Understanding how competitors describe themselves and their products helps you differentiate your brand more clearly to your target audience

## How to do a competitive analysis

A competitive analysis is a multi-step process that covers your competitors' websites, third-party coverage, and AI visibility.

By the end, you’ll have a clear picture of how your competitors operate, and where your brand fits in.

### 2. Identify competitors

Identify competitors using a combination of research methods, since no single method gives you a complete view of who you’re up against.

Find competitors by:

- Searching for your product and service types in Google and looking at the top-ranking results
- Asking [AI search platforms](https://www.semrush.com/blog/best-ai-search-engines/) for a list of businesses with similar products or services
- Testing related prompts to see which brands are mentioned or recommended
- Checking third-party content like reviews, listicles, and comparison articles to see which brands buyers research alongside you
- Asking your sales team about other solutions that prospects tend to consider
- Surveying former customers to identify which competitors they switched to
- Reviewing the competitive domains the Semrush [Market Overview](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/traffic/market-overview) tool lists in your “Market Players” report
- Viewing the “Competitors” report in the [Organic Rankings](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/organic/) for your domain

Once you’ve listed competitors, sort them by type: direct, indirect, and aspirational. Then, select three direct competitors to analyze. This way, you’ll gather a range of high-impact insights without getting overwhelmed.

You can add more competitors later, including indirect and aspirational ones, as you extend your analysis.

### 3. Analyze competing products and services

Analyze competing products and services to better position and price your offerings to meet customer needs.

Start with [value propositions](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-a-value-proposition/). Study your competitors' marketing content to understand what problems they solve, how they solve them, and who they help. Compare these value propositions against yours to better differentiate your offerings.

Next, study your competitors’ product or service information to understand:

- **Core features and capabilities**: The main functionalities and unique features your competitors offer. Explore how these features meet (and don’t meet) customer needs, so you can capitalize on market gaps.
- **Pricing**: Review your competitors’ pricing to identify whether they target premium, mid-range, or budget segments. Use this to evaluate the competitiveness of your pricing, especially if you target the same segment.

Record your findings in your competitive analysis report.

### 5. Research your competitors’ sales processes

Research your competitors' sales processes to understand how they connect with prospects throughout the buying journey.

The sales process typically involves four stages:

- **Presenting the offering**: Understand how competitors communicate value and demonstrate their solutions to prospects. For instance, businesses that sell big-ticket items may have dedicated sales teams that work closely with prospects.
- **Following up**: Study how competitors stay in touch with prospects. Tactics include sending automated email reminders and making phone check-ins.
- **Closing**: Look at how competitors convert interested prospects. They might offer discounts, money-back guarantees, or product bundles to encourage purchases.
- **Retention**: Consider how competitors encourage customers to make repeat purchases or stay subscribed to their services. Perhaps they offer a loyalty scheme or exclusive customer content.

Start by engaging with your competitors’ marketing materials: subscribe to their newsletters, request demos, and attend their webinars. As you do this, observe how they communicate, follow up, and guide prospects through each stage.

Pay attention to their messaging style, contact frequency, and the tools or offers they use to build trust and move leads toward a purchase. The messaging competitors use throughout their sales process reinforces the brand associations AI platforms pick up across their site and third-party coverage.

### 6. Study customer feedback and ratings

Studying competitors' customer feedback and ratings reveals opportunities to win business from their customers and others like them.

These same signals also shape [AI visibility](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-visibility/): AI search platforms retrieve signals from user reviews, forum mentions, and third-party coverage when deciding which brands to mention and recommend.

Public sources of customer feedback and ratings include:

- **Review aggregator platforms** like G2 and Trustpilot. See what customers praise competitors for and what they’re unhappy about. Pay attention to recurring themes — if multiple customers complain about the same issue, it’s likely a significant weakness you could capitalize on.
- **Social media platforms** like X (formerly Twitter), Reddit, and LinkedIn. What prospects and customers say about competitors on these platforms can reveal where they shine or fall short. Use tools like Mention or [Brand Monitoring](https://www.semrush.com/apps/brand-monitoring/) to track social mentions.
- **News publications**: Check reputable news outlets for mentions of your competitors, and evaluate both the positive and negative comments. The Brand Monitoring tool can help with this.

Once you've gathered the feedback, use it to refine your positioning around competitor gaps and strengthen your off-site presence by encouraging satisfied customers to leave reviews.

AI search platforms weigh review quality and consistency when forming brand associations, so a strong review presence pays off in AI visibility too.

Document your findings in your competitive analysis report.

### 8. Establish competitors’ strengths and weaknesses

Establish competitors’ strengths and weaknesses based on the detailed information you’ve gathered, so you can evaluate where you stand against them.

A competitor's strengths might include strong product-market fit, effective marketing campaigns, or consistent positive sentiment across reviews and AI answers. Weaknesses might include limited AI visibility, gaps in customer support, or pricing that's misaligned with their market segment.

Document your findings in your competitive analysis report.

### 9. Determine your positioning

Determining your positioning translates everything you've learned about your competitors into a clear angle for your own brand.

Conduct an analysis of your business’s strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats ([SWOT](https://www.semrush.com/blog/swot-analysis-examples/)). Then, define the unique value you offer.

Maybe it’s a feature that solves a pain point more effectively, a superior onboarding experience, or a more flexible pricing structure. These differentiators form the core of your [unique value proposition](https://www.semrush.com/blog/unique-value-proposition/).

Also consider the customer segments you'll target. If you've identified a customer segment your competitors have overlooked, focus your product development and marketing efforts on serving their needs.

Once you've mapped your positioning, consider how it translates to AI search. The attributes AI platforms associate with your competitors, such as their pricing, reliability, and use case fit, are shaped by the same signals you've analyzed throughout this process.

Your positioning should account not just for how you differentiate on your website, but also for how you want AI systems to describe and recommend you.

Record everything in your competitor analysis report.

I ran a basic competitive analysis for a fictional coffee subscription service to give you an idea of how the process works and the insights you can get.

Let’s go through this competitive analysis example, step by step:

### Competitor analysis

First, identify competitors by Googling relevant keywords like “coffee subscription” and noting which sites appeared near the top.

I also looked at coffee-related roundups and reviews, like the Bon Appétit roundup below.

Next, I used Semrush’s [Market Overview](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/traffic/market-overview) tool to find more competitors. Since I didn’t have a domain for my fictional coffee subscription service, I used a competitor’s domain instead.

![Market players report comparing domains by market share, visits, AI traffic, and referrals](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/18/ef/18ef1f54e05e9d793d514e659f2a7dc6/8fabd5d1a87847c56566907ecf99eed6/image.png)

I also ran the same competitor domain through the [Organic Rankings](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/organic/) tool to see if additional competitors surfaced.

![Organic competitors report listing competing coffee domains with keyword overlap and traffic metrics](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/cc/04/cc04e4c7e85a073978890f6d666b3ceb/dc37c091229b286b2de1e73641647d71/image.png)

After confirming that these competitors offered coffee subscriptions, I sorted them into direct, indirect, or aspirational competitors.

I then chose three direct competitors to analyze: Bean Box, Trade Coffee, and Atlas Coffee Club.

### Product strategy analysis

Next, I analyzed my competitors’ product strategies by reviewing their landing pages, frequently asked questions pages, and checkout flows.

I noticed all three competitors offer gift subscriptions, which signals demand and tells me I should offer them too.

![Competitive product comparison table showing features, pricing options, and value propositions for coffee brands](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/28/12/2812bb367ab9f79652c52bbf1234250d/d4b092e6926118563c5ee26179d9e78c/image.png)

Marketing strategy analysis

Analyzing my competitors’ marketing strategies with the [Traffic & Market Toolkit](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/traffic/), I learned my competitors get significant direct traffic — but this is likely driven by existing customers.

Organic search was a key channel (especially for Bean Box), while AI traffic was a largely untapped traffic source. So, I could use [SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-seo/) to attract leads from search engines and AI search platforms. Referrals, email marketing, and paid search could also be good traffic sources.

![Marketing channel comparison table showing traffic share by source across three coffee subscription brands](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/89/7c/897c9d71b177c5d2f21f5027835512db/b9c5944cff9e9c697c651de7c3df1548/image.png)

I reviewed my competitors’ social media accounts and recorded key insights in my competitive analysis report.

The analysis pointed to a few moves:

- **Pursue Instagram marketing with** [**influencer partnerships**](http://semrush.com/blog/influencer-marketing-guide/), since competitors favor this approach
- **Focus video content on TikTok rather than YouTube** — competitors post frequently on TikTok but haven't published YouTube videos in years
- **Skip Facebook and X**, where competitors have stopped posting regularly. Threads is a better option for short-form text content, given Trade Coffee's consistent activity there.

![Social media comparison table analyzing posting activity and follower counts across major platforms](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f6/51/f651ac11389c0160089d78002d5f78e9/1caa650450c8f21a5fef2c006a2bc8bb/image.png)

Finally, I analyzed my competitors’ onsite content.

Bean Box attracts substantial blog traffic even though it hasn’t published new content since 2022. There’s a good opportunity to attract visitors with fresh, [high-quality content](https://www.semrush.com/blog/quality-content/).

![Content marketing comparison table covering blogs, content hubs, and email newsletters by competitor](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e4/66/e4669332534ed5ed18f4268a8709bd97/e1b374e464279a887ee87d818f65b95e/image.png)

Sales strategy analysis

I analyzed my competitors’ sales strategies by exploring their product landing pages, checkout flows, and email newsletters.

All competitors use a quick quiz to help prospects choose the right coffee subscription. The quiz’s interactive nature gets prospects invested in the outcome, so they’re more likely to convert.

For example, here’s part of Atlas Coffee Club’s quiz:

![Atlas Coffee Club subscription quiz with coffee type, grind type, and caffeine preference options](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c9/40/c94089db2bd3f4b446d093031acb093c/8766b045f0a43e33ffbc01178f1a9037/image.png)

All three brands also use special offers to encourage conversions, especially for long-term subscriptions and bulk purchases. Most of these offers go out through email newsletters, which helps them convert leads and re-engage churned customers.

### Feedback analysis

For feedback analysis, I searched for “[competitor] reviews” on Google and reviewed relevant results on third-party review sites, industry blogs, and online communities.

![Google search results for Bean Box reviews with AI Overview and Reddit discussion highlights](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/14/42/1442b29d494034f798477086f11d175c/78d9d5a87ccd9a98d935885f80398a74/image.png)

I paid attention to recurring praises and complaints. Common praises indicate features that drive customer satisfaction, while frequent complaints reveal opportunities to outperform competitors by addressing unsolved gaps or frustrations.

According to the [Visibility Overview](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/overview/) tool, all three competitors have relatively low AI visibility, with the bulk of their AI mentions coming from Google’s AI Overviews. So, investing in [agentic search optimization (ASO)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/agentic-search-optimization-with-semrush/) could be an impactful way to increase brand awareness and sales.

![AI visibility comparison table showing scores, mentions, citations, and LLM distribution by brand](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/2a/21/2a21f5e89b5e56a10a1ddf0faec9f228/e8690946e107d4dfd613d8b4f01a9ca2/image.png)

Getting a Wikipedia page would likely be challenging in the short term. So, to improve my coffee subscription service’s AI visibility, I’d focus on building a Reddit presence and getting featured in Tasting Table’s and Bon Appétit’s product reviews and “best of” listicles.

Since I don’t have a domain to run through [Competitor Research](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/competitor-research/), I used Visibility Overview to research prompts featuring my competitors.

A few prompts, like “Coffee Collection and Cafés” and “How can I order coffee online easily,” featured multiple competitors.

Appearing in AI-generated answers for these prompts is a priority, so I set up the [Prompt Tracking](https://www.semrush.com/position-tracking/) tool to monitor them across my competitors’ domains. I’ll do the same for my domain when it’s ready.

### Determining my positioning

Finally, I determined my positioning by reviewing my findings on each competitor and conducting a SWOT analysis for my brand.

Where competitors excel, I looked for ways to match or exceed those strengths, such as building appeal with Gen Z consumers, a segment in which competitors already have a foothold.

Where competitors were weak, I looked to fill the gaps. The most obvious one: AI visibility.

Prioritizing ASO gives my brand a chance to win AI mentions and recommendations while competitors remain under-invested.

## Create your competitive analysis report

A modern competitive analysis should capture how each rival shows up across all three surfaces: their site, third-party coverage, and AI visibility.

Keep these tips in mind as you fill it out:

- **Base decisions on data**: Let real data — not assumptions or guesses — guide your conclusions to make more impactful decisions

- **Customize your report**: Adjust our competitive analysis template to meet your needs. For example, you might want to analyze competitors’ technology and infrastructure, culture and talent, and business partnerships, which our template doesn’t cover.
