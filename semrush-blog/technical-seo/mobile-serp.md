# Google’s Mobile SERP: Everything You Need to Know

## What Is a Mobile SERP?

A mobile search engine results page (SERP) is the version of the results page that a search engine displays on mobile devices, rather than desktop.

## Is the Google Mobile SERP Important?

It’s important to understand Google’s mobile SERP because it’s the most used search experience of all. [Over 60% of searches](https://www.statista.com/statistics/297137/mobile-share-of-us-organic-search-engine-visits/) now occur on mobile devices. And as of October 2023, [91.55% of searches](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share) happen on Google.

Plus, Google predominantly uses the mobile version of a website to index and rank it within its results. This is known as [mobile-first indexing](https://developers.google.com/search/mobile-sites/mobile-first-indexing).

Once you understand how mobile SERPs work, you’ll be better equipped to optimize your content to perform well on mobile. This can help you win more organic traffic.

## Google Mobile SERP Features

Although they look a little different on mobile devices, [SERP features](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serp-features-guide/) (non-standard elements like those listed below) are mostly the same on mobile as they are on desktop.

They include (but aren’t limited to):

- [Featured snippet](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1328-featured-snippet-serp-feature)
- [Google Ads](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1320-serp-features-ads-top)
- [Local pack](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1340-serp-features-local-pack)
- [Videos](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1353-serp-features-video-results)
- [Top stories](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1350-serp-features-top-stories)
- [Images](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1337-serp-features-images)
- [Knowledge panel](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1339-knowledge-panel-serp-feature)
- [People Also Ask](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1342-serp-features-people-also-ask)
- [Related searches](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1344-serp-features-related-searches)

***Further reading:** [What Are SERP Features? An In-Depth Guide](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serp-features-guide/)*

Here are three differences, though.

### 1. App Packs Only Exist on Mobile

One SERP feature that is unique to mobile is the app pack. If a search implies that the user could be looking for an app, links to the app itself are displayed.

App packs are typically positioned mid-page, below sponsored results, below the first organic result(s), and sometimes below People Also Ask. The exact position varies.

It looks like this:

![App packs shows on iPhone (left) and Android (right)](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/66/65/66658e69d1d262f176ccf6f02dc614df/8e02f06e2087f0271ec178076dc1292d/Hn1mATf7F1VHLOLnmu2wUT-2XCdRNQkMGdaXhOiMzSy8cnZwBCHru9fcWG9G5R63GRLqDp2rq37AGc2XBMX1mzCyKJsOYV6ZgbfqXoE_F0_Qhk1X-164XImPLlW5Mq0ozjvzD2CVgZvuIvR3lhMQXbw.png)

The results are specific to the device’s operating system. Links direct users to the app’s page within the appropriate app store.

### 2. Some SERP Features Are More Prominent

Some mobile SERP features take up a greater percentage of screen space compared to their desktop SERP counterparts.

For example, here’s the featured snippet for “how to prepare an interview” on desktop:

![A featured snippet for “how to prepare an interview” on desktop](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/2e/9a/2e9a87ba6c0591bbe8a7dbda3f2428af/f8915790ec0c49617ca81337f076dfcb/Mixwq9Ri0arhxXTXMDChhfxft1jI10ILI6l7IXQ_lOKR5nIB4iQYdkUxPAelanyCoCSgkB7rOncfwl_4EwerTnLabNojNH1jbFtyuMuqluaaCi9esMIFmWxqhlG3EhSSTKNcf3s2XeK2I-v_LUxv5Ho.png)

Note that the next feature (People Also Ask) is fully visible without scrolling.

On mobile, it looks like this:

![A featured snippet for “how to prepare an interview” on mobile screen](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/34/49/3449ced9828d16683d5ed65eb1b4f81a/715ae962b381b588fc6a08ed621567e7/OMQSsDEW9F70wgXsIeFFVcta7jocqt3dVLSNrGWCxy8XLk1dC4141GX-Grkfwr4KN2ZsmzVlvNBG0y20P0FSA3fD6338nNiaI4cZCd8aGu21RLkycFnltZWIdtix3YBOJl-m8vuWVEE4GLQGJ_ikXk8.png)

Almost the entire screen (before scrolling) is taken up by the featured snippet.

The same is true of local packs, videos, and other features.

Because of their prominence on screen, it’s likely that SERP features on mobile get a greater share of the overall clicks than they do on desktop.

### 3. Some Google SERP Features Appear More (or Less) Often on Mobile

Although most of the Google SERP features exist on both desktop and mobile, they don’t always appear at the same frequencies.

In this Semrush [study on ecommerce SERP features](https://www.semrush.com/blog/product-serp-study/), we found certain features appeared more in desktop SERPs, while others appeared more in mobile SERPs.

For example:

Related products appeared in 99% of desktop SERPs but only 76% of mobile SERPs. The average number of products shown was also higher (five on desktop vs. four on mobile).

![Related products on mobile SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f0/b8/f0b8d8d841868cb7adc527cb31080408/4c20b43c43511610e8bef852051af89f/yn6MXTl8JHDcV7CSqjI6FrvtEFVHmtaEaBApVS1vXvBvhzYJrVvwOmZoJUor42spNqX060lBzOezvshHmTrCnjXSwpsGNXb3L2o8Rndbzo2T0-9Qox2m5T5O5by7AVK7bNpLBs8nQ0z1WwbCH7bLrgA.png)

The buying guide feature (an expandable list of product-related questions) was also more frequently displayed on desktop versus mobile. (7% and 6% of the time, respectively.)

![The buying guide feature on SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c0/17/c0170ddedf46472cf1fe98d5e94aff4b/c2cf6885fe37ef21af69d1308e3a5194/5I2K-SAhL4ZusZ_Xxwt0iveGprJcMLytwamXitzz_4sG5VnTnnOXPLorex-PSUcTmc26h_8ne9eUMvM5dt0rFSWgHpM0wVmjQz6cGpYHi9iSXZ2aLKHkRbzpWU32WxGlQtgzI9DiK2-ejljCXDjhe6E.png)

On the flip side, local packs were seen more frequently on mobile devices (31% vs. 26% on desktop).

![Local pack results on mobile SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/51/5a/515a0b89380f5dbfae472bb593b3cd10/e19614502f7a322dd14b7035b05df976/dpNm4NzAjo2DeNZqFoaj8CcYgsqX_a5kgEoHOnqEraeF5d6sTQq6GCyjDox7cMBQT2k7QkuZEkqFfGj75c6Pl1B_PhZf925solc2fAUlv6krVkTO5PapwoZhAjrvisSw5yAZm3UWUkTyHGYyW0QBC-0.png)

## Google Mobile SERP Design

The mobile SERP adapts to suit smaller screen sizes in various ways.

Here are three unique-to-mobile design elements.

### 1. No Sidebars

Some Google SERP features, like a [knowledge panel](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1339-knowledge-panel-serp-feature), appear in the right sidebar on desktop SERPs.

(A knowledge panel displays a short overview about a topic, brand, or business without requiring the user to click.)

![Semrush knowledge panel displayed in the right sidebar on desktop SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/9f/e8/9fe8880712a79654a459dc7794e5962c/d4eb69817a086b47d98cb5db27f30880/JTMpC63_1vaIh7fwxD3zfVJ0bBl5bSi5R6a46hAkQa3nv0nN-BHTAX8fmSjMoHVa1YcUhCGvne04tQOKUqlCfdiipX0QeDCbOOyKKb6T0K0tpwiYiMivGYDINXfUsTDG4xgkQ-yXonXDQecrHmwWPrA.png)

On mobile, since there isn’t space for two columns, they appear at the top instead.

![Semrush knowledge panel on mobile SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1a/5b/1a5bbece6d04cd069ec781744f765d10/83bbcb292ae8e85436c3b62311dac359/sMaRiu2zZfXLhdSHE7psicX8_zjOwZNSTjmSRx42zHXBIEXE8XCyoXrIlOOFt6Q2nxNoeQ6gFVjVFLqAslHY6MPc95uObXyQvmQPYqRvC1GVbNnan5qzbRAUch4_vbMgAExzrneNQUmAy97vyJrM76k.png)

The same is true for Google Business Profiles. On desktop, the profile appears in the right sidebar:

![Google Business Profile for "The Otter And Fish" appears in the right sidebar on desktop SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ee/50/ee501de4e95cb30af5c6f2dc8e6aa603/77ac28291b24f823b495a1b0dabb5fcf/cx-KHDbP5zsigf3oYnF0PfyfTopZpIUv_v8ZpA2VmMk6h3mjDyDi752DiU1M5pK3_END8ePQsf30-NoCvjODac92R_L44mdPGnqfqguRSc713tGqADva2LZK-lnua8dnpum651fCiKzKcBeEVYJPlZw.png)

While on mobile, the profile appears above the organic results:

![Google Business Profile for "The Otter And Fish" on mobile SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/99/24/9924a04e9ae297f366138d3bb5009808/9490d4e1b3b2abc7c75a05afd802c7ab/xsiVJUa4BIvMbfuU_BxOprwyaNExqhQliTLIVeUe2_lCTojzZvgCw8wVpXUCEy81CCScc0L6moT5qLr9ujK1fByxzHkKPggIMHmXbPlSd8EQMhEd60GRxH0V4bsdueGtLTAB_gZePjEDup88BRuWAh8.png)

### 2. Shorter Meta Descriptions, Longer Title Tags

The anatomy of a regular, organic result is the same on mobile as it is on desktop.

Organic results consist of:

- Website name
- Title tag
- Meta description
- Favicon (logo image)
- URL path
- Sometimes other Google SERP features like [reviews](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1345-serp-features-reviews) (shown below) or [sitelinks](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1349-serp-features-sitelinks#:~:text=Sitelinks%20are%20additional%20links%20below,certain%20webpages%20on%20a%20site.)

However, titles and descriptions display at different maximum lengths on mobile SERPs.

![How title tags and meta descriptions appear on mobile SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ce/2f/ce2f86d80dbb24db8d1e7403bf551054/d5055324cfc1a47376d06486f6191ca6/r6W3UN2zcrICJfF6sfpwthjrAegmHJ5SJwIdwJg57RTXzuecTmdHNzdQxHaeeuDCTptrSGaGmLa-sNW6UzLA9P-RatPmnPQyq6lktmr9NpHVJexmvwH68858WDEPM1KKupUMnCBkt_2zLEJFIhomrms.png)

There’s no actual limit for the number of characters in a title tag or [meta description](https://www.semrush.com/blog/meta-description/). But there is a point after which Google will cut off characters when they’re too long.

Like this:

![An example of title tag and meta description cut off by Google on SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/68/41/6841c8fa815501f1e6923f877f847d20/371f0cd6a3f138c3d91441834e83ed46/9LCG-3LnT4NFBTFUxtNBswN5woSh5oR8hfxtDVeUQiIlqCVHrQwjnv2YmaMJkp0qkhTOyJZAtKFIu9NhOHF_OfHvMTgLUPWjnTGpXfCXE__mPMU6Stb4LEBaC9sDYeh7v8zYiEv5iSbUgJ2hu9pM63Q.png)

On mobile SERPs, the meta descriptions are cut off sooner than they are on desktop.

On desktop, meta descriptions cut off at approximately 960 pixels or 152 characters. On mobile, they cut off after approximately 680 pixels or 105 characters. Which means it’s best to stick with a limit of 105 characters.

And title tags?

Mobile SERPs can technically display longer ones because they can use multiple lines.

Here’s an example of a title tag that is cut off on desktop:

![An example of a title tag that is cut off on desktop](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a2/57/a257e3542acc0cd00c5444e8237f4360/fd48fcd0bb11a0e8633766be72faf712/heNbPNmNIAkd-EvuM35cnkzPD-eWjkdMXR62jumcryjDDvx7jkQw_WjXC_Du0IZNm5eS2IUUy0wadNYstNitZ92xfo_mCDLyVHBszUYnO_m0gGVHpPnZJLupCmd6slZwsOvI-w-GXpHWrYnpBqpxBmg.png)

But not on mobile:

![An example of a title tag not cut off on mobile SERP, while it was cut off on desktop](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/27/20/2720d002dadbe4e64d887af66bc107fd/611c0da905eac98c20a95e887a62025b/vZtozXkr9ODY9qom3nHv-46xyUXaeuoodB2xJrn__2PnA5XyST1tLxJ9d1UAoBGF8aK2e0KLKV2hUypr7nu45eFzNGcHrm7SMK9IBRwyVsfCzK6f7LVTkj2zc4NGoxYhDK26Xd4MldR0UPCTxRifr8w.png)

Title tags are cut off after around 600 pixels on desktop, which is about 60 characters. While they can be longer on mobile devices, it’s best to stick with that threshold to ensure your title tags don’t get truncated on any device.

## Changes to the Google Mobile SERP Design

Google regularly makes small tweaks to the mobile SERP design. And often runs experiments.

Here are three notable changes to the mobile SERP that impacted user experience.

### 1. Google’s 2021 Mobile SERP Redesign

One significant design update from early 2021 was [well documented](https://blog.google/products/search/mobile-redesign-2021/).

Key changes included:

- Larger, bolder text for accessibility and easier scanning
- Edge-to-edge results for more visual space and a modern look
- Rounded icons and corners, aligning with Google’s branding

![Google’s 2021 Mobile SERP redesign](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/66/60/666019614dd6b2ab1ba9d1cbfa7d9382/995d55c3de70d4613fe9dfa3b66797f9/N5GBFtpMzQTAPYOOxZkLOtjw_6YH37AwRYZcvzNLXaGC4mE3RZkttEVoxchzjWvYAKKBRnysj9m9JLBZY1b1C02bS4hpzcjIBQEEnOAvyDLD1P56j5-VOrOVPpRwirCd8yK2it7hb6nbd3mVwSTL180.png)

Image Source: [Google](https://blog.google/products/search/mobile-redesign-2021/)

### 2. Continuous Scrolling

Google removed pagination from their SERPs. Instead of tapping “See More,” “Next,” or a page number after 10 organic results, more now load automatically.

As a result, it’s likely that results in position 11 or lower get a small increase in visibility.

This change was introduced first to mobile in late 2021. It rolled out on desktop in late 2022.

### 3. Adding Website Names and Favicons

Prior to 2019, branding wasn’t clear for organic listings.

After a [Google SERP design update](https://blog.google/products/search/new-design-google-search/) (which came to mobile first), two new elements were added:

- Website name
- Favicon (logo image)

It’s useful for searchers to see at a glance who is the publisher of the content. That way, they can quickly identify familiar brands. Or assess credibility.
