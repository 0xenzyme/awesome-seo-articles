# What Is a Subdomain? A Beginner’s Guide

## What Is a Subdomain?

A subdomain is an extension of your main domain that helps you organize content and expand your business offerings.

For example, if your main domain is "example.com," a subdomain would look like "blog.example.com" or "shop.example.com." The part before the main domain (like "blog" or "shop") is the subdomain.

Key benefits of using subdomains include:

- **Better organization**: Keep different types of content separate and easy to navigate
- **Enhanced user experience**: Create tailored designs and functionality for specific purposes
- **Brand expansion**: Launch new products or services while maintaining brand recognition
- **Testing flexibility**: Experiment with new features without affecting your main site

Lots of companies use subdomains. Here are a few examples:

- support.apple.com - Apple uses this subdomain for customer support and troubleshooting guides
- developers.google.com - Google uses this subdomain for developer resources and API documentation
- help.shopify.com - Shopify uses this subdomain for customer support and help articles
- news.microsoft.com - Microsoft uses this subdomain for official press releases, company updates, and sharing customer success stories

Note that subdomains aren't necessary for every website, but they’re useful when you need to isolate certain business functions or create specialized user experiences that differ from your main site.

In this guide, we’ll cover everything you need to know about subdomains.

## Understanding URL Structure

Your URL has several parts. The protocol ([HTTP or HTTPS](https://www.semrush.com/blog/http-vs-https/)) comes before the subdomain. This is the file path that transfers data from a web server to your browser so it can load the website.

The root domain comes after the subdomain. It’s made up of two parts:

- The second-level domain (SLD) is the name of your website (typically your brand or business name)
- The [top-level domain](https://www.semrush.com/blog/top-level-domains/) (TLD) is the end of your domain. For example, “.com” or “.org”

Top-level domains and subdomains serve different functions in your URL structure—the top-level domain identifies your website's category or location, while subdomains create separate sections within your site.

![an infographic showing all parts of a URL](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/fa/70/fa70fafcbf91927caa27d6d418d83aa1/DAbuObld0fGkGEbqKHvk1WfxpvwMZyEKjjCq5lQHI9Ho9QaVDVNbGDjo3JmBz0C1myWtRCpWL7ntbOsP3PIaag-ypFsFZuab-mEGyzTtToDS1771QzBnZXwujfHFXrNCsCsScBKkqVwdzN0R9gSE0-Q.png)

The most common subdomain is “www” or “world wide web.” The subdirectory  points users to the main directory of a website—in other words, where your homepage and other core pages live.

Other subdomains work to create distinct parts of a site.

For example, Google’s root domain is [www.google.com](https://www.google.com/). This is the address people type into a browser search bar to access Google search.

Over time, Google has grown to include a full suite of products independent of its search engine. To keep these separate, Google uses subdomains such as [docs.google.com](https://docs.google.com/document/u/0/) for Google Docs and [ads.google.com](https://ads.google.com/home/) for Google Ads.

Using subdomains keeps Google’s search engine free from the distraction of other products. The lack of links and unrelated content lets Google focus visitors’ attention on search, which is ultimately what they’re there for.

Subdomains create a separate identity, and provide an opportunity to create a unique website within a website.

For example, Google Docs and Google Ads are unique products. A subdomain lets Google use a different design and content structure to create a clear distinction between them (and other products).

Google markets subdomains independently while keeping them under the umbrella of the primary domain that users know and trust.

## Subdomains vs. Subdirectories: What's the difference?

[Subdomains and subdirectories](https://www.semrush.com/blog/subdomain-vs-subdirectory/) differ in where they appear in your URL and how they function as part of your website.

A subdomain acts as a separate website that can use a different design or CMS from your main domain, while a subdirectory is simply a folder that sits inside your main domain.

Say you run an online pet food store and want to add a blog to give your audience tips on feeding their pets a wholesome diet.

You can add your blog as a subdirectory by creating a new page in the same way you do for a product or service.

The blog becomes a folder within your primary domain. This means it uses the same design and [content management system](https://www.semrush.com/goodcontent/content-marketing-blog/content-management/) (CMS) as the rest of your website.

When a user clicks through to your blog, the URL will look like this: **www.mypetstore.com/blog**

Now, let's say you want to expand into a completely different business area. For example, launching a pet training service alongside your pet food store. This content serves a different audience and requires different functionality, so it makes sense to keep it separate.

Creating a subdomain lets you create a blog on a different CMS (e.g., [WordPress](https://wordpress.com/)) with its own interface.

Your training site now sits outside of your main domain and the URL looks like this: **training.mypetstore.com**

A subdomain always sits **before** the root domain in the URL. A subdirectory always sits **after**.

Another slight difference between the two is how they affect the nested structure of your site.

With a subdirectory, you can add subfolders to the main folder that point to different categories. For example, **www.mypetstore.com/nutrition/pets** and then **www.mypetstore.com/nutrition/pets/dogs**.

Because a subdomain acts like a root domain, you can add subdirectories to your subdomain to organize pages.

For example, ads.google.com has “home” as a subdirectory. This helps to organize succeeding pages. In this case, “how it works” follows the subdirectory and creates a clear path that flows from subdomain landing page to subdirectory to content.

!["ads.google.com/home/how-it-works/"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a1/81/a181a19465a664920c01d57d5567cd1a/dh2jljSj0DHiqZ9IJpsOs8PqiYmP_8AyF8Vzr3IE3yFnCvnrlgEufVVoMjaQEK6nM9eblnlc9xV-w50ZgE5h5sV-giPUsUwg97aFw7jM_92qCBq4F9IkcoJpoV_qxleMS0KhYgR7b2TtZ5KXX0lHUB4.png)

This URL structure promotes [easy navigation](https://www.semrush.com/blog/website-structure/) for users and search engines.

### Should You Use a Subdomain or Subdirectory?

Subdomains are best for content that is too distinct from your main website. For example, if the topic has a different purpose or requires a specific CMS or design.

The clear separation prevents user confusion and stops you from sending mixed messages to search engines about your content.

Subdirectories are best for content that is relevant to your main website.

For example, if your website is **www.mypetstore.com**, it makes sense to place dog food in a subdirectory (e.g., www.mypetstore.com/dog-food) as it fits with the main site.

## When to Use Subdomains

Subdomains give webpages a unique identity while keeping them part of your main website. There are a few instances in which you can benefit from using them:

### Creating a Blog

If your target blog topics and [keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-research/) don’t relate to the rest of your website, using a subdomain lets you engage visitors with different content.

For example, Fiverr’s main website focuses on its marketplace of freelancer services.

Its [blog](https://blog.fiverr.com/) is a separate entity that features a variety of topics, such as company news and business tips. Fiverr hosts its blog on a subdomain, as seen in the screenshot below.

!["blog.fiverr.com"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/64/c2/64c2394c20dcda57046ddd5a79602c3f/Dr0SWazDO-l4hS9fGyBTmrgGjCjTe0qNgQpVy1zxV-uNyee2AvCozxz8LDAd7uIZUm1DrOaW0y7vO00rZjDcCKouhiNpDuVuR92RNb6H6UC2EEGsKZbYys-OfTLGl83FX6bKpSHb3IPJYiJr7JQxQJA.jpeg)

This allows Fiverr to organize its main site around the marketplace and grow its [content marketing](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-content-marketing/) without distracting from its central selling point (freelancer services).

### Adding an Ecommerce Store

A subdomain allows you to add [ecommerce](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ecommerce-seo/) functionality to a website. It also lets you keep your merchandise store separate from your main offering.

Say your website started as a blog, but you’ve grown your audience to the point of selling products to monetize it.

With a subdomain, you can create an online store using an ecommerce platform (e.g., adding [Shopify](https://www.semrush.com/blog/shopify-vs-wordpress/) or WooCommerce as a subdomain of a WordPress blog).

Hubspot, for example, is an inbound marketing platform that has expanded to offer a range of branded apparel.

!["shop.hubspot.com/Category"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/99/98/9998e373b2d7440e1b70d01a58b2fc78/uaa-XgteqxfEZ6aisdzVpOwACBpoRE2vhqPSoqK5oZhGOD50in1-t_55lYlBpIiY1x_tY0uqtQ3-pCk5VRYZJHciEwriydZpLm7lWxCzzOLyTZKVueYjLT6vTM4r85boKHswUJ4C5tf3nhdO5JKGgZA.jpeg)

A subdomain allows the company to market products and process transactions without having to change the code or functionality of its main site. It can also sell apparel without distracting from its main offer (marketing software).

### Testing or Building a New Website

Subdomains let you test a new version of an existing website or individual pages before they go live. So you can trial new features and fix bugs without affecting the functionality of the current site.

They also provide staging platforms for new websites. For example, Squarespace’s website builder hosts newly created sites on a separate subdomain.

!["cylinder-cone-fjrd.squarespace.com"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f7/90/f790ae7b6a07441057ec183e6a0cb819/39Zxf0wfikDhV5Qdwo3zZSDTpygXumqkJ29Vj4KeiUzqquM7aWD3PM3A4LVOXzl6zUFaXdWSqBcGMilKQMmM-WbTtCvO_osCsuXAU-0nkVjnl_jOj31WRPUV3WW3UK95wlNUbOdmPIpFlh0Kw2bWq4I.png)

With this, users can build a website for free before committing. After a free trial ends, they can sign up to a Squarespace plan and either buy a domain or leave the site on the subdomain.

### Optimizing for Mobile

Many new small business websites are [responsive](https://www.semrush.com/blog/mobile-or-responsive-which-site-is-right-for-you/) out of the box so that content adapts to each user’s chosen device (e.g., mobile, tablet, desktop, etc.).

But if you’re adding mobile functionality to an existing website built for desktop, a dedicated mobile subdomain makes it easier to create a tailored experience. Without having to overhaul your entire site.

Facebook, for example, defaults to the [m.facebook.com](https://m.facebook.com/) subdomain for users visiting on a mobile browser.

!["m.facebook.com/watch/" subdomain](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/19/7e/197ed83c88839320b3bd46f66d4464dd/HoYWM4BB8L9GcEAh-p0LnsPAEwZqVwKDddCTFBvM4EvDBAbIGmsO0uJ5LvPRD_QdcJtXWNxkb3cxYZOueg_znqG2Onyr9OluDSbBM0ye3wtOLkNnKQCHRDJYHMYFRwlLXL-Mz_HdlAz9QBNZ9Wd32Vg.png)

Instead of simply scaling down its desktop site, Facebook uses a subdomain to create a mobile-specific version. The site mimics the functionality of its app for easy scrolling, tapping, and typing.

This removes the potential frustration of users trying to navigate a desktop site on a smaller screen, and creates a positive user experience.

It also means Facebook keeps code for the two versions of the site separate. Development teams can work on the mobile site without worrying about the effect of changes on another platform.

### Targeting New Customers or Business Goals

A subdomain lets you create content for goals or [audiences](https://www.semrush.com/blog/target-audience/) that differ from your main website.

Take Lyft. Its ride.lyft.com subdomain homepage is for users who want to get a ride.

!["ride.lyft.com" subdomain homepage](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/45/eb/45eb1ce9a05117c3f9a77c121dd263d0/Xg0YuxE2ovPohXfon1dQfrG-V0G501NhKm7BO-K2vaIfTlsseKVhbYctwvwqPQuW4b89sKzJhHjD80iZMxMwOPmceYnG4Q5NJ7EEQG0U8TO4tKBlVjb6naiuno5qZrrpOSbOFmnWbn1GRbQlU87QXJM.png)

But this page isn’t relevant to visitors who want to learn more about the company’s self-driving cars.

For this audience, Lyft uses a different subdomain landing page.

This way, it keeps different parts of the business organized. Users who want to book a ride don’t land on the site to learn about autonomous cars (and vice versa).

On autonomous.lyft.com, Lyft goes into detail on its technology without distracting from the goal of securing bookings.

### Adding a Help Center or Forum

An in-depth product warrants detailed resources to help users make the most of it. Where an [FAQ section](https://www.semrush.com/blog/faq-pages/) can’t answer all of the questions a customer might have, a subdomain creates a dedicated platform with its own navigation structure.

Take [Shopify’s Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en). It gives users everything they need to get started, troubleshoot problems, and grow their business.

![Shopify’s Help Center](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/03/b6/03b6780c127b3ae4fd7b7888c94ebd11/rdf9MZg35xyx2eKmK3wZ9xtlz0BsJYOIOvI7r29zzfqqAnHP1RU4fv0PChtmfhfmi6vl8baTR44fcjZZsYt6HPYorS0_37Hxerd-1wkgNJIRx5pHCLgaUo3lyLXzDps7UmPG6Jl9_gxq4PiJzkW_oGI.jpeg)

By keeping domains separate, Shopify can dedicate its main site to converting potential customers, while optimizing its subdomain around keywords for current ecommerce users (e.g., “How to add products to a store” or “Using Shopify widgets”).

### Reaching International Audiences

If your business is expanding into a [new country](https://www.semrush.com/blog/category/seo/general-seo/), a subdomain extension gives you a space to create content specific to the new audience.

For example, Yahoo! News uses ca.yahoo.com to curate news for Canadian readers and au.yahoo.com for readers in Australia.

!["au.yahoo.com" subdomain homepage](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/87/23/87230173231c72d80b0ff03bdc74e376/37FeI21Erc25Vuue82gnkJFZBpY-vUKq_mn12diwg0suBX6lJPamDGMnC7dZ8aaIwUHp_fE6D2CIsQNtnMuHDTrazIQVcKB_cA2MxFwHPteuK7CjlAHjm9F-C8iicUceNncO4pZw36DRir2tw_gpxlk.jpeg)

This helps Yahoo! appear more relevant and legitimate to different audiences. Content is reputable and curated from respected local sources, which gives readers more reason to trust it as a news source.

You can create as many subdomain extensions as you need for free when you own your domain name.

This makes it a more cost-effective method of reaching different audiences than buying country-specific domains (e.g., mywebsite.ca, mywebsite.au, and mywebsite.fr).

You can also use subdomains to accommodate different languages. Wikipedia, for instance, makes it easy for users to find information in their native tongue with dedicated subdomains.

For example, fr.wikipedia.org is for French speakers and ja.wikipedia.org is for Japanese speakers.

## Are Subdomains Good for Search Engine Optimization (SEO)?

A well-optimized subdomain can be as beneficial for SEO, just like any other part of your website.

Why?

Because Google understands that subdomains are part of a larger domain and can see the relationship between the two.

Google bots can [find and index pages](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-index/) on a subdomain in the same way they do on a subdirectory.

Subdomains also have several advantages in terms of what Google looks for in an authoritative website.

With a subdomain, you can:

- Move content away from the clutter of your main site for easy navigation
- Reach a wider audience by targeting international markets or different topics
- [Grow authority](https://www.semrush.com/kb/747-authority-score-backlink-scores) in niche markets by going into detail on different subjects
- Target different keywords without distracting from the main purpose of your website

People can easily find relevant content on a subdomain. This helps create a positive [user experience](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ux-design-examples/) and increases engagement.

The [more time visitors spend on your site](https://www.semrush.com/blog/bounce-rate/), the more Google sees it as reputable and valuable.

The result?

Pages that Google prioritizes in [search rankings](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/).

So does this mean subdomains are a better option than subdirectories for SEO?

This is subject to debate.

### Subdomains vs. Subdirectories for SEO

Many SEOs favor subdirectories [over subdomains](https://www.semrush.com/blog/subdomain-vs-subdirectory/#subdomain-or-subdirectory:-which-is-right-for-me).

This is because subdirectories benefit from the authority of the main domain.

When they sit on your website, new pages reap the rewards of the hard work you’ve put into [earning backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) and optimizing your existing content.

The authority from your most popular website pages filters down to the subdirectory, and allows you to achieve higher rankings and organic search performance quicker than a subdomain.

Why?

Because with a subdomain, you’re starting from scratch.

Although Google recognizes that a subdomain is a part of your primary domain, it sees it as a separate website.

This means the subdomain doesn’t benefit from the main website’s authority and vice versa—one of the key SEO implications of using subdomains.

As Google’s [John Mueller explains](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJGDyAN9g-g&t=65s):

> You’ll need to verify subdomains separately in Search Console, make any changes to settings, and track overall performance per subdomain… We do have to learn how to crawl them separately, but for the most part, that’s just a formality for the first few days.

The video is from 2017, but the advice is still relevant today.

Essentially, you have to optimize a subdomain as though it’s a different website, which means working on a blank canvas. With no authority to leverage, seeing positive results will take longer.

And when you do eventually achieve high rankings for your subdomain, the separation means it won’t provide a boost for your primary site like good website optimization of a subdirectory will.

Given these SEO implications of using subdomains, they're best used when the content is different from the rest of your site, but you want to associate it with your brand (e.g., Lyft's autonomous car subdomain).

This lets you optimize for new keywords to secure traffic from a specific audience.

What you don’t want to do is have a subdomain targeting the same keywords as your main site. If you do, you run the risk of [cannibalizing keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-cannibalization-guide/) and competing against yourself in search results.

This can result in your subdomain pulling visitors away from your main website, which may be better set up to convert them.

## How to Create a Subdomain

You can add a new subdomain directly from your hosting account. The exact steps will vary based on the specific hosting service you’re using, so treat the following as a general guide.

First, you'll need to log in to your hosting account and navigate to your control panel (CPanel).

This is typically found in the main account dashboard or can be accessed directly via yourdomain.com/cpanel.

Look for the "Subdomains" option, which is usually located in the "Domains & SSL" section of cPanel. (In some hosting dashboards, it might appear under “Domain Management” or have slightly different wording.)

Click on "**Subdomains**" to open the subdomain management interface. Then, click the "Create a Subdomain" button where you'll enter your desired subdomain prefix.

![Navigating to “Create Subdomain” button in IONOS ](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8b/cc/8bccb1d0da5e7dc4089698d328de38b6/yo0ytda89yrVbBUmDNXbtKdPaW8w3DaHXMMJ86avngLxzP0gv9z2o5ZbByGaZHLAhX8aC-WfG6JYbzG2biIY_xzV07X82yg-G9UGddpuUPeC0RWnJe0aB_P7SVT2PYAiyAINT7ep0-6LczNjfRZnjZk.png)

Type the name of your subdomain prefix.

Keep this related to the type of subdomain you want to create. For example, “Shop” to add an ecommerce site or “Help” for a help center.

If you’re creating a subdomain to target a new language, use the correct country code top-level domain (ccTLD). You’ll find a full list of these on the [World Standards website](https://www.worldstandards.eu/other/tlds/).

This helps prevent confusion for users. It also makes it easier to keep content organized for SEO analysis and auditing.

!["Create Subdomain" pop-up window](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b6/95/b695c569952f4a51891728b3ac76fcea/ugEHd3Aa3PN77ZR1MA4WzhVenqVRqAt6HbIg0eLZB2OhPYLhB3YeQ6IOQADC3oaJr_E43a-OQrenMSte3PCt4bzXWaax0HAou9TMJ0P4vNdeXSWyeW5HjlSYXNCRPYV8x0ZvqUWfOiBr39eN1MpuFiM.png)

When you’re happy with your subdomain name, click “**Save**.”

That’s it. Your hosting provider adds the subdomain to your domain name system (DNS) settings.

It can take up to 48 hours for your domain registrar to fully implement the subdomain. Otherwise, you’re good to go.

Your new domain will appear under “**Subdomains**” in your domain name system (DNS) settings.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Subdomains

### What Are Some Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Subdomains?

Subdomains open up new possibilities for your site, but there are some mistakes you’ll want to avoid when using subdomains:

- **Poor naming conventions**: Using confusing or unrelated subdomain names that don't clearly indicate their purpose. Keep names descriptive and intuitive (like "shop.yoursite.com" rather than "s1.yoursite.com").
- **Inconsistent branding**: A subdomain should seamlessly integrate with your overall brand identity. Failing to carry over key design elements, colors, and fonts creates confusion and weakens brand recognition.
- **Poor** [**internal linking**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/internal-links/): Not linking between your subdomain and main site can hurt discoverability
- **Neglecting mobile optimization**: Every subdomain needs to be responsive, just like your main domain

### Are There Any Limitations to Using Subdomains?

While subdomains are powerful, they come with some trade-offs:

- **Separate SEO treatment**: Subdomains don't necessarily inherit the authority (ranking power) of your main domain. You essentially start from scratch with SEO efforts, which means slower initial growth compared to subdirectories.
- **Extra analytics setup**: You'll need to set up tracking for each subdomain individually in tools like [Google Analytics](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-analytics/) and [Search Console](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-search-console/) to monitor its performance
- **Additional maintenance**: Each subdomain requires separate content management, regular monitoring, and routine backups, increasing your team’s workload

### How Much Does It Cost to Set Up a Subdomain?

Creating subdomains is typically free. Your domain registrar and hosting provider usually allow you to create them at no extra charge as part of your hosting plan.

However, there are indirect costs to consider:

- Development and design work for the subdomain’s layout and features
- Costs associated with the content management system (CMS) and any paid plugins you might use
- SSL certificates for securing each subdomain (if your hosting plan doesn’t include a wildcard SSL)
- Other recurring expenses related to maintenance

### What Are Some Tools to Manage And Monitor Subdomains Effectively?

There are several tools that can help you manage and monitor your subdomains to ensure they're performing optimally:

- **Sucuri**: To scan subdomains for malware, vulnerabilities, and security threats
- **Cloudflare**:  To enable DDoS attack protection, monitor traffic patterns, and speed up content delivery for subdomains
- **GTmetrix**: To test and track page speed performance for each subdomain
- **Google Search Console**: To track indexing status, monitor search performance, and identify SEO issues for each subdomain
- **Google Analytics**: To measure traffic, user behavior, and conversions across subdomains
- **Semrush**: To audit SEO health, monitor keyword rankings, track backlinks, and uncover new keyword opportunities for your subdomain

### What Are The Security Implications of Using Subdomains?

Subdomains introduce several security considerations that require careful attention.

Each subdomain is a potential entry point for attackers, so ensure all subdomains have SSL certificates, keep your CMS and plugins updated, implement proper access controls, and monitor for vulnerabilities. A compromised subdomain can put your entire domain at risk.

### How Do Subdomains Affect Website Speed And Performance?

Using subdomains, you can actually improve your website speed and performance.

This is because subdomains can be hosted at different servers or locations. This means any request to your subdomain can be processed by a dedicated server, reducing the load on your main website and improving overall performance.

## Use Subdomains to Engage Different Audiences

If you’re diversifying your offer or expanding into new markets, a subdomain creates an extension of your main site that you can customize for a specific audience.

Adding a subdomain is an easy task, but remember that you need to approach website optimization from the ground up in order to dominate the organic search results for your target keywords.
