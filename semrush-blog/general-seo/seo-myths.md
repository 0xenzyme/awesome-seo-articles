# 12 of the Biggest SEO Myths: Explained & Debunked

No one knows exactly how search engine algorithms work—especially Google’s.

Which is why there are so many SEO myths. From made-up keyword types to claims backed by misinterpreted data.

We’re about to go over some of the main SEO myths right now. So you don’t waste time chasing incorrect tips.

Let’s dive in.

## 1. There’s an Ideal Keyword Density

[Keyword density](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-density/) tells you how many times a keyword appears on a webpage compared to the total number of words on that page. You can calculate it using this formula:

`Keyword density = Number of times target keyword appears in content / Total number of words in content`

So, if you wrote “keyword density” 20 times in a 1,000-word article, the keyword density of your article would be 2%.

SEO experts often prescribe ideal keyword densities. Some say 2% to 3% is ideal. Others say 4% to 5% is what you want to aim for.

**But there is no optimal keyword density.**

Google has said so multiple times. Including in a [2011 video devoted to keyword density](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rk4qgQdp2UA).

Instead of counting how many times you mention a target keyword, focus on writing naturally. And aim to be truly helpful with your content.

But you do need to watch out for keyword stuffing (i.e., using your target keyword so many times that it seems spammy to readers and Google).

## 2. LSI Keywords Boost Search Rankings

Many SEO experts claim that Google uses latent semantic indexing (LSI)—a retrieval method that evaluates the relationships between words within a set of documents—to understand and rank content.

LSI keywords are thought to be words or phrases that are relevant to your target keywords. But aren’t synonyms.

For example, some would say [LSI keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/lsi-keywords/) for “credit score” include “accounts opened” and “credit limit.” But not “credit rating”—a synonym.

This isn’t correct. Google has confirmed that LSI keywords don’t actually exist.

![Google's John Mueller's post on X that reads "There's not such thing as LSI keywords -- anyone who's telling you otherwise is mistaken, sorry."](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ed/5e/ed5eb2db2a3f3d62e290df223831d1c4/aeecb904cc938b9e6a131355924dbe1b/DNGTT9kZMJ6wjw-AfPjuCn0GxN34x0TclOD5R3nhVhlak45eVJrkkrxRX8fSeerFOa03IgNYvjS-3hglMOX8kaW6RGota0e9YmhwFnXUMR-IdA0E_sLab5fqIR2JTbziM5RKy9X50aLvmZ7AauOEDio.png)

But Google **does** care about semantics and [search intent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/).

Google wants you to write genuinely useful and thorough content. And when you cover a topic extensively, you naturally integrate [semantic keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/semantic-keywords/) into the content you create.

## 3. You Should Only Target High-Volume Keywords

High-volume keywords are keywords that get thousands to millions of searches per month. And they’re typically shorter search terms.

For example, “SEO” is a high-volume keyword.

![“SEO” is a high-volume keyword, with 165K monthly searches in US (as shown in Keyword Overview tool)](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f7/ff/f7ff189ceec775126f31fb47f52c8212/b3669e6f0d71bb0ef60ca7fb7379849b/QFrFH_kX2rx9-4eMupjey8CtYxp3r3Gb0UDIoSdq8JLpj9RMQ8_ely3RrZMGGaCYon6bHOCj4MgqvAm8nMoq2w6mxvr7kt3T1Ljp70wRJjCqOjLZ7RQYwf_oAksXDO6-zgAnVd42y6vZAn5Rp9iDWW0.png)

Some self-proclaimed SEO experts claim you should always target high-volume keywords over low-volume keywords. Because the latter don’t generate much traffic.

But high-volume keywords are also usually quite competitive. And often not very specific to your niche.

Low-volume keywords, on the other hand, usually have less competition. And are often more specific to your company.

Let’s say you run a website focused on helping people find the most affordable flights.

The high-volume keyword “cheap flights” gets around 2.2 million searches per month.

![Keyword Overview results for "cheap flights" show a volume of 2.2M and keyword difficulty of 100%](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/80/f1/80f14bf033b5c736043aa9d9d5e2816f/5e1f18034ae0e746d328bc38970c2093/QBbI_1YP2GVoqqg6OKf0LRGVV7CPwLcdR7LA_qKNxBM3_6wCxaicWxK_YRntIAgL2b2pEpZu2DV5E7HTfu75RLePigD_DnPNWtgBmsW3y0LZvEf5xmMBj-0rQLvLR8Y0BVhF0quG2lh6n2BxYw0lsm8.png)

But consider that the top-ranking results for “cheap flights” are from Cheapflights, Kayak, and Skyscanner—sites that will be difficult to outrank.

And also notice the [search intent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/) (the reason behind a search). When you scan the search results, it’s clear that people are interested in buying affordable plane tickets. Not reading some sort of informational guide.

Let’s take a look at a relevant low-volume keyword instead.

The term “are same day flights cheaper” only gets around 110 searches per month.

![Keyword Overview results for "are same day flights cheaper" show a volume of 110 and keyword difficulty of 63%](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/d2/db/d2db338d029ebcfca8a217b9da7ff9a6/ac6a526b99741282241c7d303f30d400/yZFeyBSi0JFNYD1nLv9hWyeCn7nGcfA-1TC3ygSjrbtOYCfrDsltYHviTvT81GrXe-ze44-geLcA3u1VMXHlAY_7Ka8H9ubW0ByHU5InbMyyoFlICycMS_FXK_j_9PdUfnNcBwVuuoB2a0fqbx69Nrk.png)

## 4. Domain Authority Is a Ranking Factor

Domain authority indicates how strong and trustworthy a website is.

But as opposed to what some SEO experts claim, it isn’t a ranking factor. And has no direct impact on rankings.

Google’s John Mueller has explicitly said so.

![Google’s John Mueller post on X saying that domain authority is not a ranking factor](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0d/5f/0d5fe914f93fe61cd67d093b29dbfb22/58fbccf76812d3d08b7396bcce221b60/bbRdZMgd3O1875KtMCf-WOaxXNSYnKYHaBV_lF5h9uVS8LAuo6XyJN33ZR6TGnzJt8YjcmFiLxVomwWaHFQ7u3UuJZVe8By2Oluq0_2wkuJpNn_LFPH1jaz0d3EHxT4xXLe0CzwpOS_3jAlBGhjV2_0.png)

So is domain authority relevant?

It depends on the specific metric. And Semrush’s Authority Score is calculated using [confirmed ranking factors](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-ranking/):

- The quality and quantity of backlinks
- The estimated monthly organic traffic
- Any spam indicators that indicate an attempt to manipulate rankings

## 5. Google Always Penalizes Duplicate Content

Duplicate content is any content that is a page that’s exactly the same or nearly the same as another page. That could mean multiple pages on different sites or multiple pages on the same site.

Here’s an example of what duplicate content can look ilke:

![What duplicate and slightly rewritten content looks like](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/5c/df/5cdf0c4e2ea8aa04b4e24ab8fac38c38/e5cbff338f301ee72a20c10aafe3a997/pqns4h9N52MoDYUkVJPnskHnA0OiFTvtDyLUuR7m_mzTz-kXr5sAcH_Y3UFVUwRFaxmD4dI0bKAXqCFTE_YVQ9EE4af4c7BoIQofV69qOMm6pt1i7ZOs5JUr9RlBVuUKeaUCQMZgoWZU8rg7wi5n_Fo.png)

The problem with having duplicate content on your own site is that it can hurt your SEO performance when Google isn’t sure which result is the “main” one that should be shown in search results. Which can lead to lower or even no rankings for those pages.

So, it’s still a good idea to avoid duplicate content as much as possible.

## 6. Long-Form Content Always Performs Better

Some SEO experts claim that long-form content performs better on Google.

After all, articles longer than 1,100 words tend to perform better in organic search, according to our [2023 State of Content Marketing Report](https://www.semrush.com/goodcontent/state-of-content-marketing/report/#the_anatomy_of_high_quality_content).

![Text length graphic showing word count for high, medium and low text length](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f9/41/f941b4e4f934918d8fcf114c227db990/730f41db7ec46c9b77ac2425a693424c/ub4hPhBhZKMeeA0EjOZP6hEPQ4BCHVby3WN3J2Du9FoxemHIhc2HtTNWLhsB-XxH5jw-tPaVo6QvNYWZ38bB_mNV23rXUfsmVppoe9-Eu5Fq_m7B_YU7NNPZnXSn-rNaYfJpFBlnvmFQch3peqN9I8Q.png)

But it’s an SEO myth that longer articles rank better due to their length.

A 2,000-word article that covers all the wrong subtopics probably won’t rank. But a 1,000-word article that exhaustively covers a certain topic likely will.

In other words, covering the topic comprehensively and in a way that matches search intent is what helps content rank well. Not word count.

Apart from meeting search intent and being comprehensive, high-quality articles also have the following elements:

- A title that effectively summarizes the article
- Logical headings and subheadings
- Accurate, up-to-date information
- Primary and secondary keywords that are incorporated naturally
- A scannable structure with images, bullet points, lists, and white space

## 7. More Content Is Always Better

There’s an undeniable correlation between publishing frequency and content performance.

Our [2023 State of Content Marketing Report](https://www.semrush.com/goodcontent/state-of-content-marketing/report/) shows that almost 25% of high-performing blogs publish content every day. And low-performing ones tend to publish less frequently.

![A graph showing median publishing frequency - impact on performance](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0c/73/0c7339f055692e9e14cb5e6f17b6c771/ff50ea021de9031a36b5dc7fa883ac63/32x2yXFXUFwLwGhZiEmPAw4jfpkUWH7p9mLoSNAF9Iw-5YC9AorFDrG5iRkwcXQIRF3QWv__pnkX-7OqNdRvQBg9j4KB29w-83EbJzPLyIoRQiqGpbtuSrSoemKlw2S5KWxtHE_8-rZ6WJr4GHtgNUk.png)

But that doesn’t mean publishing often itself leads to better results.

And Google Search Central held [a webinar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKoriHae71w&list=PLKoqnv2vTMUN6lFDz6qMBsz7-Jm8YRV9H&index=3) to discredit this SEO myth. And clarified that you shouldn’t produce content just for the sake of it.

Instead, your [content strategy](https://www.semrush.com/blog/content-marketing-strategy-guide/#7-steps-to-creating-a-complete-content-strategy) should focus on meeting your audience’s needs and wants.

## 10. All Backlinks Are Good

[Backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-backlinks/) are links that point from one website to another.

![What is a backlink](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/13/46/134683e46eb2d00b0c56d1aabbc03365/3845065185822d286a2dd98dcbcf1c65/gHGRjgl9rDVRA-LcHHGJ0YyCyN7V9chOvTmVxGMv4udGzjtskeylpdqtnrBpdQThd1j6JpMap0_7kWf6L2M6TWSrMHjCNJrA2cyAtrnVAS94e42P6lQuR9FX8zpPeEfvWfo6a-1Fe2O3juzvgfgKuP0.png)

They’re important for SEO because Google sees backlinks as votes of confidence that signal other sites see your content as reliable. Which can lead to better rankings

This has led to the SEO myth that all backlinks are good. And that you need as many as possible.

But not all backlinks are equal.

Some backlinks contain the rel="nofollow" attribute. Which tells Google not to follow the link or pass link equity to the specified page.

Even for standard backlinks that do typically pass equity, the reputability and relevance of the linking site matter.

One backlink from a reputable website in your niche will pass more authority than 10 backlinks from spammy websites that have nothing to do with your business.

![A visual showing how authority is passed for high authority sites vs low authority sites](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0d/88/0d88be9d72ad8b64f06c82e765b8b9a6/76a8f3159191831964d1afa670a02f6c/6_YxFOys7CR5MDdB_nCELch67ZIJfm2TUIg10Yn-vkRBZ_nFo5RlZdsANVGiSah1fjUujZNfPP4NjVYzWKxG4eW9IQz27r2oWE4NNYHsyfHHUqxj-wD-AtpEFBZXGsqPb9GgwrBBEMqKkgZ2eD9OyvA.png)

## How to Separate SEO Facts from Myths

We’ve debunked 12 common SEO myths, but there are more out there.

If you’re unsure of how to tell the difference between what’s true and what’s not, consider the following questions:

- Does the data really show causation or just correlation?
- Who’s sharing this information? And how reputable are they?
- What does Google have to say on the matter? What’s communicated by its [Search Central Blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog), [official X account](https://twitter.com/googlesearchc), or [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/c/GoogleSearchCentral)?
- How old is the information? Does it consider the most recent algorithm updates?

It also helps to have tools that allow you to keep an eye on what changes affect your performance. Like those available in Semrush.
