# Finding SERP Weak Spots: A Guide to Improving Your SEO Rankings

The combination of Google’s latest updates, rising competition, and the surge of zero-click searches are reshaping visibility and the SEO industry.

But where some players lose ground, others have a chance to climb. Gaps in the SERPs (search engine results pages) are opening up every single day, and savvy marketers who know how to spot weak spots in SERPs —outdated content, thin answers, low-quality results—can step in and take over.

In this guide, we’ll break down how to do [SERP analysis](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serp-analysis/) to find weak spots with data-driven insights and real-world examples. Let’s dive in.

## What’s a “Weak Spot” on a Google SERP?

In the context of SEO, a "weak spot" on a Google SERP refers to any issue in the content or technical setup of web pages that rank well but don’t fully meet user needs or follow SEO best practices.

This means that not all top-ranking pages deserve their spot on Google. Many rank due to legacy authority, weak competition, or outdated content—leaving gaps that savvy marketers can exploit. These **weak spots** in the SERPs present opportunities to **outperform existing content and claim valuable rankings**.

Let's go over some real-world examples:

**1. Outdated Content**

Older articles that haven't been updated may still rank high but lack current information. For instance, if you Google “best content marketing trends,” you may expect to find information relevant to 2025. **3. Poorly Optimized Metadata - Title tag or Meta description**

Some top-ranking pages lack optimized title tags and meta descriptions, which can lead to lower click-through rates.

For example, if you Google “ruby on rails mistakes,” this page ranks sixth despite the title not containing any words from the search query.

This oversight could lead less people to click on it, especially since the meta description also lacks the search term.

By crafting compelling and keyword-rich metadata, you can increase your chances of higher rankings and better user engagement.

**4. Slow Loading Speeds and Poor User Experience (UX)**

Pages that load slowly or aren't mobile-friendly can frustrate users, leading to higher bounce rates. For example, a website that takes several seconds to load on a mobile device may deter users from continuing their search.

By optimizing your site's performance and ensuring a responsive design, you can improve your rankings over such competitors.

**5. Lack of Structured Data**

Your competitors who don’t use schema markup are missing out on the benefits of [rich snippets](https://www.semrush.com/blog/rich-snippets/) in search results. For instance, a recipe blog without structured data will not display cooking times or ratings in the SERPs, making it less appealing to users. Implementing structured data can enhance your search visibility and increase click-through rates.

By identifying these weak spots, you can develop a targeted SEO strategy to improve your site's rankings and visibility.

## How to Find SERP Weak Spots Manually

The simplest way to find ranking opportunities is through careful analysis of current search results. Let's walk through this process using "best coffee machines" as our example.

When you look at the search results for this term, check out the date it was published. Notice how many top-ranking pages don’t have the most current information about the best coffee machine products available in 2025.

This is a weak spot! Searchers will want the most up-to-date product recommendations rather than outdated content.

By researching the current market for coffee machines and providing a better, more current listicle of products, you can improve your ranking positions for this particular SERP.

At the same time, you can provide higher-quality information to potential customers searching for these products.

Next, look closely at the title tags of ranking pages. In our example, only one page in the top 10 results actually includes the exact phrase "best coffee machines" in its title.
