# Keyword Mapping for SEO: Guide + Free Template

## What Is Keyword Mapping?

Keyword mapping is an SEO technique that involves assigning target keywords to specific pages on your site and tracking these decisions in a keyword map document.

You can see an example of a keyword map below:

![Section of Semrush Google Sheets template showing various columns and rows with research data related to keyword mapping.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/cc/5b/cc5b9a56d8984d9cc7cb7f1c8d1e71a2/70604e7bcc0a7d7e66bfdb9e31a37f57/AD_4nXcON5D2OrckEUpF9Rk2kf89znuCkdu3Lrnw1eq3HZZWNGpO7zGn2xZEaiv15Up1Ml125bk0Sjo_-Gw7kaYcTrB4guezI8t-k6_aELGYYq9WK-lm1qVtiuM0s31ClrxqDWgTrFd5FA.png)

The goal of keyword mapping is to match each keyword with a page and URL that best satisfies the search intent (what the searcher is trying to achieve).

Why?

Because a page dedicated to a certain keyword and what users are trying to find when they search it has the best chance of ranking highly in [search engine results pages (SERPs)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serp/).

## Why Is Keyword Mapping Important in SEO?

Keyword mapping is important in SEO because it helps optimize your site structure and content, and it can maximize the total number of keywords your site ranks for.

Google looks for keywords (search terms) on your page to determine relevance. If Google can’t find the keyword on your page, ranking may be more difficult.

If Google detects the same keyword on multiple pages with a similar purpose (i.e., duplicate or near-duplicate pages), it may struggle to identify the best result. And multiple pages focused on the same term leads to [keyword cannibalization](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-cannibalization-guide/) and can harm ranking potential.

By mapping keywords to their most relevant pages—or by creating new pages when needed—you can:

- Better satisfy search intent
- Prevent keyword cannibalization
- Identify and fill content gaps on your site

Keyword mapping may take time initially but provides long-term time-saving benefits, like preventing the need to re-optimize pages later.

Mapping can lead to higher visibility in relevant SERPs, as long as the content is high quality.

### 2. Identify Your Site’s Topic Areas

Your site’s topic areas depend on your niche and your products or services.

For example, a dog training website might focus on topics such as:

- Puppy training
- Dog behaviors
- Dog treats
- Training collars
- Agility training

Each broad topic area has many [related keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/related-keywords/) for possible optimization. These areas make good starting points for building your keyword map.

[Google’s Helpful Content ranking system](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) prioritizes content that demonstrates expertise and experience, meaning you should choose topic areas related to your industry and your knowledge.

### 3. Group Keywords into Topic and Keyword Clusters

A topic cluster is a group of related pages interconnected by internal links that includes a [pillar page](https://www.semrush.com/blog/pillar-page/) (the main page) and related subpages focused on subtopics.

Both pillar pages and subpages can be created around a keyword cluster—a group of similar search terms that can be targeted on the same page.

Use the [Keyword Strategy Builder](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordmanager/) to form topic and keyword clusters.

- [**Keyword Difficulty (KD %)**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-difficulty/): How difficult it may be to rank for each keyword. Higher scores indicate higher competition.

These metrics can help you decide which topics to include in your keyword map, where they should appear on your site, and what new content to create.

For example, a less authoritative website may focus on topics with lower keyword difficulty to improve ranking chances.

Or, the site may prioritize commercial intent topics (meaning searchers are researching options) to create product pages that can gain visibility without paid ads.

### 5. Assign URLs to Pillar Pages and Subpages

Next, assign a target URL—the page you want to rank for that group of keywords—to each pillar page and subpage in the keyword map.

If no existing page meets the search intent, you'll need to create a new page.

In your keyword mapping spreadsheet, indicate whether the page needs “To optimize” or “To create” in the “Status” column.

### Use Keyword-Rich Anchor Text

Google uses [anchor text](https://www.semrush.com/blog/anchor-text/) (the clickable link text) to understand a linked page’s topic, which means keyword-rich anchor text can establish relevance for target keywords to improve rankings.

Refer to your keyword map when adding [internal links](https://www.semrush.com/blog/internal-links/), and then optimize anchor text with your page’s primary keyword or a secondary keyword.
