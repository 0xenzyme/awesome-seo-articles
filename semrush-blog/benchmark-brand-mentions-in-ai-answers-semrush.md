# Use Semrush to Benchmark Brand Mentions in AI Answers

AI platforms like Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT are shaping buying decisions before customers even visit a website.

When people turn to AI for purchasing guidance, they’re asking the system to make sense of a crowded market and point them in the right direction.

Traditional search handled this by ranking pages and letting buyers compare options themselves through metrics like rankings, impressions, and clicks.

AI answers change the structure of the decision.

![brand mentions in an AI answer](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/fa/40/fa405e7aba8b379ab2b2b6a905111184/fb7de8a054a9c576f2eaa8d00777d208/image.png)

Brand mentions in an AI Overview

Instead of presenting a list to evaluate, the system synthesizes what it knows and names a short set of brands it believes fit best.

![brand mentions in a ChatGPT response](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0c/e5/0ce54a16704063a4ab4aa749cfdd079c/c701f99eb6a258073847edcc9405e24a/image.png)

Brand mentions in a ChatGPT response

If your brand isn't being mentioned in AI answers, another brand often is—meaning you may be losing visibility before a click even happens.

Semrush helps you track when, how, and where these AI platforms mention your brand, and benchmark that visibility against competitors.

In this guide, you’ll learn how to:

- Identify your AI competitors
- Benchmark AI brand mentions
- Measure visibility by topic or campaign
- Analyze sentiment and narrative
- Build an ongoing AI visibility report

The workflows below range from quick baseline checks to deeper competitive analysis, depending on the level of access available.

## 1. Identify Your Brand’s Competitors

Before you can benchmark your [AI mentions](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-mentions/), start by choosing which brands to compare against.

These AI competitors don’t always match your traditional SEO competitors, as AI systems may surface brands based on different signals and narratives.

[Competitor benchmarking](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ins-and-outs-of-competitive-benchmarking/) gives your visibility numbers context. Without the context of the competition, it’s difficult to know whether your AI visibility score means you’re a leader, a peer, or an outlier.

### The Quick Way: Start with Brands You Already Know Are Competitors

You probably already have a short list in mind of your close competitors. For a benchmark, aim to pick 3-5 close competitors who are similar in audience size and cover similar topics and services as you.

With a list of at least 3 competitors, you can start benchmarking in Semrush. If you’re ready, jump to step 2 of this article.

If you don’t know which competitors you should benchmark against, you can use Semrush (even with a free account) to find your top competitors in search.

## 2. Measure Each Competitor’s Brand Mentions in AI Answers

With your comparison set defined, the next step is to figure out how often [AI systems mention each brand](https://www.semrush.com/blog/brand-mentions/).

Keep in mind: counting brand mentions will tell you ***how often a brand gets mentioned***, but not ***why the brand is mentioned***. A mention could be positive, neutral, or even negative.

This is why mention counts should be read as a starting signal, not a performance verdict on their own.

We’ll explore this later in the post, but for now, start by benchmarking the total number of mentions.

Semrush offers several ways to measure AI mentions depending on your access level, from free snapshots to deeper competitive reports.

Free tools are useful for directional checks, while paid reports support deeper trend analysis and competitive comparisons.

## 3. Analyze Where AI Mentions Come from (and Why They Happen)

Mention totals tell you how often brands appear. What they don’t show is how those brands are being described, positioned, or contextualized.

A brand can appear frequently while being framed narrowly, inaccurately, or even unfavorably.

Without narrative context, visibility alone can give a false sense of competitive strength.

Narrative analysis fills that gap.

Looking at which topics trigger mentions and how brands are described shows how AI is positioning a brand, and where that [narrative](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-shaping-your-brand-narrative/) needs to be corrected or reinforced.

To understand what’s driving those patterns, examine the topics and intents behind the mentions. Semrush’s [Prompt Research](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/prompt-research/) and [Brand Performance](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/brand-performance/) tools can help uncover the patterns shaping AI-driven visibility.

## Tips to Increase Your AI Mentions

Once you know how your brand compares, you can adjust your content based on where and how your brand appears in AI answers. Below are several ways to work toward greater AI visibility.

### Make Your Content Easy for AI (and People) to Understand

Google’s AI Overviews are most likely to cite pages that are:

- Well-structured (use headers, lists, short paragraphs)
- Direct (answer the question immediately)
- Helpful (clarify concepts, include definitions, show steps)

This doesn’t mean rewriting all of your content for AI; it means structuring key information so both users and AI systems can interpret it quickly.

For example,to target AI Overviews for a query like *“which glasses are best for round faces,”* an eyewear brand can create a short, scannable page that answers this question.

***Learn more in:***[*How to Rank in AI Overviews*](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews/)

### Reinforce the Brand Qualities You Want AI to Associate With You

AI models learn through consensus. When your brand consistently reinforces the same strengths, like affordability, fast shipping, and sustainability, AI becomes more confident connecting those qualities with your brand.

Consistency builds memorability, for users and AI. This is the essence of LLM seeding, or shaping how AI perceives your brand by repeating key themes across many pages.

For instance, if free home try-on for eyewear is a signature feature, this topic should show up in your product descriptions, FAQs, category pages, etc. When AI sees this benefit repeatedly, it’s more likely to include you in responses to prompts surrounding try-on options.

***Learn more in:***[*LLM Seeding: An AI Search Strategy to Get Mentioned and Cited*](https://www.semrush.com/blog/llm-seeding/)

### Target Topics Your Audience Is Already Using

Semrush’s Prompt Research tool reveals the topics where AI already recommends other brands. These are high-impact opportunities.

For example, if Zenni Optical frequently appears for affordability-related topics such as “best affordable glasses” and “cheap prescription glasses for kids,” and your brand doesn’t, you can create or refine content that speaks directly to those needs.

This could include pages or sections that:

- highlight budget-friendly options
- compare price tiers
- provide clear recommendations for kids’ eyewear
- include a structured section such as **“**Affordable Options for Kids”

Aligning your content to the language people (and AI systems) already use makes it more likely that AI will recognize your brand as relevant and mention you in future answers.
