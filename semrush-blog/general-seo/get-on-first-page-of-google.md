# How to Get on the First Page of Google (Without Paying for Ads)

When your website appears on the first page of Google’s search results, it’s much more likely to get clicks. Few users click through to the second page.

On desktop, the top of the first page might look like this:

![the top of the first Google page on desktop](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e0/6b/e06b127a9069f7981b9a0dcd8a62c982/mh8qUu-cky7IgJFigSnUdnhgiDZ0s9XuSt9SQDTk6lSfLaK8AXTBuGcB31M0ICjNRyUvJxd-OI2hu6r382g-zw9dIwXnb8RCPazd7J4mY7Q_yYzedvY7Q6a-_kxNhBtUEWKnuSXPSCyGHugY0Fc7NOw.png)

When you scroll to the bottom, you’ll see page numbers or a “More results” button:

![bottom of Google search page with “More results” button](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f5/a9/f5a9039c34b6f0c03f57df2909ae9b70/k2mk-UkWZBQQ3ANaX6H_X6YbVAKquRZrB0sc9JUq9wr9zPTb4eoWh4NkXrVrz-QtPKqgcI8WvuG1vMvMGrzlhfKvHAXVsOBbDuEgKSWwW8tJjvskSrZY-Y6DtVhdNjLkoA0oHLs0xI13u3r6PoCDIPw.png)

On mobile, pages are less obvious because Google uses infinite scrolling. In other words, it automatically loads more results when you reach the bottom.

Google’s first page often includes ads.

![Google’s first page including ads](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8b/dc/8bdc78056335f828219bc57fc7b6d92f/Ytt2tBrbmyricr_b9rCModMcEwDfqupkMCN3eA72J9cOwic8ZhASZgZvD0c104G4VMqly8StUWN9M9FlnhyTLkPqiewN56vHf_DKc2wtpGQm_xHk5MW5_ow9FD1wzYPIhIokdYsnllQ4Rw2Hn-DNlS8.png)

However, there are many chances to rank organically (for free).

Typically, the first page includes seven to 10 organic search results:

![examples of organic results on Google’s first page](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/09/de/09de5d9b5649462b9a506cf74af02436/aBQcM4aU1zgvzcTkzDVBCMDhL96hltrVAX6ehZEhEbnzM_tjfuMSfoTPZ9B_WJkDACNAoJcbn6L851AoeRHvj3qM-h4QvFfi3eICfN3ZsVT6d2ECetq9lzPf4M9C_7wJuMN86YEZnMh1-4qp6J744wM.png)

Many search engine results pages (SERPs) have SERP features, which display content in various formats.

For example, the “People also ask” box:

![“People also ask” box](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/bc/76/bc763383fcdd552f9c1a5f1ddb6db1a2/vO03p5hlmoL0m14KXlrFSzWAEvQyBPGbY93R4yMyRvjPL3RtIcIXYJbMVMC3uQJxtIad8D1YDAG8C_IIZUF_Tl1GZc6ByRj5W7EbR52scSr5T75ToIk2fvUDSUQNZs_FTqqgmOoRAaJm654XCCrOYU8.png)

If you want free exposure, you must earn your spot.

Google ranks organic search results according to five main factors:

- **Meaning:** The intent behind the user’s search
- **Relevance:** How well the content satisfies the user’s intent
- **Quality:** The content’s helpfulness
- **Usability:** The page’s functionality and accessibility
- **Context:** The user’s search settings and history

Below, you’ll learn how to get on Google’s first page using beginner-friendly SEO techniques.

## 1. Choose a Target Keyword

A target keyword is the search term you want to rank for. Once you know your target keyword, you can map out how to get on Google’s first page for that term.

Improving existing rankings first is often a good idea, especially for keywords that rank on the second page. If you’re on page two, Google already views your page positively. You just need to outperform the competitors ranking above you.

## 2. Understand Search Intent

[Search intent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/) is the purpose behind a query, or the *why* behind a user’s search.

For instance, **“**toy dog**”** and “dog toy” sound similar, but Google shows different results because users have different goals.

“Toy dog” yields results about a dog breed:

![Google results for a “toy dog” search](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b4/0d/b40d0f91ae31a44292bcc317daed356a/uh7m4fngVWjzrIP4O3FHWO0URi_HwUiacvigezcsLGiOD0DOEBdD4dCLKihxNRfMMlFfb2UC-m7DsYx4v-WpSQ1rA0sEaFR74xogUwOOvIsbjWSbKWGuCZfruL3CGHxqqFIj4xfeNtjs3eSIkx48EX4.jpeg)

### Analyze the SERP with Semrush’s Keyword Overview

Search intent isn’t always obvious. The best way to understand it is to analyze the SERP.

You can search the keyword yourself and review the first page. But Google may [personalize results](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12410098) based on your location, search history, and more.

Semrush’s [Keyword Overview](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordoverview/) provides unbiased data that makes analysis easier.

Enter your keyword, choose the country you want to rank in, then click “**Search**.”

(If you click a keyword in Organic Rankings or elsewhere, this step is done for you.)

![Semrush’s Keyword Overview tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/eb/10/eb104eb9f2939b24e4d0da947d78fe59/SjgELwN5SzI4WzQv-D55GMr6r1liJCKMgoEcZBwfASxlpNxvXKLPuI8RreaDdJ_qa7HI7MEmyj_pFPnVwFPfMnddOb8_SdRsMXVT4nnooLGFXBNwPt0HL_JELOOgJ7TzH6XkNNWIfEca8Fv_3V__RE0.png)

- **Informational:** Users want to learn more about something (e.g., “toy breed dogs”)
- **Navigational:** Users want to find a specific page (e.g., “dog toys amazon”)
- **Commercial:** Users want to do research before making a purchase decision (e.g., “good toys for dogs”)
- **Transactional:** Users want to complete a specific action (e.g., “buy dog toys”)

## 3. Create Quality Content

If you already have a page that aligns with your target keyword’s search intent, update it. Otherwise, create a new page.

To get on Google’s first page, [quality content](https://www.semrush.com/blog/quality-content/) should be:

- Helpful
- Comprehensive
- Accurate
- Trustworthy
- Original

Look at the high-ranking pages to determine the best [content type(s)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/types-of-content-marketing/).

Let’s consider the “good toys for dogs” example.

It predominantly features listicles with product details (and images).

## 4. Optimize for Google

Optimizing for Google involves creating content for humans *and* search engines.

Google [says](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), “SEO can be a helpful activity when it is applied to people-first content …”

In other words:

If you want to rank on the first page of Google, further optimize your people-first content for search engines.

This is [on-page SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/on-page-seo/).

### Add Keywords

Adding keywords to your content is a crucial component of on-page SEO.

Include your target keyword from step one. You can also add related keywords with the same intent (e.g., **“**best dog toys**”** and **“**good toys for dogs**”**).

For example, searching Google with the keywords “best dog toys” and “good toys for dogs” produces similar results.

![Google search results for “best dog toys”](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c3/1e/c31e0272e6801638d21ae1a80273d283/T-qvtQN_4xNt5LjgwgIo665HQCvxM5hIvDA_0JGxrDLiBBW7VXcud311lJwsHo2zINNPWVYzUZnDmbFgJIWgMOjGrpDnXdgnu2YVOOCskJzqzLvRIdogmqu_tZ6DZkpMf55sJUk5pprsCbZADI68Res.png)

You could target both keywords on the same page and potentially get on Google’s first page for both.

One of the easiest ways to find related keywords is to review your competitor’s top-ranking keywords.

Click their URL in Keyword Overview.

![URL from "The Spruce Pets" highlighted in Keyword Overview](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/db/8c/db8c84cb6494ffa4704e7f555990291d/mDpz0lVqoJTIEEQwXEb1RU2ASQRLKY2SAzEoRUPVz1_pSRX9pv5UqyMfuG_Gqt279EohG4L9krZ_1Zw8BPCI5iAKDaiqt5ncPNBmfRlIwpQTiTsY2u9aSxzQhLjKnPAneXoM0k25IOV5jg8UagDP8UY.png)

Or manually search the URL in Semrush’s [Organic Rankings](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/organic/) tool.

## FAQs

### How Long Does It Take to Get on the First Page of Google?

The amount of time it takes a website to rank on the first page of Google varies significantly.

Some pages rank quickly; others take months of improvement.

Rankings change constantly—and [Google algorithm updates](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-algorithm-update/) can shift results. So, focus on creating people-first, long-term SEO content.

Pursue multiple relevant keywords to increase your chances.

See our article on [how long it takes to see SEO results](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/).

### Should I Advertise on the First Page of Google?

Yes, you can advertise on the first page of Google. [Google Ads](https://ads.google.com/home/) allows you to pay for first-page spots.

You pay for Pay-per-click (PPC) only when users click your ads.

![example of product listing ads and PPC ad shown in Google search](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/41/61/416149ef558f1e2bea755ed1841af68d/PYBJ7hsQSUhpdflVOCWbG5OV57jnpoOQ4peSJzfXv0HaZo8zWWqWIRXizfSOMJuQiPCSOOSfVLFT3WJqRHpZ-Y0W8Q8s7g6NKsv0IGrUJteNpGRmLHE56ZfnOgOLURDqpqitLRbugZZoML6RwNpksTg.png)

[PPC](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-ppc/) (or paid search) can be an effective strategy if you do not yet rank organically or want faster results.

Google offers varying ad types, each with different requirements. The right approach depends on your resources and goals.

Check out our [beginner’s guide to advertising on Google](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-advertising/).

### What Are the Click-Through Rates on the First Page of Google?

The average click-through rate (CTR) on the first page of Google ranges from 22.2% to 1.6% on desktop. And 22.4% to 2.3% on mobile, according to the Semrush [State of Search 2023](https://www.semrush.com/blog/state-of-search-2023/) report.

This research supports the idea that higher rankings generate more clicks.

!["Average CTRs for Organic Results" graph](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e1/ef/e1efec42a2190e115a61393b3bee48b8/idydwrL4alGZx0UAnF-pvOMWadd00t45YMVQvzInX-F6yaExLd7vuiDZVWPXfID414ecgIcZPVseSS8DFQ-nYEUaBzjMCjNzneyVoiusjt24m5NMwZifYhOnq0HSGIwu97Oi0hPFVY5Sj8T4Ko1BFis.png)
