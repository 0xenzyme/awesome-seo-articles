# 20 Easy SEO Tips for Higher Rankings and Better AI Visibility

If you want more traffic to your website from search engines, you need to rank higher for keywords that people are searching for.

Below are a few simple search engine optimization (SEO) tips to help you do that.

## 1. Target low-competition keywords

Many things affect ranking difficulty. But if you see pages from similar caliber sites in the top five with few backlinks, that’s usually a sign of a keyword that’s easy to rank for.

Here’s how to find these keywords:

1. Go to Ahrefs’ [Keywords Explorer](https://ahrefs.com/keywords-explorer)
2. Enter a broad topic
3. Go to the **Matching terms** report
4. Set the Keyword Difficulty (KD) filter to ≤10
5. Set the “Lowest DR” filter (top 5) to your site’s DR (you can check this in [Site Explorer](https://ahrefs.com/site-explorer))

![Finding keywords with low-DR websites ranking in the top five in Ahrefs' Keywords Explorer
](https://ahrefs.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/image11-7.png)

For example, the top-ranking page for “ski helmet headphones” has one website linking to it and lives on a DR 19 website:

Internal links can pass [PageRank](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-pagerank/), which can help boost a page’s rankings.

4. Choose “Target page” from the dropdown

## 4. Win more featured snippets

Featured snippets are quick answers in Google search results pulled from a page ranking in the top 10. You can often “shortcut” your way to the top of Google by winning them.

Here’s how to find the easiest opportunities:

1. Go to Ahrefs’ [Site Explorer](https://ahrefs.com/site-explorer)
2. Enter your domain
3. Go to the **Organic keywords** report
4. Filter for keywords in positions #1–5
5. Filter for keywords that trigger featured snippets “where target doesn’t rank”
6. Look for keywords where your page is missing the answer, then add it

## 7. Craft compelling titles

Title tags are a [small but confirmed ranking factor](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/title-tags-are-tiny-ranking-factor/425417/), and Google often uses them for search snippets in the results.

![Example of a title tag in Google's organic search results

Here’s our advice for writing a compelling one:

- **Keep it short** – Ideally under 70 characters.
- **Match search intent** – Show that you have what searchers want.
- **Be descriptive** – Give them what they want.
- **Don’t clickbait** – Focus on overdelivering.
- **Include your keyword** – Or a close variation.

## 8. Refresh outdated content

Rankings rarely last forever, so you need to refresh content periodically to maintain them.

Here’s how to find declining content with [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about):

1. Go to the **Search results** report
2. Click the “Date” filter and select “Compare” mode
3. Choose “Compare last 6 months to previous period”
4. Hit **Apply**
5. Click the “Pages” tab in the table
6. Sort the results by “Clicks Difference” from low to high

![Finding declining content in Google Search Console

From there, look for pages where searchers would expect fresh information and update them.

For example, take [our list of the top Google searches](https://ahrefs.com/blog/top-google-searches/). This page’s rankings almost certainly declined because searchers want an up-to-date list.

## 10. Improve Core Web Vitals (CWV)

Core Web Vitals are site speed metrics Google uses to measure user experience as part of its [Page Experience signals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience).

They’re not a super strong ranking factor, but they can still impact rankings.

The three Core Web Vitals are:

- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)** – Under 2.5 seconds
- **INP (Interaction to Next Paint)** – Under 200ms
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)** – Under 0.1

INP replaced the old FID metric in March 2024 and is now the most commonly failed metric—43% of sites don’t meet the 200ms threshold.

To check if your Core Web Vitals need work, use the **Core Web Vitals** report in Google Search Console or the **Performance** report in Ahrefs’ [Site Audit](https://ahrefs.com/site-audit).

![Checking Core Web Vitals performance in Ahrefs' Site Audit

## 16. Make your content easier to read

If visitors can’t easily scan and understand your content, they’ll bounce. Readability affects both user engagement and how well search engines and AI models understand your page.

Here are quick wins for better readability:

- **Use short paragraphs**: 2-3 sentences max. Big walls of text scare readers away.
- **Add subheadings frequently**: Every 200-300 words. Let readers jump to what they need.
- **Break up text with lists**: Bullet points and numbered lists make information digestible.
- **Write simply**: Aim for an 8th-grade reading level. Tools like Hemingway Editor can help.
- **Front-load important information**: Put the key point at the start of each section (a concept known as [BLUF, or Bottom Line Up-Front](https://ryanlaw.me/bluf-bottom-line-up-front)).

This post you’re reading uses all these techniques. Short sections. Lots of bullets. Clear headings. That’s not an accident.

## 17. Write click-worthy meta descriptions

Meta descriptions don’t directly affect rankings, but they influence whether searchers click your result.

Here’s how to write one that earns clicks:

- **Keep it under 155 characters** – Longer descriptions get truncated.
- **Include your target keyword** – It’ll appear in bold when it matches the search query.
- **Add a clear value proposition** – Why should someone click your result over others?
- **Match the search intent** – If someone wants a guide, tell them it’s a guide.

Google sometimes rewrites meta descriptions, but AI platforms like ChatGPT and Claude use page information like titles and meta descriptions to evaluate the relevance of your page—so the effort is probably still worthwhile.

To find pages missing meta descriptions, run a Site Audit crawl and check the **Content** report for the “Pages without a meta description” issue. (You can even use [Site Audit’s Patches](https://ahrefs.com/patches) feature to automatically write thousands of meta descriptions for you.)

## 18. Optimize your images

Images can drive traffic through Google Images and help your pages rank better by improving user experience.

Quick image optimization wins:

- **Use descriptive file names**: “blue-running-shoes.jpg” beats “IMG\_4521.jpg”.
- **Add alt text**: Describe what’s in the image for accessibility and SEO.
- **Compress images**: Large files slow your page. Use tools like Squoosh or ShortPixel..
- **Choose the right format**: WebP offers better compression than JPG or PNG for most uses.
- **Specify dimensions**: Set width and height to prevent layout shifts (helps CLS).

For product pages and how-to guides, unique images often outperform stock photos. They give searchers a reason to click your result in Google Images.

## 19. Add video where it makes sense

Video can increase time on page, and for some queries, video results dominate the SERP.

You don’t need to create video for every page. Focus on:

- **How-to content**: Showing is often better than telling.
- **Product reviews**: Buyers want to see products in action.
- **Queries where video already ranks**: Check the SERP first.

If you already have YouTube videos, embed them in relevant blog posts. This gives the video more exposure and makes your page more comprehensive.

Keep video loading fast by using lazy loading and hosting on YouTube or Vimeo rather than self-hosting large files.

## 20. Fix basic technical SEO issues

Technical SEO sounds intimidating, but many fixes are straightforward.

Start with these quick wins:

- **Submit a sitemap** – Go to Google Search Console > Sitemaps and submit your XML sitemap
- **Fix crawl errors** – Check the Pages report in Search Console for pages Google can’t access
- **Ensure mobile-friendliness** – Most themes are mobile-responsive now, but test yours with Google’s Mobile-Friendly Test
- **Use HTTPS** – If you’re still on HTTP, get an SSL certificate. Most hosts offer free ones via Let’s Encrypt.
- **Check for duplicate content** – Use canonical tags to point Google to the preferred version of similar pages

Site Audit catches most of these issues automatically. Run a crawl and work through the issues sorted by importance.
