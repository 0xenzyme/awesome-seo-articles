# SEO Results: How to Track & Measure SEO Performance

SEO performance refers to how well a website ranks on search engines (like Google) and attracts organic traffic.

But there are **many** useful ways to monitor SEO results.

## 1. Organic Traffic

[Organic traffic](https://www.semrush.com/blog/organic-traffic/) is the number of visits that come to your site from **organic** (meaning unpaid) search engine results.

It’s a good indicator of how well your webpages rank for relevant [keywords](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-keywords/) (terms and phrases that your ideal audiences search online). And how many visits you get that may eventually translate to more customers.

One way to measure your organic traffic is by using [Google analytics 4](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-search-console/). And heading to “**Reports**” > “**Acquisition**” > “**Traffic acquisition**.”

Like this:

![Navigating to “Traffic acquisition" report in GA4](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c4/5e/c45e3b9c2c6ab9e273cdf6ed1c506577/e8fcbbed18e7a6c76dd1d7d42f8378cf/gGGeCq0Hy76vHJ6oCsQX_8FDNU6AJ2BbWyG8w0gsjK9CMmeh0iHbeDILiK6swxycE-rbsYEKiZVl-40M_qECYbMe5jaUdxZV0Hl6G_P6AKps4Usb0pUSHf-lX3BCmk47vCVEO7cwO6P4hZYKWmSnyog.png)

Then, adjust the report to show data for the last full month. And check the number of sessions (visits) you’ve received in the “Organic Search” row.

![Number of sessions (visits) highlighted in the “Organic Search” row](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/27/8f/278fd9bd76e430120b6cdbcafab6010d/a0bee92904e8081cebb153166ac266b1/7jZZXbgNDGEMui_h3PbmyS7rIVVvesgnVDh1BoLbj7hsTze40tgGI_PGi4jBOkdHhir8z0vfsdOj8d_0lE1UmB03KVDQxLQG07doYn9f-0XSM9rT1RL9Y45sGKPxSE7A_1ZS6rgquhDGxIV8qxQ-e34.png)

To assess how strong your organic traffic performance is, compare it against the main players in your space. Go to [Traffic Analytics](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/traffic/), enter your main competitors’ websites, and click “**Analyze**.”

![Search bar in Traffic Analytics tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/88/d7/88d775a218cee43309b9b8892037f797/3d820148af90fddab72c709a38ef2a78/6D0kZpFM-I03En60C9_wXiOWVt2-nmYwIxrOe2wdHxCNo_7m_G7F2qVhkUcAIUjLvRTqw3RcNUIUPsnwB8q-o5mv2dBQPNJmSXO0oi5WTJtPSeKyGKvWWl9X7_dYzX7l8eY1QZjWHZCnCa1wb-kvGes.png)

Make sure the selected time range matches what you used in Google Analytics.

Then, scroll down to the “Traffic Channels” section and hover over “Organic Search” to see how much organic traffic your competitors get.

![A widget showing competitors' organic search traffic](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/74/d2/74d225dd1db59c8099e0cf95bcae9669/769c6149b402cc8fb1854de5b51dc568/SjokwLhXDDAjbOE53DDiUn6PlKRUh665TUgpjlnOVMZr0yzVazQQYrFxXYhRfAQ42y_zrLpr2Ev3pZtNu9_6jOEIp66rFtO10Le9OX0YZO4OLznLOKkn1H72LNSW9HtUjfTL8VZyPtlAVMfj-toYgSg.png)

Compare these values to your own organic traffic measurement.

If your competitors are getting significantly more traffic from organic search, perform a full competitor analysis (which includes finding search terms they target, analyzing their content, etc.) to figure out why. And come up with a strategy to outperform them.

If you need help, check out our [SEO competitor analysis](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-to-do-seo-competitive-analysis/) guide that teaches you how to do that.

## 2. Keyword Rankings

A keyword ranking is your website’s position on a search engine results page (SERP) for a specific keyword or phrase.

And the higher you rank, the more traffic you’re likely to drive to your site.

But a website’s keyword rankings can frequently change. For any number of reasons.

Perhaps Google updates its algorithm, you lose backlinks, or your competitors update their content to improve it.

So, keeping a watch on your [SEO rankings](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-ranking/) is important.

Losing ranking positions may mean losing traffic to your site—which may hurt your business.

## 3. SERP Visibility

SERP visibility is a measure of how often and how prominently your website appears on search engine results pages for relevant keywords.

It takes keyword rankings into account. But also the presence of [SERP features](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serp-features-guide/)—nontraditional organic results like featured snippets, knowledge panels, image packs, and others.

You can easily check your website’s visibility using the [Position Tracking](https://www.semrush.com/position-tracking/) tool we outlined earlier.

First, you’ll see a “Visibility” index in the dashboard.

![“Visibility” index showing "53.74%"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/16/de/16de39a06e25f77e04e70fccef0bf7e8/79238d733f644269cc7a7870923179a1/SQiY2DlFf2QbZdmQfxfVS82yDTGf_x3AiBLOUsHGKO6hKOi0qDzmlyV8ycE0De-cRHZR1f-px5pTQ9DJ3SF1FtML6sFFLbLjvVfTCAd8sSEwjLdqUNujrgbzghEvg4mJijE1V2PkebiwwJkHy3662jI.png)

This percentage is based on click-through rate and shows your site’s progress in Google’s top 100 results for the keywords in your tracking campaign.

A 0% visibility score means your domain isn’t ranking in Google’s top 100 results for any of your tracked keywords.

And a 100% visibility score means your domain ranks in the first position in the SERP for all your tracked keywords.

The higher your visibility percentage, the better your chances of bringing visitors to your site.

Now, click on the “**Overview**” tab.

![“Overview” tab selected in Position Tracking tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c4/e2/c4e200556349229bed15889d1050b1ec/fc47973076ea23209f9c053114e96d38/3o-tw8TmwyWgNNpZyHSxOjhiFMcLzxCNnwuX6CPcS4np73jPYNunqbfXdfmTQVi34NVd7s0Z5xDC_P85qMBQCEoJ2MhHQGdwrT8IsfFB_IgXJJ290Eijm5iE_g9Cx5gEZxtOSr9dqVAqx7FSo6LXm38.png)

![table with keywords with "visibility" metric highlighted](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/44/f9/44f9a95bed297bfe0a79387e69a1650b/d647be43954de7d8f6a0c4ef54f3be6c/2S54hurQ3AHfXnDEB_9lFE5dq1R8fqVfzHAlBMY1lPoZBkK39QYZEMYqh5fCCfMiJTVL59M7mT6cw0BQxe9cSJXX_DKppQqz1TPseTZFwyRApIr_KDkjDYMC5UM89KwkJDCYfBFTQsJ7AM5mRN3FYfI.png)

- Editing your content to better optimize for the SERP features that are currently appearing in the search results
- Adding [structured data](https://www.semrush.com/blog/schema-markup/) (special code) to your pages to help Google understand and better present your content in the SERPs
- Building more [backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-backlinks/) (links from other websites that point to your pages)

## 4. Impressions

Impressions are the number of times your site was viewed in search results in a given time period.

It’s an important metric for tracking your SEO progress because it gives you an idea of whether your efforts are helping you appear in search results.

Often, the first sign of SEO success is an increase in the number of organic impressions for your website.

To track impressions, go to the “**Search results**” report in Google Search Console.

![“Search results” report in Google Search Console](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b5/b9/b5b9d062b5b4510b6de699cfe08cf4e9/27e16525a1ae586f40fb783198901498/I0WNZtxgIB0YRo-ECpeE0bZBuYA7tvvcM77D-lcY4q3r1GGr2XRW7miX4Qk8zrK4TsTWl9c_EHHxG9UOXZVHBFv8edF0DrfAMnpxr2jmREQBg6DumiozzxSAGDJ-KEo_WkN-aL-1EKqufUbmI3Qdsuo.png)

And you’ll see the total number of impressions your website received in the selected time frame.

## 5. Click-Through Rate

[Click-through rate](https://www.semrush.com/blog/click-through-rate/) (CTR) is the percentage of users who click on your website from the SERP.

It shows how effective your titles and descriptions in SERPs are in attracting searchers’ attention and interest.

And it’s calculated by dividing the number of clicks by the number of impressions, then multiplying that number by 100.

For example, if your site appeared on a SERP 100 times in a month and 10 people clicked on it, your CTR is 10%.

To review your Google CTR, [log in to Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console).

Then, click on “**Search results**” under “Performance.”

!["Search results" report in Google Search Console](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/91/7e/917e57634ba724866d5dc7bc04aafb8d/3575ff073d95264d6dd390c4cf424c62/Nui_Sb9VCdWB6Ja5_engR1D6nkvynvB14aZx7dJe3eWK6_-5kkKXwEqZH8a-mAKl1Ds9uWbKB1ZQkkiJCi1qOkivMQdBAtLcb9LyQz4krRFWdO7CWV6WjFPIeRyJ-aM1-woWfkqxTghDtEUjrwJOAn0.png)

Check the box next to “Average CTR” to see your site’s average click-through rate.

!["Average CTR" box highlighted in Performance on search results report in GSC](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/68/cc/68cc613faa7efded915b5f02b795fbdf/523de94c632a6caed1a6e569280b47d7/JEJoqqS2tAkMI_6vC0kxnPVqAhBV0Uf1RcIs8yokR0HAxTiVa4xXN4WpjbaDll95KPIRA53d8BI_vfIDsRSxO5fGdrVFF3aUPbYvZNAAww_DO9oD4hKQ6mPLdBbM3DEzdsGkLctaFs7JnLpu6ElEn2k.png)

You can also check CTR on a page-by-page basis. Scroll down to the table below and switch to the “**Pages**” tab.

!["Pages" table in GSC showing CTR data on a page-by-page level](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ac/0b/ac0b7d49d850f7e5ee8dacf24778a1d9/7c9e82a1f3449d9896a6b1dd6d2dab20/yg-mckv4Ynd2Fjb5fPB-0GI5xAKsOJ25B9Ix7HQ5NbyHuafMx1B-UHKi22ffedJTWD8_-JuoEWzUFqwW93KqIPbT7BwRQ-Lo-CHMMvRzugMIKAh8WB5BSetoAQZ2nIdWK1pCZo76_oES6chPdZK85aI.png)

To increase your CTR, consider rewriting your [title tags](https://www.semrush.com/blog/title-tag/) (HTML that specifies your page titles and may appear in search results) to include power words (e.g., “best,“ “free,“ “step-by-step,” etc. ), numbers, or brackets.

And make sure each title tag is 55 characters or less so it doesn’t get truncated in search results.

You can also rewrite your meta descriptions (HTML that provides page descriptions and may appear in SERPs) to try to improve your CTR.

So, ensure you’re using phrases like “find out,” “learn more,” and “dive deep” that can nudge users to click through to your pages.

And make sure your descriptions are brief and contain no more than 105 characters each. To avoid truncation.

## 6. Conversions

A conversion is when a user completes a desired action on your site—like subscribing to a newsletter, downloading an ebook, signing up for a trial, making a purchase, etc.

If you have high conversions, it means your pages are effective in turning visitors into leads or customers.

In Google Analytics, conversions are called “key events.”

To track your key events, set them up in [Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/analytics/web/provision/#/provision).

Here’s Google’s [guide on creating and editing key events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12844695?hl=en&ref_topic=13387439&sjid=6020977341809770577-NC) on how to do just that..

And then, you’ll be able to track the organic visits that translate to key events.

To see your key events, go to “**Reports**” > “**Acquisition**” > “**Traffic acquisition**.”

![Navigating to “Traffic acquisition" report in GA4](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/12/0a/120a1d2682ec9264bfc7ac3d079db2b1/7e81664bf9076161d9cc093aed43f175/7O8_CvVk42aAgq1TOJewM80ptTHT7w_7RP-L3UtgO83Un-H9QEI3NhcXcmp-obPsfh9MnCDi2A5W5vs3ESG2nw7Ea9zBeYDC_2xDJGWgYblxvJxkKTj2YSY_yYWgPgsU-VoSMSpP8oredjrXI6azQsU.png)

Now, you’ll see the key event rate for all channels. Including organic search.

!["Key events" column highlighted in the GA4 report](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1e/0f/1e0f9f73b0d3671da0f9fc695cbb4769/42f81a08108b706556f0c961ec1118b8/h08VS9n4Id09a5bkdVO93H1I1ApSblu-Ylg1jkM6JHjO8pVWJDFiBIkIjvXTEv_oVxDYzaqQQ1Iv4ZbXIWE7bqOiUO6mIhbxtgaC8cae6b9WuOtvAVrUbqnzsaRaoRAS0g1t21xy91EC25PnREReQuU.png)

Here are some [conversion optimization](https://www.semrush.com/blog/conversion-rate-optimization/) tips to improve your results:

- Use clear and compelling calls to action (CTAs)
- Simplify forms and checkout processes by only asking for essential information
- Leverage pop-ups to capture visitors before they leave

## 7. Bounce Rate

[Bounce rate](https://www.semrush.com/blog/bounce-rate/) is the percentage of users who visit your site but leave without engaging—like going to another page or filling out a form.

This specific SEO measurement shows how engaging and relevant your content is for the visitors who land on it.

And monitoring bounce rate can help determine if there are any weaknesses in your SEO approach or website—like slow loading speed, poor mobile optimization, a mismatch between content and keywords, and more.

You can check your bounce rate in Google Analytics.

Open the tool and navigate to “**Reports**” > “**Life cycle**” > “**Engagement**” > “**Pages and screens**.”

## 11. Engagement Time

Engagement time (sometimes called time on page) is the average amount of time your webpage was the user’s main focus.

It’s a helpful metric to see how well your content meets search intent. Which is important for SEO.

When a searcher types a keyword into Google, lands on your page, and spends a lot of time there, it suggests that your site satisfied their intent.

They found what they were looking for. And it was useful.

On the other hand, a low time spent on a page can indicate a mismatch between what the user was looking for and what your page contains.

(However, engagement time is not a perfect measure of content usefulness or whether you’ve satisfied search intent. For some queries, engagement time will naturally be low.)

In any case, you can track the average time visitors spend on your site using Google Analytics.

[Log in to your GA4 account](https://analytics.google.com/analytics) and go to “**Reports**” > “**Engagement**” > “**Pages and screens**.” You’ll see the metric under the column “Average engagement time.”

![Average engagement time metric in the “Pages and screens" report in Google Analytics](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f0/c3/f0c3e3ffe863bf1bce7c0d880cd735f3/c0806e46aaa351fa5cdd869cbd160787/4kbB-QNO2HfujQgxnyp6_TR9KIj9wKN76YjLTP6yEYMhvKyE8KJVXO74gPfInajcC_VU4GGVwQX6sAT62hIIVPvIpqN0lnpLg3PiFpuY7XM3dNzGwEbuI5RS2TS68MCzDz-I1hwpbbGAm8picNNSZhQ.png)

Pages that have a notably lower number are worth investigating.

And an easy way to determine search intent is to run the page’s target keyword through [Keyword Overview](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordoverview/).

You’ll see its search intent in the “Intent” box.

![“Intent” box displaying "commercial" intent for "dog food" keyword](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c0/33/c03344876a9c8ff1a265cf5538432a5a/90ab2e8fefc735cdcc2b067e389eee80/0S8RILWYhPC58KV-mffMZRkxAQoBU6WLlkJNEJ3zZPIPvX6Umk9Cwd21T5haJfLkJSp7LLNIB0t7-U8VaL9zwwTHZU4fAm-Bu0XZkqTXdg3u2DjaTbX3ACWsVPPBCqBp1H3LTDrrDZnDM4JLvdQhQM4.png)

Then, ensure your pages’ content meet the right type of intent.

## How Long Does It Take to See SEO Results?

It takes [a noticeable amount of time to see results with SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/).

So, treat SEO as part of an ongoing project that will never be complete.

Your timeline for seeing results will also depend on how new your site is, how good your SEO strategy is, and how consistent you are in implementing your tactics.

If you have all that going for you, expect to start seeing results within four months to a year.

Remember that your website’s visibility and rankings will be heavily influenced by who your competitors are and what keywords you’re trying to rank for.

To know if you’re making progress, track results and refine your strategy as data comes in.

Over time, you’ll see it pay off. Prioritize sustainability over speed. And you’ll enjoy the rewards for longer.
