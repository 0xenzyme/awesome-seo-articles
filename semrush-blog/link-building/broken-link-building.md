# Broken Link Building: What It Is & How to Do It Right in 2025

## What Is Broken Link Building?

Broken link building is the process of finding dead links on other websites and convincing web admins to replace them with working links to your website.

A link is “broken,” “dead,” or “invalid” when it points to a page that’s inaccessible.

Like this:

![an illustration of a broken link leading to "we got lost" page](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a3/fa/a3fa1b6f52f6670c8b67223199d2c8dc/Dc8e6ip6qphV5HJ6gypAzKVvVjnACjKYxXdVRqCPGCbZUv9RPDLu626zX4tP0S30DRW_spQaG3Mo_iCiPMwkiWmMfkmPq2q7IoKCXp-xGpX1jATZALXapDhEh_6O_orCqz2QRAZJwUyzlMy_Watrmrg.png)

Usually, you’ll look for links to external pages that return [404 Not Found](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-does-error-404-not-found-mean/) errors. This [HTTP status code](https://www.semrush.com/blog/http-status-codes/) indicates that the page was deleted. You can offer the linking site an alternative resource to link out to.

However, you can look for other kinds of [broken links](https://www.semrush.com/blog/broken-link/). And work to inherit them.

## What Are the Benefits of Broken Link Building?

Broken link building can be an effective way to earn links from external domains ([known as backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-backlinks/)).

In addition to driving valuable referral traffic, backlinks can benefit your SEO. Because Google treats certain types of backlinks like votes of confidence.

The higher the quality and quantity of backlinks you have, the more authoritative your site is perceived to be. And that can lead to higher search engine rankings.

Broken link building can have a higher success rate than other [link building tactics](https://www.semrush.com/blog/link-building-strategies/). Because you’re helping web admins solve an issue on their site. And providing a reason to link to you.

Plus, the strategy can help you:

- Earn backlinks on pages with established traffic and authority
- Inherit links that previously pointed to competitors
- Generate [link bait](https://www.semrush.com/blog/link-bait/) ideas (i.e., link-worthy content ideas)

### 1. Identify Your Broken Backlinks

Start by finding dead links that were supposed to point to your website in the first place (i.e., broken backlinks).

In these cases, you’re more likely to have a viable alternative link. And the admin is more likely to update the link in your favor.

Find broken backlinks with Semrush [Backlink Audit](https://www.semrush.com/backlink_audit/) tool.

Enter your domain and click “**Start Backlink Audit**.”

Then, follow the prompts to [configure the Backlink Audit tool](https://www.semrush.com/kb/579-configuring-backlink-audit-tool).

Once your backlink audit is ready, go to the “**Audit**” tab.

![“Audit” tab in the Backlink Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1a/ac/1aacbe2bd971dd3912f9e784b33c771c/0-Qmp8iGXzlqBh4niz8yCge-itDhzBV_8IwncmyXGRz2zh8Q81Ij4zDTBEf07zTLPInqV-jruWsXl9NeY_9loZ1hZMWuCjEuB7_vwkNr_26DNFJ1k10qurdfJUld6zsImW9aSF9YZS1QMuv5SpjeFtE.png)

#### Check for Broken Links on Resource Pages

If these pages contain dead links, you might be able to inherit them. (Along with any other links to the broken pages.)

Find resource pages using Google.

[Google search operators](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-search-operators/) can help narrow your search. Here are some templates:

- “useful links” AND [topic]
- [topic] intitle:“useful resources”
- [topic] inurl:resources

### 3. Evaluate Link Opportunities

Next, evaluate and prioritize your broken link building opportunities. So you can use resources more effectively.

One consideration is **the potential value of the backlink**.

Gauge this with Semrush’s [Authority Score](https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-authority-score-explained/) (AS) metric. It measures the authoritativeness of a page or domain on a scale of 0-100.

Generally, the higher the referring page or domain’s score, the more valuable the backlink for SEO.

Sort the “**Page** **AS**” and “**AS**” spreadsheet columns into descending order.

![Backlinks exported in a spreadsheet](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/eb/6a/eb6a6ac6664e6fac10b1f4c9e360e502/DjmUCHIoJHQwqQ_ZwjeSyQBIYhY69Q5wjDp5J7J2eWjh4Q2fGtjU7qRuNugvyvPmuZKelUaAWcCt0jRGv00jKbU-j9IIGvWg9tCjZG1xy10SWoYQIGVj78q4RpjTVzmmQsfAVhPhrI1ZA4gP2eS4Utw.png)

***Tip:** For more ways to evaluate the potential value of a backlink, read our [backlink analysis](https://www.semrush.com/blog/toxic-links-guidelines/) guide.*

Next, review each broken link **in context**.

Simply open its source URL and find the hyperlinked element.

![broken link highlighted in opened source URL](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a0/32/a032cd34d82be4ea97226f5cceb1e5a8/OuzV399HCWloIM6CjRi9vq0z3FijsNm9wuAe0xPfFWcYQKidTRIdbxRSkpRv6Oq45spnZeyfBqoK_4HlfAVeWFgPNSgNFvD_HpLpSMjxSZNx9vACms05G7rTlqrPU40j47gtl0tfnUGYOkXuwafaYmE.png)

To find out what the linked page used to look like, enter the dead link into [Wayback Machine](https://archive.org/web/).

Then, click the date of the most recent snapshot. You can check earlier dates if you need to.

![click the date of the most recent snapshot in Wayback Machine](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b8/05/b8057656ebca49a301a4f90ed7c62265/JN94pvAqw_kuRwHUABxU1HgzI0B3LRkk_EcvPoQjovrwfqg8v95e5YTpRGTGeaOwk9khlvWjE6cs5xW6ehCxmJjadoqzmwZ_SURNP0dFqLfJZ41uLRSL2HZXYBQzZchROeGktKFziKLxL_KAdunKLQY.png)

When you open the snapshot, you can see the deleted page. And work to find a comparable resource on your website.

![example of a deleted page titled "east village history project"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a5/e6/a5e654a03e50c4b2107bfd63d61d46cb/dvX6HyqJ8g3_iQUvSZxITFJdNFC_Yj07z5YkvMrXc6TIHqvtwFUmmcApQY85qSD9vP-EvT-fp21sP-AvR5PY9i8jewMbOP3GUyAUaJK4sLIsIpmtIRUQWF_aEHfg4r-R9DgxfzFY-IJiTyrlxrToM_Q.png)

Consider the alternative link you could recommend to the web admin. And the likelihood they'll accept your suggestion.

The web admin is **more likely** to link to your site if:

- Your suggested link is relevant and useful in context
- Your suggested page is the best of its kind
- Their page gets ample traffic
- Linking to your site presents no conflict of interest
- The original link was supposed to point to your site

***Tip:** Maximize your chance of success by improving your content. Or creating [quality content](https://www.semrush.com/blog/quality-content/) from scratch. But you’ll need to weigh the potential benefits against the effort required.*

Conversely, the web admin is **less likely** to link to your site if:

- They can easily find a better alternative
- Their content is outdated (e.g., an old news article)
- Linking to your site presents a conflict of interest (e.g., you’re a competitor)
- The original link was sponsored or pointed to a partner site
- The original link appears within user-generated content (e.g., a forum comment)

In these cases, the admin might decide to link elsewhere, leave the dead link in place, or remove it altogether.

So, pursuing these broken link building opportunities could do more harm than good. You’ll waste time and potentially harm relationships with useful contacts.

Decide whether the opportunity is worth pursuing and add notes to your spreadsheet.

Like this:

!["Notes" highlighted in a speadsheet](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c3/6e/c36e32a4926d0332b211a952c4749223/oft-z2bO1CGklYwWEe28k6hOhASL99_Z6gKX5V45EZ2rSnmmPNzKGhw615sstfUGzY8Ktr606pXidWxwqHJXQ3K7Rv-7LQr0Q6MaEGs-Wyyu1jKwF5UGP3zJPPRUSHjNhaoD5P3U44qE9eTw_p0pwvY.png)

### 4. Identify Outreach Contacts

[Outreach](https://www.semrush.com/blog/link-building-outreach/) is the process of contacting web admins (usually via email) and requesting that they link to your site.

In broken link building, the idea is to point out the broken backlink. And offer your alternative.

First, determine **whom** to contact.

Staff writers typically have the motive and power to update dead links. Open the source page’s URL to check for an author byline.

You might need to check the writer’s author page or social media profiles to learn more about them.

Natasha’s author page shows that she’s a staff writer. And even includes her email address.

![an author page for "Natasha Mascarenhas"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/db/6a/db6a95627e8cd913d62b328b5ac2e1ab/7ri6kwYHR8rdlxAcA7EcT7gy0u_rHkv2FL6mYtSrX_dxHUu_E2bo-1hK8ACoEWT_ohIzijNHBADdQ4JrkQPpSHAgzlE9vNqWEFaQANZaPxJa0-aPljdktcwoIRXfvoiM-6t3npdX6JuMJ03_5YkX0n8.png)

You could even try direct messaging her on Twitter.

!["Natasha Mascarenhas" on Twitter](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/eb/77/eb77b942130f2d82339f9550a420be30/oit0JNhfqaqbp7AQmu7IOzg6heEDR9vnmZe5ci6gPWB5jlO8f-BdkPHFOLphmJIbfCxi530pcufTiLD8lvKQgtteuXw3VR7PWNqWCQaR7gh8Ll9UpkBpTD04ZZ8dCF1hIjwUD03DyfDmB5S8cGeM8ts.png)

If that tactic doesn’t work, check the site’s “Contact” or “Meet the Team” page.

Look for a specific contact (a particular person’s email address) rather than a generic email address (like hello@example.com). **Direct** requests to **the most relevant person** are more likely to succeed.

### 5. Send Your Pitch

Next, write and send your pitch.

Admins are often receptive to broken link building emails. After all, you’re bringing their attention to an issue on their website. And providing a suggestion to help them fix it.

You’ve ensured your alternative link is a good fit and identified the right contact. All that’s left is making a **clear**, **friendly**, and **compelling** proposal.

Here are some best practices:

- Start with a personalized greeting
- Reference the broken link upfront
- Explain why your link makes a good alternative
- Include hyperlinks for easy reference
- Keep your email concise

Here’s a broken link building email example:

*Hi Michael,*

*I noticed your Dunder Mifflin article “How to Make Your Office More Economical” contains a broken link.*

*Looks like EcoMag deleted their paper recycling report, as the URL brings up a 404 error.*

*If you’re looking for an alternative, we’ve just published a new paper waste report here at WasteCo.*
