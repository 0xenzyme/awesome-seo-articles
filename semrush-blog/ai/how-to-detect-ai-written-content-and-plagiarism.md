# How to Detect AI-written Content and Plagiarism

Pure AI-generated content isn’t ideal.

If you’ve tried ChatGPT for writing, you may notice that AI-generated text often sounds predictable and generic.

Human writing tends to be more dynamic and less predictable, with a richer vocabulary.

And unlike AI, human authors are prone to typos, which can make human writing appear more imperfect—and natural.

If you are an editor, professor, or content marketer, it’s crucial to carefully evaluate the content you review.

## What Is AI Content Detection?

AI content detection is the process of using machine learning (ML) and natural language processing (NLP) to determine if text is human-written or AI-generated.

These “AI content detectors” learn from large datasets of both human-written and machine-generated text.

## What Is Plagiarism Detection?

Plagiarism detection is the process of comparing text to extensive databases to find **exact or partial** matches.

These databases can include online content, research papers, journals, and other publications.

In contrast, AI detection tools look for **patterns** in word usage and sentence structure.

Plagiarism detection is even more relevant now that people use AI tools like [ChatGPT](https://www.semrush.com/goodcontent/content-marketing-blog/how-to-use-chatgpt/).

Technically, AI-generated text may not copy passages word for word, but it can paraphrase them. Plagiarism checkers can sometimes flag such paraphrased text.

## How AI Content Detectors Work

AI content detectors work by analyzing two main characteristics:

- [**Perplexity**](https://www.techslang.com/perplexity-in-nlp-definition-pros-and-cons/#:~:text=Perplexity%20is%20a%20statistical%20measure,the%20model%20predicts%20the%20text): Measures how predictable the text is to a language model
- [**Burstiness**](https://machinelearning.wtf/terms/burstiness/): Assesses how often certain words or phrases repeat

Lower perplexity means more predictable text and a higher likelihood of AI generation. Low burstiness can also indicate AI text.

Therefore, predictable, uniform sentences often indicate machine writing.

## How to Detect Plagiarism and AI-Generated Content

You can detect plagiarism and AI-generated content by using plagiarism checkers and AI-detection software.

Another way to identify AI-generated is to look for common signs, such as:

- Incorrect and outdated information
- Limited depth and personality
- Lack of anecdotes or personal stories
- Repetitive language

### Incorrect and Outdated Information

Incorrect and outdated information is a common issue with generative AI.

While the text may look polished, it can often be irrelevant or inaccurate ("[artificial hallucination](https://cloud.google.com/discover/what-are-ai-hallucinations)").

Always verify factual accuracy, as many AI models rely on limited or incomplete data.

### Lack of Depth and Personality

Lack of depth and personality is a key difference between human and AI-generated content.

Human writers share anecdotal stories, opinions, and perspectives. This personal knowledge shows how people think and write.

[AI tools](https://www.semrush.com/blog/best-ai-tools/) generate text based on patterns in training data, without real understanding. This can lead to superficial responses and a lack of critical thinking.

AI does not have a personality, so its text can sound robotic.

Conversely, a journalist or copywriter can have conversations with subject matter experts.

These conversations create deeper understanding, engaging stories, and relatable insights that AI struggles to replicate.

### Repetitive Language

Repetitive language is another common trait of [AI models](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-models/). These tools often repeat the same words or phrases.

This repetition may come from a keyword in the prompt or a limited training set.

AI models also tend to be neutral and cautious, which can lead to repetitive language patterns.

ChatGPT, for example, may [overuse transitional words](https://www.wordrake.com/blog/wordy-choppy-generative-ai#:~:text=While%20human%20writers%20understand%20the,to%20strange%20or%20repetitive%20choices.) like “furthermore,” “in conclusion,” “firstly,” and “secondly.”

No detection tool is perfect.

Different checkers use different methods, so one might label a text as fully AI-generated while another calls it partly AI.

Use multiple tools to confirm your findings.
