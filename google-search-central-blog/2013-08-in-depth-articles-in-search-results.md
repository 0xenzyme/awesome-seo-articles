# In-depth articles in search results

Users often turn to Google to answer a quick question, but
[research suggests](https://www.technologyreview.com/news/507451/how-google-plans-to-find-the-ungoogleable/)
that up to 10% of users' daily information needs involve learning about a broad topic. That's why
today we're introducing new search results to help users find
[in-depth articles](https://insidesearch.blogspot.com/2013/08/discover-great-in-depth-articles-on.html).

These results are ranked algorithmically based on many signals that look for high-quality,
in-depth content. You can help our algorithms understand your pages better by following these
recommendations:

- use [schema.org `article`](https://schema.org/Article)
  markup,
- provide [authorship](https://plus.google.com/authorship) markup,
- [`rel=next` and `rel=prev`](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/1663744)
  for paginated articles (also watch out for
  [common `rel=canonical` mistakes](/search/blog/2013/04/5-common-mistakes-with-relcanonical)),
- provide information about your
  [organization's logo](/search/blog/2013/05/using-schemaorg-markup-for-organization),
- and of course, create compelling in-depth content.

Following these best practices along with our
[webmaster guidelines](/search/docs/essentials) helps our
systems to better understand your website's content, and improves the chances of it appearing in
this new set of search results.

The in-depth articles feature is rolling out now on google.com in English. For more information,
check out our [help center article](/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article), and
you can post in the comments in our forums.
