# Google adds a dedicated Agentic Browsing category to Lighthouse

Google Lighthouse now includes an [Agentic Browsing category](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/scoring) that audits how well AI agents are able to navigate, understand, and act on a site.

Google’s documentation notes that the Agentic Browsing category is experimental. Practically speaking, that means you need to run Lighthouse in Chrome Canary, Google's experimental Chrome channel that’s released daily.

The Agentic Browsing category runs three sets of checks:

- **WebMCP**: Validates your WebMCP integration — a proposed web standard that lets a site provide directions for AI agents to act upon
- **Agent-centric accessibility**: Reviews the part of your accessibility tree that matters most for machine interaction, including names and labels, tree integrity, and visibility
- **Stability and discoverability**: Measures Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) and checks for the presence of an llms.txt file

Marie Haynes, Owner of Marie Haynes Consulting, shared an [X post](https://x.com/Marie_Haynes/status/2060419184832467111) about the announcement that pointed out how interesting it is that one of the checks centers on an llms.txt file:

![Marie Haynes points out on X that the new Lighthouse category checks for an llms.txt file.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a0/12/a012e62241415ff0557e4ef7d9ae3d1b/92f42e07b4889eb4a21dc75b65fb03ab/image.png)

Per Google's documentation, the standards for the agentic web are still emerging. Instead of the familiar 0–100 score, the Agentic Browsing category shows a ratio of how many readiness checks your site passes.

## What the Agentic Browsing category signals for marketers

Agentic readiness has been easy to put off until now, but the recent change to Lighthouse signals that isn’t feasible for much longer.

Agentic protocols and tools are arriving fast. Just recently, Google has been building toward an agentic web with:

- [AI performance report in Merchant Center](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-to-add-ai-performance-report-to-merchant-center/): Tracks how AI-generated shopping experiences are performing
- [AI Assistant channel in GA4](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ga4-adds-ai-assistant-channel/): Attributes traffic from AI assistants as its own channel in Google Analytics
- [Google-Agent user agent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-ai-agent/): A dedicated crawler identity that signals when Google's AI agents are visiting and interacting with your site
- [Universal Commerce Protocol](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-ucp-checkout-in-main-serp/): A standard that lets AI agents complete purchases across retailers without leaving Google's surfaces
- [WebMCP](https://www.semrush.com/blog/webmcp/): A proposed web standard that, if adopted, would let sites direct agents on how to act

A Google-backed audit for agentic readiness provides search marketers with a reputable way to justify their work to stakeholders.

Google has also said llms.txt isn't required for generative AI search. Its inclusion here suggests the file matters more when your site has agent-specific elements, which is exactly what the Agentic Browsing report evaluates.
