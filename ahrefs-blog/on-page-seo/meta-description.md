# How to Write the Perfect Meta Description

The meta description is an HTML attribute used to describe what a page is about. It can appear in search results as a snippet under the [title tag](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tag-seo/) to provide more context.

Here’s how the code snippet looks:

`<meta name="description" content="Site Explorer runs on a huge database of 12 trillion links and 402 million tracked keywords to give you the most complete SEO data about any website or URL.">`

Even though the meta description attribute is [not a ranking factor](https://webmasters.googleblog.com/2009/09/google-does-not-use-keywords-meta-tag.html) and [Google shows it in search results only 37% of the time](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/), it’s still a part of [on-page SEO](https://ahrefs.com/seo/on-page-seo) that content publishers often encounter.

In this guide, you’ll learn how to create great meta descriptions fast and how to audit them efficiently. But first, let me explain why meta descriptions still matter (and why their role has actually grown more interesting in the age of AI search).

## Why meta descriptions still matter

Having a compelling meta description entices more people to click on your website in the search results. That’s important, even though Google only shows meta descriptions around [37% of the time](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/).

For example, if your page gets 50,000 impressions a month, Google shows the meta description for 18,500 of those impressions on average. Increasing your CTR from 4% to just 4.5% in this instance would result in almost 100 more clicks.

But the case for writing a good meta description goes beyond Google search results. Here are four reasons to spend the time on it.

### 1. Meta descriptions influence click-through rates

Even at 37%, that’s a meaningful number of impressions where your description is the deciding factor between a click and a scroll-past.

Think of it as conversion copy, not SEO copy. Your job is to tell the reader exactly what they’ll get and why it’s worth their click, not to stuff it with keywords.

### 2. Meta descriptions appear on every surface your page is shared or linked

When a page is shared on social media, it pulls the description from [Open Graph meta tags](https://ahrefs.com/blog/open-graph-meta-tags/). But if you haven’t set a separate OG description, it falls back to the meta description. The same applies to link previews in Slack, Teams, Discord, and messaging apps.

If you’re only willing to set one of these tags, the meta description is the right call.

### 3. AI platforms can use meta descriptions when referencing your page

When ChatGPT retrieves a page as a source during a web search, it extracts a “snippet” from the page to represent it in its source list.

According to [Chris Long](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/chris-long-marketing_seo-tip-meta-descriptions-are-still-important-activity-7388555714991906816-D1Ys/), this snippet can come from several places:

- The meta description
- An introductory paragraph
- Content under a heading
- A bulleted list
- Or even be auto-generated by ChatGPT itself

However, your meta description is your most direct opportunity to influence how your page is represented when AI platforms cite it, since it functions as a neatly wrapped summary of the page’s content.

### 4. If you don’t write one, AI will

Google has always rewritten meta descriptions when it decides your version isn’t a good match for the query.

But lately, Google has been testing something new: fully AI-generated descriptions powered by Gemini, complete with the Gemini logo in the SERP.

These don’t pull from your page content at all. They’re generated fresh.

A weak or missing meta description is effectively an open invitation for the AI to write whatever it thinks best represents your page.

So while you shouldn’t obsess over meta descriptions as a ranking signal, it’s still worth spending a minute on pages where ‘yes’ is the answer to at least one of these questions:

- Is the page meant to drive organic traffic?
- Is it likely to be shared on social media or messaging platforms?
- Is the page being cited as a source by AI platforms?

## How to write a meta description

Follow these five steps to create your meta descriptions quickly and easily:

1. [Expand on your title tag](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/#1-expand-title-tag)
2. [Match search intent](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/#2-match-search-intent)
3. [Use active voice](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/#3-use-active-voice)
4. [Be concise](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/#4-be-concise)
5. [Deploy the meta description](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/#5-deploy-meta-description)

Let’s get into it.

### 1. Expand on your title tag

Always think about the description as something that complements the [title tag](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tag-seo/). Titles should summarize a page using a few main keywords that characterize the topic. The meta description offers a chance to expand on that further.

In the case above, the short description motivates users to click on [our broken link checker](https://ahrefs.com/broken-link-checker) because:

- It checks pages and websites.
- It takes internal and external links into account.
- The tool is fast.
- It’s not a lead generation trap; you can use it on the page right away.

Don’t worry about shoehorning keywords here. It’s more important that you create something that connects with the reader and entices the click. Use your USPs where suitable.

### 2. Match search intent

[Search intent](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-intent/) is the ‘why’ behind a query. In other words, what are most people looking for when they search for your main keyword?

- Do they want information?
- Do they want to buy something?
- Are they looking for a specific website?
- Do they want a quick answer to a question?

You can use the search results as a proxy for this. Google strives to provide the most relevant results for searchers, so look for commonalities amongst the snippets of top-ranking pages.

### 4. Be concise

Meta descriptions are not a place for storytelling. Every word matters because users quickly decide what to click on by skimming the results, and because descriptions truncate after a certain length.

Right now, that length is 920px (~160 characters) on desktop and 680px (~120 characters) on mobile.

Your best bet to keep them within length is to use a tool like [this one](https://blog.spotibo.com/meta-description-length/), or a [WordPress SEO plugin](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-seo-plugins-for-wordpress/) like Yoast with the functionality built in.

Whether you’re better off optimizing for mobile or desktop users depends on your audience. If you generally go for longer descriptions for desktop users, make sure to get to the point by ¾ of the length, so even mobile users can see it.

What’s the takeaway here? Be concise for users, not search engines. The length limit is a good signal that there might be unnecessary words.

### 5. Deploy the meta description

If you’re using a CMS, this is super easy. In Wix, Squarespace, [Shopify](https://ahrefs.com/blog/shopify-seo/#on-page-seo), and most others, you’ll find a place to type or paste your meta description in the backend.

In WordPress, plugins like Yoast and RankMath add this same functionality.

If you don’t use a CMS, just paste the meta description tag into the <head> section of your HTML code.

Recommendation

If you’re adding the meta description straight into the code, don’t use quotation marks in the description itself because it will break things. Instead, use the HTML entity `&quot;` so it looks like this:

`<meta name="description" content="Just showing how to use &quot;quotation marks&quot; in meta description.">`

If you use quotation marks when adding a meta description in CMS and plugins, they will usually take care of the HTML transcoding for you.

## Great meta description examples

Let’s wrap things up with a few examples of well-crafted meta descriptions across industries and search intents.

### 1. Healthline

Most searchers are seemingly looking for a definition here, so that’s what this meta description shows. It then prompts the reader to learn more about the benefits of glycine by clicking. Simple and efficient.

### 2. Patagonia

Here, the searcher is looking for sale items. I like the urgency signal; it feels more original and less aggressive than these tend to be. Then you see an affirmation of what you came for, ending with Patagonia’s distinctive asset: supporting sustainability and conservation.

### 3. Nordstrom

This is the epitome of a great ecommerce category page in the search results. It’s probable that Nordstrom lists their most popular types of coats and brands here. Free shipping is also likely a USP in these SERPs, as only one other result mentioned it.

### 4. Shopify

Our last example is a homepage. This is a category of its own because you have to reflect your brand and what you do. Shopify does this well. It’s clear and prompts you to try the service for free.

Your homepage is the only page where I’d advise spending more time on the meta description because it mostly appears for branded queries where the intent is clear. As a result, Google is more likely to show the meta description in the SERPs.

## Final thoughts

Meta descriptions are among the first topics most people encounter in the world of SEO.

Luckily, it’s also one of the easiest to understand and implement if you follow these basic principles:

- Write it for the reader, not the algorithm
- Consider it a clear, compelling one-sentence pitch describing your page
- Keep it short and front-load the value in case it gets truncated
