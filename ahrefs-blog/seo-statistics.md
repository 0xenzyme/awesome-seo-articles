# 107 SEO Statistics for 2026

Curious about the state of SEO in 2026? Then look no further.

We’ve curated, vetted, and categorized a list of up-to-date stats below.

## Top SEO statistics

1. 68% of online experiences begin with a search engine. ([BrightEdge](https://videos.brightedge.com/research-report/BrightEdge_ChannelReport2019_FINAL.pdf))
2. 63.41% of all US web traffic referrals come from Google. ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/who-sends-traffic-on-the-web-and-how-much-new-research-from-datos-sparktoro/))
3. SEO drives 1,000%+ more traffic than organic social media. ([BrightEdge](https://videos.brightedge.com/research-report/BrightEdge_ChannelReport2019_FINAL.pdf))
4. 96.55% of all pages get zero search traffic from Google. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/))
5. 87% of marketers surveyed use AI to create or help create content (769 out of 879 respondents). ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/marketers-using-ai-publish-more-content/))
6. 87% of marketers use AI to help create content. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/marketers-using-ai-publish-more-content/))
7. 96.55% of all pages get zero organic search traffic from Google. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/))
8. AI Overviews are associated with a 58% lower average click-through rate for the top-ranking organic page. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/))

## Search engine statistics

1. Google holds a 90.39% global search engine market share across all devices. ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share))
2. Google maintains a web index of ~400 billion documents. ([Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-index-size/))
3. 61.5% of desktop searches and 34.4% of mobile searches result in no-clicks. ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/google-ctr-in-2018-paid-organic-no-click-searches/))
4. 15% of all Google searches have never been searched before. ([Google](https://twitter.com/Google/status/1493681643290300425))
5. The average top-ranking result has a CTR of 9.28%, followed by 5.82 and 3.11% for positions two and three. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377272216_Device-dependent_click-through_rate_estimation_in_Google_organic_search_results_based_on_clicks_and_impressions_data))

## CTR and SERP behavior statistics

1. Organic CTR on queries with Google AI Overviews fell to 0.61% in September 2025, down 61% from 1.76% in June 2024. ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update))
2. The first organic Google result has an average CTR of 39.8%. ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/google-click-through-rates-ctrs-by-ranking-position/))
3. 96.6% of all Google search clicks go to results on the first page of results. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/serps/))
4. The top 3 organic Google results receive 68.7% of all clicks on the search page. ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/google-click-through-rates-ctrs-by-ranking-position/))

## Ranking and content statistics

1. Only 1.94% of all pages get between one and ten monthly search visits from Google. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/))
2. The top-ranking page gets the most total search traffic only 49% of the time. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ranking-number-one-is-overrated/))
3. Only 1.74% of newly published pages reach the top 10 search results within one year for at least one keyword. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/))
4. 72.9% of pages ranking in Google’s top 10 are more than 3 years old. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/))
5. 7.4% of top-ranking pages don’t have a title tag. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tags-study/))
6. Google changed title tags 76.04% of the time based on Q1 2025 data. ([John McAlpin](https://www.johnmcalpin.com/seo-data-study-how-often-google-changes-title-tags-and-why/))
7. Google rewrites title tags 33.4% of the time, making it 33% more likely to rewrite title tags than previously measured. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tags-study/))
8. When Google ignores the title tag, it uses H1 50.76% of the time instead. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tags-study/))
9. Google is 57% more likely to rewrite title tags that are too long. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/title-tags-study/))
10. 25.02% of top-ranking pages don’t have a meta description. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/))
11. Google rewrites meta descriptions 62.78% of the time. This drops to 59.65% for fat-head and rises to 65.62% for long-tails. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/))
12. 40.61% of pages have meta descriptions that truncate. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/))
13. Google shows meta descriptions in search results only 37.22% of the time. That rises to 40.35% for fat-head keywords and drops to 34.38% for long-tails. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description-study/))
14. The average top-ranking page also ranks in the top 10 search results for nearly 1,000 other relevant keywords. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/also-rank-for-study/))
15. There’s no correlation between Flesch Reading Ease scores and ranking positions. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/flesch-reading-ease/))
16. 30.9% of featured snippets rank in position one in Google search results. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/featured-snippets-study/))
17. AI-cited content is 25.7% fresher on average than pages ranking in organic Google results. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/fresh-content/))

## Backlink statistics

1. There’s a positive correlation between the number of websites linking to a page and its search traffic. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/))
2. Pages ranking higher in Google’s SERPs tend to acquire more new followed referring domains — a positive correlation found across 10,000 SERPs analyzed. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/backlink-growth-study/))
3. Most top-ranking pages get “followed” backlinks from new websites at a pace of +5%-14.5% per month. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/backlink-growth-study/))
4. 73.6% of domains have reciprocal links, meaning that some of the sites they link to also link to them. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/reciprocal-links/))
5. 43.7% of the top-ranking pages have some reciprocal links. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/reciprocal-links/))
6. 66.5% of links to sites in the last nine years are dead. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/link-rot-study/))
7. 10.6% of all backlinks to the top 110,000 sites are nofollow. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/nofollow-ugc-sponsored/))
8. 0.44% of referring domains to the top 110k sites use rel=“ugc” and 0.01% use rel=“sponsored”. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/nofollow-ugc-sponsored/))
9. URLs with a larger number of anchor text variations from internal links are highly correlated with more Google search traffic. ([Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/internal-links/seo-study/))
10. Pages with at least one exact match anchor had at least five times more traffic than pages without. ([Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/internal-links/seo-study/))
11. SEOs typically take 1-2 hours to build a single link. ([Aira](https://aira.net/state-of-link-building/link-building-measurement-and-reporting/))
12. Top-ranking pages (#1 position) tend to gain followed backlinks from new referring domains at a pace of between +5% and +14.5% per month. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/backlink-growth-study/))
