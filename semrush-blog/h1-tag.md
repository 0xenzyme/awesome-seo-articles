# What Is an H1 Tag? Why It Matters & Best Practices for SEO

## What Is an H1 Tag?

An H1 tag is the HTML used to create the main title on a webpage and helps indicate the page’s primary topic to visitors and search engines. It contains an opening <h1> tag, the title text, and a closing </h1> tag.

Here’s an example of an H1 tag in HTML and on the live page:

### How to Check H1 Tags on a Webpage

It’s easy to look into the H1 tags on your own (or any other) webpages without needing special skills.

Here are some of the most common ways:

#### View the Page’s Source Code

Right-click anywhere on a page and select “**View Page Source**” or “**Show Page Source**” from the menu.

![“View Page Source” button on Semrush Blog page](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/3d/4a/3d4a6c5cbb06da37ce8220608c356127/22128f6dac60f478e455d36722af87b0/6QNapmDLZhB--tCF85BIdMwN1NdRmQRvh9vEPcR50c1hbxe9L5Q-zWSMqz_-NY4q6JZdn6M3KgW_hYtUovzDkqFKmETt_woRilcJ49N3AZSiDnTpOXwE72C8EsYqrtNaFTnKyHPhuwjj8aJ8tBamZbA.png)

This will open a new tab with the page’s source code.

Now, you can manually look for the <h1> tag.

Or use the “**Command + F**” shortcut on Mac or “**Ctrl + F**” on PC and type “h1.”

## Why Are H1 Tags Important for SEO?

H1 tags aren’t a confirmed ranking factor. But they’re still important for SEO.

Here are the top three reasons why:

### 1. H1s Help Search Engines Understand Content

Search engines look at your H1s to learn about your pages and their content.

The more descriptive your H1, the better.

The H1 in this blog post from Chewy is a great example of a descriptive H1.

It tells search engines exactly what they’ll find: The best coffee shops with cats in the United States. To ensure they have an easier time understanding what’s on the page.

![H1 tag shown on the live page and page's HTML](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/77/a4/77a410c444d8232fcb9bfcc1cdff5c48/e0f6ce84782b6c4044eb92c05b19efb0/F5mJuZGiW-lYTyRxjLJP_SfRDusiQZfJySKC8eH_ngMj0IIY8SdJMkOC5Z_spsWB5YkQmy5pv2gDQDBViRNVZWtNinmfOknJ-o_RvebutjFu5QXMczmwLEINrEJUSc4kjDogcOGRhorliFw1rel5BRc.png)

### 2. H1s Improve the User Experience

H1 tags improve the user experience (UX) by allowing readers to learn what your page is about at a glance. Especially when you consider most websites display the H1 as the largest text on the page.

And improving UX is fundamental. Google has emphasized it [contributes to better rankings](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience).

Not to mention:

A quick browse of headings can show readers what they’ll learn before they dig in. That way, they can pre-assess your content’s value.

If you’ve done a good job, your H1 will provide hierarchy and reassure visitors that you have the information they’re looking for.

Consider this image:

![A visual showing how headings help readers skim the content](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/21/c1/21c151c556274bb7454af311b4119621/09e1afc5d54346bb8178e2d9a8dcb307/_klgUtz2xgwjL4XBQzrMOX37dBRNnSgsSqdhRb6IXdsNQVB30Nr4X-k-TLNoH_pKYbIp2ZkGvzXo63d-hZGILYhY_KPnTE1n95Dh6AjYQeYI3K9yHqhFYPxrpH_UK4fqvyXk9JGYZKAesiGraZblZjI.png)

The example on the left has no clear structure and order. It’s really hard to tell what the content’s about.

The example on the right shows the H1 as the most prominent heading on the page, then H2, then H3, etc.

It’s easy to skim, follow, and understand.

### 3. H1s Improve Accessibility

H1 tags (and other headings in general) can help those who use screen readers understand the page’s content.

Screen readers are programs thatcommunicate computer text with a speech synthesizer or Braille display.

Here’s an example of how the headings in The Penn State University’s accessibility guidelines appear on a screen reader:

![screen reader displaying the headings](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/7d/b0/7db09692d731b161f84fd25fea93ed59/aefa22eaa7561b48bd7159580ab61c38/LSSV8VERBaQ1oOIxDMsMLq-chtADZeQzAeLIzvuP31uRTwBXI8oxNGO5LbqB1IyWvn53jBxyqza3BEhKyl5G3MaziR9aQgfOtd6MkLxw4bQhhsWZ17iK9gEigV3a1MGKnCNa-mc73_8u4sgbUo9Nd-o.png)

**Image Source**: [The Penn State University](https://accessibility.psu.edu/headingshtml/)

Quickly improve your site’s accessibility by crafting descriptive H1 tags and keeping an organized heading structure.

## How to Add H1 Tags to Your Blog Pages

Adding an H1 tag to articles in most website content management systems ([CMSs](https://www.semrush.com/goodcontent/content-marketing-blog/content-management-system/)) is easy. In fact, many CMSs have a placeholder for your article title that’s automatically formatted to be your H1.

That said, here’s how to change your H1 tag using WordPress, Squarespace, and Wix.

### Adding an H1 Tag in WordPress

Most WordPress themes are coded so the post title is the H1.

To change this (or any other) heading, click on or highlight the text you want to reformat. A toolbar should appear above your selected block.

Like this:

![highlight text in wordpress to reformat](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1a/79/1a79f6bec952a1fd97a9a87b10bbf0b9/0d10187b370d24999c6817cdc0d23db7/GdR2KoCeNqpUJoo25YYuEKDfqJ6r3qlhuu89mrLPQdevujeezGPlkv9DhwB7BuVaNz3FnYSV7buhDdNtDLFQ9IQfWPaBRcSmlh8nP4eBVwWXHQZAIiCAlIvplLDfNGMbivl_wUipkOLm6ubH84ji0gg.jpeg)

## H1 Tag Best Practices

Here are six H1 tag best practices you can follow to improve your SEO.

### 1. Use a Single H1 Per Page

Using just one H1 creates hierarchy and gives your page a more logical structure.

It makes it easy for readers (and those using screen readers) to skim and understand what your page is about.

The H1 is for the [headline](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-headline/) conveying the main topic. The H2s are for important subheadings. The H3s are for subheadings that support those. And so on:

![A blog structure shown with H1, H2, H3, H4, H4, and H6 tags](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/16/fc/16fcca3ade43c480d516f32ea3742bca/d68aacdc5bdf7548df723734a4f5a37d/qy_SqpoFbOkMZDF5rxY8DnozNCGcTz_jyJR9CcvbjWQZmRqHWcasv6b-0r-AScLjpusOiC0yysDpNtwpluxxpaO5nq9Sm2RLDCMrQHjxl1Dp2MzCwF0zeQJZFklV470FsTH95GO2G7ZeZZLAPixC0oI.png)

So, how many H1 tags per page?

Each page should have only one main topic. And that should be your only H1.

### 2. Make Sure Every Important Page Has an H1

Considering that an H1 tag is the most important heading, every important page should have one.

Which pages are considered important? That’s up to you. But a good place to start is with any page you want to rank on Google.

To find pages with missing or empty H1s:

1. Go to [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/)
2. Enter your homepage URL and click “**Start Audit**”
3. Click on the “**Issues**” tab
4. Type “h1” in the search bar
5. Click on the warning for pages that don’t have an H1

![A warning in Site Audit tool that reads: "28 pages don't have an h1 heading" highlighted](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c3/92/c3924372b735cd14cb66bca47b7173cf/444bf81a4c1bc27080520d6f3f2a944f/eUEbgzuSYp35uFu_w6rQHBusPbQEC_xS5gV-x2LFVC-TGQ644CRKGz9J5C5Ey_uWq_R8RKQ_VR-tKVwAOzvYrKwhrP-_5hP7qALSdJEJQmiDW4EBUdSFoJDOb8CqaBI-fPBt9GUeI6Paz4d8G2R5tSY.png)

You’ll then see a list of affected pages. Which you can address by adding an H1 to each.

### 3. Include Your Target Keyword

Along with your title tag, Google scans content like your H1 to determine whether it’s relevant to a searcher’s query.

In other words:

A page with an H1 that mentions the search term is likely to rank better for that query than a page with an H1 that doesn’t.

We do this for all our blog posts.

Below, you can see how we included the keywords “title tags” and “alt text” in two different blog post H1s:

![include target keyword in headings](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/db/85/db8579abf7fa8e574f94958e863cc04d/63888999b3d7d930aacf27e18dd9d38a/cNEh3KMAPnnFTaOgjJ3EwQtZ4ul2hylTRalMbkP2eV0BLOlR3SGHU-gw20RWdjI0xw5VO97K9ybLWTuGJ7y3VwURXz07jw3hwuk2BHF9tVW2osJ91AhzMnr5QL1H1ZlQR260B4CqQ9sKf7yTXnpIYdY.jpeg)

### 4. Keep H1 Tags Under 60 Characters

The clearer and more concise you make your writing, the better. It’s no different for H1 tags or other headings.

The best practice is to keep it between 50 and 60 characters—just like your title tags.

Why?

Many CMSs use the same copy for your H1 and title tag. Plus, Google occasionally switches title tags for H1 tags. And you don’t want Google to truncate it in the search results like this:

![An example of h1 tag truncated in Google search results](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/43/ee/43eec3126ba84d7b480581e9c9642baf/fe41e2fa6a30681502740dd210a7ea1a/edym4wKD7su7bLFiHWSp3LRaiV6dL9QjhdGJZwI1OTTZeurjOEMMzBY-dRKtK2oAMEKNmYHBoCCu4oXTxLJvXB4T6twRHPSOQ0AlDPONr8ezgeajLMmYywtRV83sxBcBS2yeo3RvyZVK2e-xq0f20zM.jpeg)

So, there’s no technical limit to the length of an H1 tag. But your best bet is to keep it under 60 characters.

### 5. Match Your H1s to Your Title Tags for Article Pages

A common [SEO mistake](https://www.semrush.com/blog/biggest-seo-mistakes/) is to confuse the title tags added in the <head> section of your code with H1 tags. Because both the title tag and H1 tag describe what a page is about.

The main difference is **where** they appear.

**Title tags** appear at the top of the browser tab. And may appear on search engine results pages (SERPs). Other than that, they’re not visible on the webpage.

Here’s an example of a title tag in a browser tab and in a SERP:

![A title tag in a browser tab and in a SERP](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/61/c6/61c696951edafdbfa272abb659f46cb3/6c1cf41edf102f361a9caeb3b1a0045d/d0ti6sBYBOsv3KKkpkEw153sGtBjEzbufiyHFlbwEZ0DRvBAqikcDYkWLR2LyZwrqXcAGmhxSPOOpesUkWqMLXoawKCaqFu97LxV9W7ck3vpsA_6spEWXq1l26y0UWHayXOUuC4mA3Recyo2oM9GaTo.png)

**H1 tags** are what users see on the page, often in large text. They usually don’t appear on SERPs if they differ from the title tag.

Here’s an H1 for one of our blog posts:

![An H1 for a Semrush blog that reads: "Keyword Research for SEO: What It Is & How to Do It"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1a/e7/1ae7b9815adb86ff11fe72751b1f2ddf/ca401622cee352ce1d626e23debf6aaa/OWhazIyTyaSXB69_VFx6nzSohZqgR04O7vi9OpN4ZscG6zCr-JGyVEEApQdxB5XsfxhuRabnX15MzffLRDKDmiZUQ-yO3-sX7PEhlG5bMCuIdle-ToJaJWiXRGVceefON-AQ3fhZ4di0QMkGRUwF7mI.png)

Google [recommends](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9607104?hl=en-GB) matching your H1 tags to your title tags to prevent inaccurate article titles from showing up in search results.

If they are different, they should be very similar.

Like this:

![An H1 on Semrush blog that reads: "6 Market Analysis Steps to Building a Surefire Marketing Strategy" and a title tag in code that reads: "Market Analysis: 6 Steps to Building a Surefire Marketing Strategy"](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/45/2b/452beb453cda0877111fbe0660ec7c7d/24a34741ae1a744bdfdfe9a8496d47a9/D3-2KIcdo5kHXqXq4m5ll7xcsISYXnAdmTuGT-qgnetTHrFRp0MjJ0Hiars4qJcCcDplhVJp5OsED3ZJj7nZmCN9bXUNQY7-1SMpDxL4iIjkUb6xEI3ioGBB1XfEeEWS5MdT9jLBueG5mUQmdC9DPR0.jpeg)

Users will feel deceived if they land on your page and see a completely different (and unrelated) H1. You don't want that.

### 6. Optimize Your H1 to Satisfy Search Intent

[Search intent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/) is a user’s primary objective when searching for something.

Your H1 should immediately tell the reader that you’re going to satisfy their search intent.

In other words:

Your content is going to give them what they want.

Satisfying intent is important because it’s [Google’s main goal](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/ranking-results/). Which, makes it a primary goal for you if you want better rankings.

To determine the search intent for a keyword, type it into Google and analyze what comes up.

For example, the top results for “content marketing” focus on explaining what content marketing is:

![Google's top results for “content marketing” query](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8b/86/8b860fcf052a2ad30adcb656553299af/09da691c50ee1920d99551c0a39d0107/VA1U9OwAXVHqaTmTtatgNHFeNXwUNWn7zF7xjCLqsNdheGmBa3e_ul8A3JsSowFAV_dzA0QxadOm-FQMp1mfQx0Fnt9ya0VPqIHfVa9tgvtYvwncTa9BeiLsKlyHqbB9kCXUIGk3H1gDIMwAnA0zlYk.jpeg)

None of those articles are too in depth. They also aren’t selling anything. That tells us the search intent is informational, with articles written for people who want to learn something.

This means we need to write an H1 (and accompanying post) that satisfies that informational search intent.
