# How to Do a Backlink Audit (5 Simple Steps)

## What Is a Backlink Audit?

A backlink audit is the process of analyzing the quality, quantity, and relevance of links pointing to your website.

![an infographic showing a website and multiple backlink pages pointing at the main one](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/11/1f/111f99d495387107ec3ce5f45bf168fd/oeZjjtQt-v-5QAIm_aToYjM5pHpUzotcKxzeKG8-3JPu8ZFXC7FPzKtQu65xEeJbsvU1axw9E4bSkiTcxwA3f2-e5FeYkHHpKR_KLpHk6zbLiS61fPuad8TvIsHdgCfAyXfHPQ4cZJv-Ey-fOFjOVNo.png)

A comprehensive link audit can give you a gold mine of information that can greatly impact your [SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-seo/) and backlink strategy.

So you can build better links and beat your competitors by outranking them in search results.

In this article, we’ll run through a simple backlink audit process you can replicate for your own site.

***Note**: We’ll be using Semrush’s [Backlink Audit](https://www.semrush.com/backlink_audit/), [Backlink Checker](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/backlinks/), and [Link Building Tool](https://www.semrush.com/link_building/) for this entire process. Semrush tools have the [biggest backlink database](https://www.semrush.com/blog/biggest-backlink-database-study/) on the market.*

Let’s get started.

## 1. Benchmark Your Backlink Profile Against Competitors

This step is a must. Comparing yourself to your competitors can give you a lot of valuable insights and answer important questions like:

- Do you have more backlinks and [referring domains](https://www.semrush.com/kb/503-backlinks-referring-domains-report-manual) than your competitors?
- What does your link acquisition rate look like compared to your competitors?
- What’s your [Authority Score](https://www.semrush.com/kb/747-authority-score-backlink-scores)? How does it compare to your competition?

All of this matters.

Because the stronger your backlink profile is, the better you’ll end up ranking in search results. Which can translate to more traffic to your site.

To start your analysis, go to the [Backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/backlinks/) tool. Enter your domain name and click “**Analyze**.”

![enter your domain to Backlinks tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/92/fe/92fec99d5ea59b34a91a01f8a2d2e5cf/lkX2E-m8uQym85CFzj7yXMpJ7DIdOrjz-8KKagns9V_v0nSr8XsIqN5h0fzyFhMwJGBMXpu6TkfQsoxfFnVxHXqxHtCUu311d6y2vHx2I9y8Vw7Pk6k71mcfg_uE62wkTa89i54vXq_9rl1PpMXqQmo.png)

Then, in the “Overview” tab, enter some of your competitors’ domains and click “**Compare**.”

![compare your website with competitors in “Overview” tab](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0d/ae/0dae6a6ab820a98dbdf9c9aa00581bd6/Zz1XxxHuXrfCp3RpPKkHTmKPzBjPFHVMQuDhhvi_zabVv-TGn3r3VOSIg73iukPOmbNme7dF7PVenjE8etjulz4zb9hL8uH5TjGdOPT8ajhqcgkBT_zfRt7lkFQt7VTmOV7kaiUbxL00zKgQVkZneWU.png)

You’ll find lots of data points to see whether you’re trending in the right direction with your link building strategy.

### What You Should Analyze

In the “Overview” tab, review the:

- **Backlinks, Referring Domains, and Referring IPs**: To estimate how big your backlink footprint is compared to the competition. Generally, the higher the numbers, the stronger your backlink profile is.
- **Authority Score**: To determine the quality of your website. This quality is measured on a scale from 1 to 100. The higher the number, the better quality the domain is.

![a table in the “Overview” tab with columns showing backlinks, referring domains, referring IPs and authority scores](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f5/10/f510ac9f27bfb0800a51ab3e483b1eaa/BU5i10CyUN9E7B0eQgbacL5GwaBn2oopuTcD8_cHRy3d9YKsjtRdIpcFSfoiNiTKStXgmAh82DlApTNd5DT58urORy-QjPu392BwkHsmJR_X39KYrFYI2CQdGw0vi9fWmH4Gor-uJs3XWHxgRVsyT_w.png)

- **Link Attributes**: To identify linking patterns and see what proportion of your links are “[follow” and nofollow](https://www.semrush.com/blog/nofollow-links/). “Follow” links influence your rankings because they transfer link equity, but nofollow links don’t. So, you want to prioritize building “follow” links.

!["Link Attributes" page shows a graph comparing link attributes](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/14/4a/144a1922f1064b541fb742831a1a83ca/wRQXenBkNneCg64Uhs8bMBAXLEDYai0tV3h29XSK5zoEeBmxQQc3W9cpD8xH_v_vf0insf6eYvoTypTOTqOChVc-bj_OP18L9Om0dDx4ZTPP4x_2bfovkGHaaNAMmrW-9c4XM3dBg9iomdjRikqaHPw.png)

- **Referring Domains**: To find out whether your referring domains are growing consistently. Or whether your referring domains growth is stagnant. And see how your growth compares to your competition.

!["Referring Domains" page shows a graph comparing referring domains over time](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a0/70/a0705398fb94d10883b2ef3b6115607c/36eQnIFTTi0Ew1thbseOyYeZH6tV0oQGLZhVe1VUgqZyBSNYNiOkIausEItH2XQ8b1Rh_bQ0EyERdmakQkbCLGyOACuYej2qWeIfiRrzjUJj7VM2RjCFuxsT8wRNvtrZJ__vbQYI3ltRGZLMu8uljfw.png)

The analysis above should reveal strengths you can capitalize on and weaknesses you’ll want to overcome.

For example, if you notice your link growth is stagnant and your competitors have more backlinks than you, then start building links.

Read our full guide on [link building strategies](https://www.semrush.com/blog/link-building-strategies/) to learn how.

## 2. Find and Evaluate Each Backlink You Have

Now that you know how your backlink profile stacks up against your competition, it’s time to dig deeper and analyze each link individually.

Why?

Some backlinks are more beneficial than others. And if you have a lot of low-quality backlinks, they’re not going to move the needle for your site.

Or worse, they could trigger a manual action penalty from Google (meaning some or all of your pages won’t appear in search results). Especially if your site was involved in any [link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam) (links intended to manipulate organic rankings).

You can use Semrush [Backlink Audit](https://www.semrush.com/backlink_audit/) tool to analyze your backlinks.

First, [start a project](https://www.semrush.com/kb/579-configuring-backlink-audit-tool), configure the tool, and click “**Start Backlink Audit**.”

Once the audit is complete, you’ll see a detailed report. Like this:

!["Overview" tab in Backlink Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/17/87/1787567f8f49f68ce58fdbe052f5a919/8o39uIyLoPgyBBw5fxxFsf98VdDrRw9Jucoph0MqaSMdco-2WxGGxfvpsqQbDFzMfeUz0k5PcZHvgno1vASaGpt0fN8C6o-Zcb0y1dimU0KgIaF--1_3QKWfZIKQjSfaPFbZzxvkSj5P2EfWjztHGGU.png)

### What You Should Analyze

In the “Overview” tab, look at the:

- **Overall Toxicity Score**: To see whether you have a high score, meaning there are too many low-quality links pointing to your website
- **Analyzed Backlinks**: To find out whether the number is higher than what you expected it to be, which could indicate a [negative SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-negative-seo/) attack. In most cases, you don't need to do anything. Google is pretty good at ignoring link-related negative SEO attacks.
- **Referring Domains**: To see how many domains link to your site. Multiple links coming from a single domain won’t have much impact from a ranking standpoint. The key is to build links from as many **unique and relevant** domains as possible.

![important metrics in the "Overview" tab in Backlink Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/57/75/57754c859e306d7606ac3d514909b6df/LjSqB9nyw1boa28y0hjQ8Obyown_9IvGjywD00GINS1FnYpYTYv5LcEts-7X1_LhWmPP5HUy6YjCHachJ5fleyTqlmK3Wz0CNpIwbb1Rp7W92hQC54RUsWGD2iI4nI1mdl9ATOe-ShFVMpU0CkCNJs4.png)

The “Overview” tab gives you a very high-level view. To delve deeper, you need to go to the “**Audit**” tab.

There, you’ll see all the backlinks pointing to your site.

![“Audit” tab shows backlinks pointing to your site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/95/a4/95a4b23fc4a2b729aaa3e90b1a342252/2tCDa7u_LWbgQD3pkSHqoKLDFYTSu0ObZEMox5-Qp49MldcISTWrF2SKhdg0I6dZbRI2GsUf2jUzZy4ybBqEpkH-d5owCbWT5r0oDMyRu3qBT6R3ADPIkmvygigqxh7EZ7WU52xh5UJ3eaZEbeGQ_6w.png)

From here, look at:

- **The URLs**: To see whether they come from legitimate websites
- **Anchor text**: To see whether the [anchor text](https://www.semrush.com/blog/anchor-text/) is relevant to the linked page. High-quality backlinks tend to have anchor text that is relevant to the linked page’s content, while low-quality backlinks tend to have unnatural or spammy anchor text (i.e., something random).
- **Authority Score (AS) of the linking domain**: To gauge the reputability of the linking domain, which impacts your search rankings. A higher AS means a more positive influence on rankings. A lower AS means a less positive impact on rankings.
- **Toxicity Score (TS) of the linking page**: To measure how potentially harmful the linking page could be. The [Toxicity Score](https://www.semrush.com/kb/580-auditing-your-backlinks#:~:text=updated%20your%20Project.-,Toxicity%20Score,-The%20Backlink%20Audit) ranges from 0 to 100. The lower the TS, the higher the link’s quality. The higher the TS, the lower the link’s quality. You can click on the TS score to see why Semrush considers a particular link as low-quality or bad.

## 3. Decide Whether to Disavow or Remove Bad Links

The next step is deciding whether to disavow or remove any bad backlinks.

[Google says](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/2648487) to do this:

- If you have a significant number of low-quality links built using tactics that are against [Google’s guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies?hl=en&visit_id=638237239224735113-1164014359&rd=2#link-spam)
- If you’ve received a manual action penalty from Google

If you’ve deliberately violated Google’s guidelines and built an influx of links in ways that violate Google’s guidelines, you risk everything in terms of SEO.

You’ll receive an alert in Google Search Console notifying you of a manual action penalty.

You can check for these penalties by going to "**Security & Manual Actions**," and then "**Manual actions**" in Google Search Console.

If your website hasn't been issued any penalties, you'll see a green check mark.

Like this:

![Green checkmark with "No issues detected" text](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/6c/27/6c27e51bebd41a8586e3dc9460786569/eLQQ08ww0ru4E6R--NxCWBfTl3Ik3x_hIT8d1F4Sb3qP3G3jxiKdCLyKQuWqIxLwCbPFJMJVSep1Ezs4URMSmdZm0cA7ae_-BkXE2d2sITibPb39nUsXe6hr1yEZ-hV1DrHVm-O9E-F0ziU98yMAedE.png)

You can deal with manual action penalties in one of two ways:

- **Remove**: Reaching out to linking websites and asking them to delete links
- **Disavow**: Disavowing links using [Google’s Disavow tool](https://www.google.com/webmasters/tools/disavow-links-main)

First, reach out to websites and see if you can get those links removed.

You can use Semrush [Backlink Audit](https://www.semrush.com/backlink_audit/) tool for this purpose.

Here’s how:

1. Go to the “**Audit**” tab of the tool and filter for links that have a Toxicity Score of 60 or more. These are likely going to be low-quality, bad links.

2. Check the boxes on the left-hand side. Then, click the “**Remove**” button, select “**Domain**” or “**URL**,” and send them to your “Remove” list.

## 4. Reclaim Your Lost Backlinks

This step is about making sure you recover any links you used to have that you lost.

Building backlinks takes time and effort. So, it pays to reclaim them when you lose them over time.

This happens for many different reasons:

- The author removed your link from the content
- The linking page has been redirected to another page
- The author replaced your link with something else
- The linking page accidentally got deleted

Use Semrush’s [Link Building tool](https://www.semrush.com/link_building/) to recover your hard-earned links.

First, [configure the tool](https://www.semrush.com/kb/827-configuring-link-building). After that, go to the “Prospects” tab and click on “**Lost** **Backlinks.**”

![“Lost Backlinks” metric shows 2376 lost backlinks in Link Building tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e3/2b/e32be7f94eb32d861c04e796b7c57cf9/plEFG7QH3SY93l-2K-MPC68WGV22ZIOkXjsGqaCX3OjPzu-qzljQjjJYsS5TdLSDOrAKRhlFDwKBNUu6yCd2q3rw2XrDwp4KQsPl_p2jW9CutI6SnPvipVlGtGFSJWiNUawMK1CO-oaJ-basBASRi3U.png)

You’ll see links that you used to have.

From here, open each link individually. You’ll be able to see information about why your link disappeared.

For example, if it’s returning a 404 error, it could be because the page was deleted.

Then, you could reach out to the website owner and ask them to reinstate the page.

A simple email like this could do the trick:

*Hey [Name],*

*Just came across your \_\_\_\_ page and found it’s broken.*

*Did you do this on purpose or was it deleted accidentally?*

*In any case, having a broken page on your website is not good for UX or SEO.*

*So, I would recommend reinstating the page.*

*No pressure if you don’t want to do so, though. 🙂 It’s all up to you. I just thought it would be helpful to reach out and let you know.*

*Thanks,*

*[Your Name]*

## 5. Find New Opportunities to Build Valuable Links

A backlink audit is more than just analyzing your backlink profile and reclaiming your lost links. It can also help you spot new opportunities to build high-quality links.

There are two main ways to do this:

1. Studying what content types attract the most backlinks to your site. So you can double down on producing the same types of content to continue attracting links.
2. Researching your competitors’ link building strategies and replicating the same strategy for your site.

Here’s how you can do both.

To look at what content types attract links to your site, go to the “**Indexed** **Pages**” tab in [Backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/backlinks/) tool for your website.

You’ll see your most linked-to content.

![“Indexed Pages” tab in Backlinks tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/46/b1/46b15a6d191770318fd5e260818fe48c/CSy9PafTXudgkKyhmh4r8n4ANrFfN-MRLv_8THuk-iaRxEx2EV7AS0bw9Kzhg5Tuz48vXGW1FOkTTE5BX2gBc-iPQlYnx1gNzCMzg5fqURhLRjbgrwhObjHVrmp3ba9_R3-HO5q6hu162DFav3B2QYI.png)

If you see that listicle-style posts are the top link earners, create more listicle posts.

If you see how-to guides attracting links, create more how-to posts.

Now, let’s go over how you can research your competitors’ backlinks to develop new link building strategies.

Open the [backlink tool](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/backlinks/), enter a competitor's domain, and then click "Analyze." Like this:

![enter a competitor’s domain in Backlinks tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c2/9c/c29c8f5ac5a8ec06b81465b3fc1a5559/50EulEq3w5YwjjV-4xDsNemAsaWxsaXvzCSDC2DDoToJXpIihfogdMyreAbN5YeW012OGkXAzbNppEV_CeLJ6Z0DXUxUQOMiHa9Ni3qtAaqNbqjXJJUey2n5J4ANADW3dJyGqTfKw721QlcYgJSH89E.png)

You’ll see all the websites that are linking to that competitor. Like this:

![“Referring Domains” tab in Backlinks tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/88/3f/883fcc4e7577306e9aa8eb0eedf31d88/bRWGAy_as1gbYDRqlwCHvkUDwcuaPQKfRppJi650_BWqJm7q2Ew6iLbInipfC8XDGQ8kYSWlammaS_M_vMGdBTIT0g4MU3rauJfnrT4rG0Y_OgcaFK4a50JJrum0VaszyqbZXo4FJ_XEkzGSHiizNvY.png)

Look at the domains that frequently link to them. Or types of websites that link to them the most.

For example, if you see your competitor is getting links from popular media outlets like BBC, TechCrunch, etc., this probably means they’re putting efforts into digital PR.

You could do the same for your own site. And earn links that way.

Read our guide to [digital PR](https://www.semrush.com/blog/digital-pr/) to learn how.
