# How to Build a Website (Start to Finish, With Walkthroughs)

You don’t need to be tech-savvy to build a website.

With AI, you can bypass coding, hiring a developer, or staring at a blank page for hours. You can automate everything from writing content to choosing a professional layout and images.

This guide walks you through the entire process, step by step.

Imagine having a fully functional website up and running in a few hours. Whether you’re launching a business, portfolio, or personal project, here’s how to get started the simple way.

## Step 1. Plan out your website and its goals

Before you click through templates or generate content with AI, it’s important to step back and get clear on what you’re trying to build.

A bit of upfront planning goes a long way. Here are a few things to think through before jumping into design tools:

- **What’s the goal of your website?** Are you trying to attract clients, sell products, showcase your work, or simply have a presence online?
- **Who are you trying to reach?** Knowing your audience will shape everything from tone of voice to layout and features.
- **What makes your brand different?** Even a one-line description of your unique selling points helps AI tools generate better content.
- **What are people actually searching for on Google?** This is where keyword research comes in. It helps you speak the same language as your audience and appear in search results.

You don’t need a 20-page business plan, just a simple outline to guide your decisions.

### The most popular website builders

If you want a tried and true solution, a website builder that’s been around for years and has a solid support community, it’s worth considering:

- [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/) – Perfect if you’re building an online store with built-in payment, inventory, and shipping features.
- [Wix](https://www.wix.com/) – One of the easiest platforms for beginners. Great for small service businesses and creative portfolios.
- [Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/) – Popular for personal brands and creative portfolios. Visually polished with easy drag-and-drop editing.
- [WordPress](https://wordpress.org/) – Offers the most customizability and flexibility, especially for SEO, and can be paired with a drag-and-drop builder, like [Elementor](https://elementor.com/), to make it easier for beginners.

These platforms have a ton of helpful resources and libraries of pre-made templates, like [Squarespace’s:](https://www.squarespace.com/templates)

If you choose to use WordPress, it will have a few extra setup steps that other platforms handle automatically.

For instance, you’ll need to connect it to hosting, backups, and security.

I use [SiteGround](https://au.siteground.com/) for my sites. Over the years, I’ve tested over 200 hosting platforms for speed, security, backup reliability, uptime, and ease of use. SiteGround consistently comes out as a top beginner-friendly option.

You’ll also need to add plugins to enhance WordPress’s base functionality. You can figure out what you need as you go, but some basic ones to consider include:

- An SEO plugin like [RankMath](https://en-au.wordpress.org/plugins/seo-by-rank-math/), or its AI-powered equivalent, [Rocketship SEO](https://en-au.wordpress.org/plugins/rocketship-seo/)
- [SeedProd](https://wordpress.org/plugins/search/seedprod/), if you want to set up a Coming Soon page until you’re ready to launch
- [Site Kit by Google](https://sitekit.withgoogle.com/) to connect your website to Google Search Console and more
- [WP Rocket](https://wp-rocket.me/) for caching and improving your website’s loading speed and technical performance
- [Ahrefs SEO](https://ahrefs.com/wordpress-seo-plugin) for easy content audits to check how each page is performing over time

### Up-and-coming AI website builders

The biggest downside with traditional website builders is the learning curve. Without code or design skills, it can be challenging for non-technical folks to build a professional website quickly and with minimal frustration.

That’s where some newer AI-powered and “vibe coding” solutions may be up your alley. Some popular platforms include:

- [Looka](https://looka.com/) — For AI-generated brand kits and assets, including simple websites.
- [Bolt](https://bolt.new/) — For creating an entire site (multi-page layouts, copy, and images) from a single prompt.
- [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/) — For ultra-fast one-prompt, website prototypes with a strong design aesthetic.
- [Framer](https://www.framer.com/) — For sleek, responsive websites with built-in animations and components.
- [Relume](https://www.relume.io/) — For building wireframes, sitemaps, and page layouts fast.

Generally, the less you can control and customize within a website builder (especially with the technical elements and code that search engines need to see), the more likely it is to have SEO issues.

Many of these new AI website builders fall into this category, and their SEO capabilities do not (yet) match those of classic platforms like WordPress.

This might be ok for your needs, at least as you’re starting out.

But at some point, you’ll need to consider hiring a professional developer or learning some technical skills, so your business can be found in Google and LLM searches.

## Step 5: Build your SEO pages using simple templates

While most themes are a great place to start, you must go beyond the pages that come with them if you want your website to appear in search engines and LLMs.

To speed things up and stay consistent, create a few reusable templates for common page types, such as service and location pages.

You could also create templates for key sections you’d like to use across many pages. If your website builder already has some pre-built templates, it’s worth going through and customizing your favorites for:

- Testimonials
- Calls to action
- Benefit statements
- Your process
- Contact forms

These can be duplicated and tweaked as needed, saving you time later.

Once you have these elements in place, it’s just a matter of using them to build the SEO pages you planned out earlier. For instance, for my brother’s plumbing site, I could build out:

- **Service pages** for each of the core plumbing and gas services that have search potential.

## Step 6: Design the navigation menu and footer

Your website’s navigation helps people find what they’re looking for. It needs to be simple and clear.

Start by setting up your main navigation menu, usually found at the top of every page.

Include your most important pages like Home, Services, About, and Contact. Also have at least one call to action (like a phone number or a “book now” button).

If you have multiple locations or services, consider using drop-down menus to group them.

Next, design your footer. This is a good place to add:

- Contact info
- Social media links
- Legal pages (Privacy Policy, Terms of Use)
- Newsletter signup (if relevant)
- A small Google map (if relevant)

If you’re building a blog, think about adding a sidebar with a table of contents to help readers navigate longer posts.

Internal links (like linking from your homepage to a service page) also help visitors and search engines understand your site better. Most website builders make it easy to connect pages with just a few clicks.

Just think about how you want people to move from one page to the next on your website and add links, buttons, and navigation elements to make that journey easier for them.

## Step 7: Set up your SEO fundamentals

If you want people to find your website on Google or ChatGPT, follow these simple steps to optimize your website for SEO.

Start by navigating to the SEO section of your website builder. Here’s what it looks like in Looka:

If you’re using WordPress, go to the settings screens for your SEO plugin and follow these [best practices](https://ahrefs.com/blog/wordpress-seo/).

Take your time to read through every setting and customize it to your needs. Pay attention to [page titles](https://ahrefs.com/seo/glossary/title-tag) and [meta descriptions](https://ahrefs.com/blog/meta-description/). Make sure every page has them:

If not, you can use our free [AI writing tools](https://ahrefs.com/writing-tools) to help fill these out.

For titles, add a keyword first, then a benefit or unique selling point—think “Plumber in Melbourne | Fast, Reliable Service” instead of just “Home.”

You can also use the descriptions to persuade a searcher to click on your page from Google.

Next, make sure your images have [alt text](https://ahrefs.com/blog/alt-text/). This is important for search engines and also for accessibility reasons. Alt text describes the content of an image, and you can usually add it to each image in the media library of your website platform:

Speed matters too. Slow sites drive visitors away.

Most builders, like Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, and Looka, [optimize page speed](https://ahrefs.com/seo/glossary/page-speed) automatically. But you can help by keeping your images small before uploading and reducing the number of apps or plugins your website uses.

Finally, check if your platform created a [sitemap](https://ahrefs.com/seo/glossary/sitemap) (a file that helps search engines crawl your site). Some tools do this for you, but plugins like RankMath can handle it easily if you’re using WordPress.

There are a few ways to find it:

- Look at the SEO settings in your website builder for the sitemap section
- Add /sitemap.xml after your home page URL
- Add /sitemap\_index.xml after your home page URL
- Add /robots.txt at the end of your homepage URL and see if there’s a sitemap link included

Sitemaps can look like a page of code sometimes.

You can ignore the code and focus on the parts that start with “https://”. These are the links to each of your pages. If you haven’t published any pages yet, this will likely be empty.

These basics go a long way in helping your site get discovered. For more guidance, you can also check out our [Beginner’s Guide to Technical SEO](https://ahrefs.com/seo/technical-seo).

## Step 9. Hit ‘publish” and celebrate your site launch!

Once your pages are ready, it’s time to go live and hit publish! But, before you announce it to the world, double-check a few things.

Ensure your site isn’t accidentally set to “noindex” (which blocks Google). You can often find this in the main settings or the SEO settings of your website builder, though it can be worded a little differently.

Look for the setting that allows search engines to crawl and display your site in search results. Here’s the setting in WordPress, for example:

Next, remove any demo content or placeholder pages that were accidentally published. For instance, if you see anything like this, unpublish it ASAP!

You’ll need to carefully check every single page to make sure you haven’t missed anything.

Next, you should confirm your sitemap is live and working. Check that all your pages are included here. Also, check for any accidental pages that went live without you realizing (and unpublish them asap).

The last thing you need to do is to go through each page and click the links and buttons to make sure they’re all working as expected.

After you’re done, share the good news!

Post it on Google Business Profile, social media, or in your email newsletter.

If something breaks down the track, don’t stress. Most issues can be fixed live. You can set up Ahrefs’ Site Audit to alert you if technical errors pop up on your site.

If you’re tech-savvy, you can also find directions on how to fix major errors yourself.

Otherwise, you can give these instructions to a developer to take care of it for you.

If you’re more technical, you can set up a staging site for testing. But for most beginners, it’s totally fine to go live first and tweak things after.

## Final thoughts

There’s no such thing as a perfect website, especially not at launch.

What matters most is that your site exists, reflects your business, and gives people a way to find and trust you online. Don’t wait until it’s flawless. You can always improve things later.

As your business grows, so will your site. When updates or fixes become too time-consuming, that’s when it makes sense to bring in a developer to help.

For now? Focus on building something useful, publish it proudly, and start sharing it with the world.

Done is better than perfect, especially when your next customer is already out there searching for your product or service.
