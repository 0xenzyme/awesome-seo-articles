# Website Health: A Guide to Optimal Online Performance

## What Is Website Health?

Website health is that foundation that keeps your site working and performing properly.

It’s conveyed by a collection of metrics that measure your site based on how secure, easy to use, and optimized for search engine visibility it is.

And maintaining a healthy site can help you attract more traffic and potentially increase conversions or sales.

But how do you know if your site is healthy? Let’s get into that next.

## How to Measure Website Health

Different website health checker tools can all look at site health a bit differently, giving weight to different variables.

Run our free [SEO checker](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/) for a quick assessment of your site.

For a deeper site-wide audit, Semrush Site Audit runs a holistic website health check by looking at components like:

- [**Crawlability**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-crawlability-and-indexability-of-a-website/): How well search engines can navigate your site
- [**On-page SEO**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/on-page-seo/): How well-optimized your pages are for search engines and users
- [**Technical SEO**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/technical-seo/): How strong your site is from a technical standpoint. Which looks at things like site structure and mobile-friendliness.
- **Mobile-friendliness**: How easy your site is to use on mobile devices
- **Speed**: How quickly your website loads and responds to user requests

## 8 Common Website Health Issues and Solutions

Let’s take a look at some of the most common issues that you may have identified in your [technical SEO audit](https://www.semrush.com/blog/technical-seo-audit/). And how to fix them.

### Broken Internal Links

A [broken link](https://www.semrush.com/blog/broken-link/) leads users and search engine crawlers to a page or resource that no longer exists or can't be found.

This can happen if the linked page is moved or deleted. Or if the URL you’ve linked to is typed incorrectly.

Multiple broken links can hurt your site’s user experience and crawlability—which limits your ability to appear in search results.

To fix broken link issues, start by identifying them using [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/).

Go to the "**Issues**" tab, click the "**Category**" drop-down, and select "**Crawlability**."

If your site has broken links, you’ll see them here:

!["143 internal links are broken" issue highlighted under "Errors" section in Site Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b1/e2/b1e2e65cc3857eb70635e474f3211235/f38a41d8a32aa8cdc5a266e3a37d0df9/RUdqsCiLtKHB-0y5vOcL55gbk37pcGni0CF2i46oJGo_LAoyK-xotJDZNiCFjk114nOVTzDa1mRkyNie11jyH60TOqmjlnb3RyfX3nMdF-JONJg4KHbQ86ncHguB5rgh4mMFFV-_XVcShhAnAQxNhYA.png)

Click on the issue to see a list of all the affected links.

From here, update the broken link with an appropriate one or remove the link.

### Redirect Chains and Loops

A redirect is a way to automatically send both users and search engines to a different URL than the one that was originally requested.

There are many reasons to do this. But if you set one redirect that leads to another redirect, it creates a redirect chain.

Here’s a visual representation:

![A visual showing what a redirect chain is](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/3d/80/3d80824f81ae21a9972f61ee603fce24/f321a3b7f0f053869dbdcf5b89697e61/UvcxMPzbZ2H4UVRAxXLwNfyJwk1l08foffXWCf06o8NFMs0sSJsAm4DBnPwCJ5TyRhoQHRNIAyo-vQ_pUcw_y6hB3at648y5KYUQZbJVw11O_79_gmPSb1AXZ7GQv90NfQ0C1zsT299rZa-kEPZbSog.jpeg)

A redirect loop happens when a URL is redirected back to itself. Or when there’s a series of redirects that ultimately lead back to the first URL, creating an endless loop.

Like this:

![A visual showing what a redirect loop is](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/2a/01/2a0193c1f6a9c8b41e3b9f45642eb7ec/e6599aa7af0d7456a6b812ee66bea686/0Ihq0I_YQbnuxAUMP_b8DCQ25J6ECRZFAkuzqig_54Wc4Tt1c6loRCXmyqyGKQck8d9PN11cogpIk57h7D-7wPD8m0G62V6aIeQYEUpjEkjI90wCIB4PjzLjTMkgrB2jPUpUg_h9aaIrANlqgdvOyFw.jpeg)

Redirect loops and chains can hurt your site health by causing longer load times, confusing search engines, and frustrating users.

To fix the issue, use [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/) to identify any chains and loops on your site.

You can find this error under the "Crawlability" category on your report’s "Issues" page.

!["3 redirect chains and loops" issue highlighted under "Errors" section in Site Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/7d/9b/7d9b5f5e8e1b1815eb31bdb737cba2a3/8bc550b2ddb3dcf5301df169b38f81ab/yc5pngTm98_xzY9M9HVaZ_FtkvrKGBtM-j9SKl3Gu57D7wsmVSwMZ9xX0Cp0BD3u5xs_3YBep-VcomBm3_Z9WA61jTPuuN4kkzAeniLOB779fZItD0VvhrS-nCXJi-ou-oenKmNAzFIqsP1M-D8ohlw.jpeg)

Click on the "**# redirect chains and loops**" issue for a list of the affected URLs.

![A list of the redirect chain and loops affected URLs](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/3a/e2/3ae21727ac3fd74eebc7728432cf1ca3/7c8c17450cfdd58d2b3f51598c4cb8b7/5lPr9v_Lwj7TQAvIP-2Q6OhcuOGdlhoSc4UQCLz9eRrFPkkPX04KRDsxKLmxxi9ynCLvfzmoC9kuoUaTlAKS3qEEZNbwPb8OeZUTu9-OqHiGE8b6HHnIV4-JqBRGrudKn2UiXHsYZVDzn_g-4VEGh5A.jpeg)

Address chains by redirecting each URL in the chain to your final destination page.

To fix loops, you’ll likely have to change one of the redirects so it points to an appropriate final destination. Instead of going back to the previous URL in the sequence.

### Duplicate Content

[Duplicate content](https://www.semrush.com/blog/duplicate-content/) is when the same or very similar content appears on multiple pages within your site.

It can hurt your website health by confusing search engines about which version to index or rank, potentially diluting your site’s visibility and performance in search results.

Find duplicate content by going to the "Issues" tab in [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/).

And click the "Categories" drop-down and select "**Indexability**":

!["0 pages have duplicate content issues" result highlighted under "Errors" section in Site Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/9c/94/9c942c174e731b698524345cbfa5445b/d2d60b13a86c8996dd4fe4861b12aa7d/mxg4ax2I-sj0PvbkT66RIpHUioaUFA81WRzPDP73gu8OD_2uQhZH2U_mqv_8rWfDIVWsnnfQSc9CNgIZwa2UcZJ29lyphLo11ISr7pfQ2K7rqINBnxe58sHHJYsHRWKgLtD9X46B5vEDMcnlYDSKo20.png)

If you find duplicate pages, consider rewriting the content to be unique and original for each page.

You can also use [canonical tags](https://www.semrush.com/blog/canonical-url-guide/) to help signal to search engines which version is the original.

### Mobile-Friendliness Issues

Over [58%](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/worldwide) of web traffic happens on mobile devices. And Google uses [mobile-first indexing](https://www.semrush.com/blog/mobile-first-indexiing/)—meaning it prioritizes indexing and ranking the mobile versions of your pages over the desktop ons.

So, addressing mobile-friendliness issues enables you to attract a larger audience and improves your site’s usability.

Run a free [SEO check](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/) to quickly spot mobile-friendliness issues. For site-wide mobile coverage, use Semrush Site Audit.

Go to the "**Issues**" tab in Site Audit. And set the category filter to "**Mobile SEO**":

![Category filter set to "Mobile SEO" under "Issues" tab in Site Audit](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8e/46/8e467ab62874244a818b74432f7d8f2d/af1f6983e2a576e5184489b957ae5cbd/FtHdLCcOfGP89SRPGNVoIaBbqik1lM2rpSe7k-Ojb5Eg7_L37dpb_JMLKAbWI0vc2pjz7SVkO9Mde2s3gkC_WFr-fguo--xQGfoY34uJSg3rStr6OcvsFQv8JOpoWDhFrb6dg43IHw6hWAl34wPYQBk.jpeg)

One issue you’ll see is related to viewport tags—pieces of HTML code that instruct a webpage to scale to different screen sizes to keep it user-friendly.

Click on the "**# pages have no viewport tag**" error to find the affected pages.

!["3 pages have no viewport tag" result highlighted under "Errors" section in Site Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/38/5f/385f3b7aeaed49041b3204d98a0e8887/e623e7a96acf186b02ca7573374e3cd2/MUEGinrbxtdinDw-weWmdXTaXC-0NUF9eFw-Bgzlf_fMFu92HCY-lzOLIXAecF2B8a28c9aqewTN1nB46Klzjyb_p8Qx7ucA_JM5_EfmA3y0ueTPtNd2Iw4p5ZnF2gmJcgdv5T05uySDVEJTq2QRdLU.jpeg)

Then, add the following tag to the <head> section of your source code:

`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">`

For a more in-depth walkthrough, visit our guide to [viewport meta tags](https://www.semrush.com/blog/viewport-meta-tag/).

### Security Vulnerabilities

[Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-https/) is a more secure version of HTTP. And it’s a set of rules that helps safely send data between browsers and servers.

If a site is using HTTPS, visitors will see a padlock symbol in their browser’s address bar. This symbol assures them that their data is encrypted and secure.

![A padlock symbol in browser’s address bar, with HTTPS protocol](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/15/3b/153b48f5d009ab587fb8bf26fd1c751c/1d94cf44ee4efc17605c1e70e06a5b9b/tFdUm313hoCzz9Z8baDCQleVXMOpD_9CpEDIKBG0nUh53fKjlhVeDzwomE_IyvAa34I8czBAoF4OJ7D2P_T8KBDbTtRd1r4pDt-VfP6FIPRlx5Rn7y1u2uzI04wN8oxIAhMr1JwG4OTEEEMQ0O5HlmE.png)

If your site is accessed over HTTP, browsers may label it as "not secure." Which tells users that their personal information (like payment data) isn’t protected by encryption.

Plus, Google considers a site’s security as a [ranking factor](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2014/08/https-as-ranking-signal). So if your site isn’t using HTTPS, it might become less prominent in search results.

There are a number of other security issues as well.

To check for any of these possible security issues your site might have, select the "**Security and HTTPS**" category filter while on they "**Issues**" tab in [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/):

!["Security and HTTPS" category filter selected under the "Issues" tab in Site Audit](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ce/b1/ceb17917ddbcc9ef3b95a66d98725b20/3a4c9a60ffa2ecc01eefa244c3f8ccd7/bxwYQmgDMZwWPDJFSvOohBjG5F9iuiwzGhy2wIF8Vj4XZFq0i7vCupdt0ICwNxhYwdkydMZ9puUDFlc3nGgQWxIH_rFQkrDC6H9TO2H94chZL3sE_AdDKKGMDYBkvk2d3YPxoHJk3nSLzKwpTm7ThPM.png)

From there, follow the recommendations to address the problems.

### Slow Page Speed

[Page speed](https://www.semrush.com/blog/page-speed/) is how fast your site loads and becomes interactive for your visitors.

If your site takes too long to load, users will leave and find what they’re looking for somewhere else. This makes optimizing page speed crucial for providing a good user experience.

Page load speed is also an important [ranking factor](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2010/04/using-site-speed-in-web-search-ranking) for Google. Which means faster loading times can contribute to your page performing better in organic search results.

Run a free [SEO audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/) for a quick speed check. For ongoing speed monitoring across your entire site, use Semrush Site Audit.

Simply click "**View details**" in the "Site Performance" module on the "Overview" report.

!["Site Performance" widget highlighted in the Site Audit's "Overview" dashboard](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/42/e7/42e71b8089e8b0723137ce2923bb6df0/3c99c0e3f31a48f0d2b0510298c29b53/n-LMlkkNAQf8abbwUSIswPPus-pPpooeWQSM7uhBCJXrDJS594SSFC8x5diaTjnTHRbUyyyDdSGwclGAuSiEm6YvhVBN06TD_xB3y-GHxJaolbAemXUfRLoKRD5DSoZBy1S-zDphE1JFp_FkbRo4O_4.png)

You’ll see a page similar to this one:

![A section of "Site Performance" dashboard in Site Audit](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/84/98/84983515cfe8440bac9a4a6d7a805ba4/f14f370dc1b731bfdbcb064d570931a7/DacIAHs7SKCy4Pk8bW-kkmtA6jqmzcD6UZWZShoNYcSTiZID98ZDfUDvAIKGlfQgjM7jtYWoKDuZYjb2H8MZtWtqLIemd8sYEvT-GaXIQnBdGZMIAuUfzSiPtUtpJDRkWltMVR0-6l_heWBN4Kink8E.png)

One of the most common errors you might see is "Large HTML page size."

This error means your page’s HTML code is bigger than recommended. This could be due to heavy code, lots of embedded media (like images and videos), or third-party scripts (like add-ons and widgets).

You can reduce your HTML size by optimizing:

- **Images**: Compress them or opt for more web-friendly image formats like WebP.
- **Fonts**: Opt for using system fonts like Arial, which are already installed on most users’ devices. This speeds up your page because it avoids having to download new fonts.
- **Code**: Remove unnecessary code elements. Tools like [HTML Minifier](https://github.com/kangax/html-minifier) can help you do this automatically.
- **Plugins and add-ons**: If you’re using a CMS like WordPress, go through all the extras you've installed on your site. And remove any that you no longer need.

### Broken Images

Broken images are images on a website that fail to load or display properly to the user. Which can happen if the image no longer exists, its URL is misspelled, or because the file path isn’t valid.

If there’s a broken image on your page, users might see it as a blank space or a broken image icon like this:

![A broken image icon on a page](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/6e/04/6e049adee4011d96f69ec12c19eb2fc9/bb6384a92c1a1d4b081b7172b18f9706/D3uW09ZhhQrCIT_dUsow3xsLp5TIlII_MPQRCrE1jSTxpDzFryW7Sul2cEudzmp7Py7RRgnsMYyJQ81M4FYFAB5Ryps_44gDePyX2WTy_WwnA8pz-_n5gP7AA6ia1cO6ESE0bugA0TjtciHDan8b__c.jpeg)

Broken images hurt the user experience and make the site appear unprofessional. And that can indirectly affect your rankings.

You can find this error by going to the "**Issues**" report in [Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/). And choosing the "**Crawlability**" category filter.

!["1 internal image is broken" result highlighted under the "Errors" section in Site Audit tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/74/42/744295a81aeac9a767a1f657a2c7364d/2a5228c6e7dc3fe4c6d2d8f714a4e642/W6V70v1fZCkm6w7USaBgZJurcRqVEUKMfhHWbKSL-u1O_4-fIWg_NDFsq2XDOTKdxnJEBxAkQ8sQJBJan8I-oMDuHwxmgTSGwOEVpGqxbglNi0zWpnj2kP7cLGsX6G9127fTa8viCmap3jXPbGYDa-4.png)

To fix a broken image that’s been moved, update the URL to the correct one.

If an image was deleted or damaged, replace it with a new one. Or if you think your page doesn’t need that image anymore, just remove it from your page’s code.
