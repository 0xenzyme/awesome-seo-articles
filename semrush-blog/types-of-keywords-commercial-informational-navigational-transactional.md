# 4 Types of Keywords in SEO (+ Examples)

## What Are the Different Types of Keywords?

The different types of keywords for SEO are: informational, navigational, commercial, and transactional. These categories reflect the general purpose behind a user’s search.

- **Informational**: Searchers want to gain knowledge about a topic
- **Navigational**: Searchers want to find a specific website or page
- **Commercial**: Searchers want to research brands, products, or services
- **Transactional**: Searchers want to complete an action (for example, make a purchase)

There are other types of keywords too, like local or long-tail keywords. Focusing on intent, however, helps you figure out how to rank better in search engine result pages (SERPs).

A [searcher's intent](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/) shows whether they're ready to buy, browsing, or just learning.

Let’s look at the different types of keywords in more detail below.

## 1. Informational

Informational keywords are search queries used by people who want to learn something. Examples include:

- “Coffee calories”
- “National coffee day”
- “What is the difference between cold brew and iced coffee”

!["coffee calories" keyword showing informational intent in Keyword Overview tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/6e/a9/6ea95a4ffa5510b663e110e7cdf4555e/927c2a8b87028c9eb4556d70937f9bdd/AD_4nXcsHHTyrly9v8yaJEFDOmhxSMoWWRwN7AiUYErYsBhlK07QPRZLlZYrVDe4zd6ISAixYDxX8U7Ga8VvfSG0AKbUThVvBi8pHW-aP2QIfGz1td-hk1vWXi80z2o7N9ed5tpaVM7pAx-80Mava8pWI7joUrs.png)

Many informational queries take the form of questions, starting with words like “what,” “why,” or “how.” Google often displays articles, videos, or guides for informational searches.

![Google search results on coffee calories from Healthline, Mayo Clinic, and Calories.info](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/76/b5/76b5e9acc30775baf4b943f14b8252ef/78a56713d039bbcd2ee2f0861790c29a/AD_4nXcAZpcJgmWUyAf6aR6zsKRBGaVaVbPzSOY1s3fJeVHluNM4qSujzkAox57OWKKZYEiC8raup_Fpl1d2_AtfdCUp5LD4NmoncprMtcdIy-27XdkyaAHs1duI9IsXA-A55Oj3V1XpDvlUpzLEgv5CEJa7gIZM.png)

Sometimes you'll see a quick answer pop up—a [featured snippet](https://www.semrush.com/blog/featured-snippets/).

![featured snippet for “what is the difference between cold brew and iced coffee”](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/7e/e1/7ee136861265b5c7792335693a2f0c82/f4a84c3d55ca222349b8606a4bf140b9/AD_4nXcbPUHlzcCC1-_8_6_zyPqWq6qUhcf-JHRkItbAAhDYMq2-C1gEjKoTzuk8prPLtJl_I3AXJDcCPwxsvLbmcbZ2497lf20DCuZwy6UBS_K5Sc1v548oSFr65Bsp6TLX8N7w3W2rEepA3RwAOXQ5n3xfNQ.png)

These keywords are usually at the top of the [marketing funnel](https://www.semrush.com/blog/marketing-funnel/). People at this stage are less likely to make a purchase. Still, informational content can help build brand awareness and position you as an authority in your niche.

## 2. Navigational

Navigational keywords lead searchers to a specific site or page. Typing a brand or website name into a search engine is often a navigational query. Examples include:

- “Youtube”
- “Semrush blog”
- “Where is angelino’s coffee located”

Searchers already know the company or product and want to reach a certain website or location.

!["youtube" keyword showing navigational intent in Keyword Overview tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/93/e7/93e71d9e4314101c9cacf8b058851c9c/02b444d4953a14021806249e91285df1/AD_4nXcjF77o4kKWZTmIGF1pK9z7SEbZ6-t7gZTbPn07g0PwU6lDSKRb9ccFqCiHF_z_BajmiYWz6BBPvBUmMJPDZyqO2SyH1Ea9uSHhBjobHkDv14JIPXyq9MoznANtw8dvaYtqJx8pCSBYTpyNjuwdbd_V4lHe.png)

When a brand or site is well-known, ranking for navigational keywords should come naturally.

For example, when users Google “YouTube,” the top result is YouTube’s website with relevant [sitelinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/sitelinks/):

![Google search results for "YouTube," showing links to Music, History, Channel, HER YouTube Channel, and Subscriptions feed](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/da/56/da563c5dee37ca11b746c2b04cb4e9ad/06ed34aa616b393d6fac5992533bf303/AD_4nXdbIG6OCULD03Q_JVwr5VFc6SZVF31HRqEmpU_k8x2WzB6TyXd0wcOT-V14V-O7qFVoirndKsgz3-brH50PTGCWx0lDUn11HC0_TGGyxpYD68dyo7TWicP_7s4WO-jqzdMOvdRck1YwI45ovmNtdG8ukdLV.png)

## 3. Commercial

Commercial keywords help people research specific products or services. They may check reviews, compare options, or look for discounts. Examples include:

- “Free coffee”
- “Dunkin iced coffee flavors”
- “Peet’s cold brew vs starbucks cold brew”

!["free coffee" keyword showing commercial intent in Keyword Overview tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/97/b0/97b00e56a64c8ec55b1fd05a000c9fd7/312fc78431a57d5353f71ac6682a658c/AD_4nXci1CF1P7kqtnbrng0JgOi_6e27S-Y2T8SBPaZP8ndoWB8FUM3mIi8FBZ9kCov50Q9AECf9uw5tYq3y2PGpUfmi391pIVRdh5alh4ui60cnhGS1Aa-GV-gKHPtvq4nHFtvtDiWuvgcXwSySrNJWi12SEkYY.png)

Searchers with these queries often plan to buy soon. Content such as comparisons, listicles, reviews, or how-to articles can target these searches.

For instance, someone searching “Peet’s cold brew vs starbucks cold brew” will probably see a comparison article that guides their purchase decision:

![Google SERP for "Peet's coldbrew vs starbucks coldbrew" showing top result from Daily Bruin, and a "People also ask" section](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/60/4d/604da0a8cf0821a3ee060fcead2124db/8f6a01577ca4f1c84ebe1297a4a173fd/AD_4nXc0p7YS1uEgsdynXO4KlofUDOXIFK3-H0hrcU2xXZcQdy9zNoQa03XDGDSli94yNfRjSlPD3pjDSmxvYEnAoivn5QW36sq6EKJ9Ha2_SfXjvCP5zZWVVdhUJ4IRBMmGKtDO_GF7XLiHePwXapwcZaqmV3Md.png)

To make the most of commercial keywords, create high-value content. Answer any related questions (like those found in the “[People Also Ask](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-to-maximize-people-also-ask-seo-opportunities-study/)” section) to offer a more complete resource.

## 4. Transactional

Transactional keywords show the strongest intent to buy or complete an action. Target these with ads and optimized [product pages](https://www.semrush.com/blog/product-page/) or [landing pages](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-a-landing-page/) where users can act immediately. Examples include:

- “Buy crypto online”
- “Sandwich places near me that deliver”
- “Pickup truck for sale”

!["pickup truck for sale" keyword showing transactional intent in Keyword Overview tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/a1/51/a15187ed8bfd3b3593d02febe5fe2144/c5711da9a943fe3f0249d864951368ca/AD_4nXdIeft6xMeyRDQXM-oIKV7TXg5rAoP5TvFUvYUP1UAFuT-TBvAB10I5wQ5S7kWbYqh-i4Il_DlcMdwV3VJYE3rf8Y3eEv6fp6l84TG4zvCF6palZLsHOfokMpO57Xaiu2XLvaE-2FlerSrOHRISEt52g1Ki.png)

These searches usually occur after a person has finished researching and is ready to purchase—that’s why they’re also called [**buyer keywords**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/buyer-keywords/).

For example, “buy crypto online” leads to pages where users can buy cryptocurrency directly.

![Google search results for "buy crypto online", showing top links from Bitcoin.com, BitPay, MoonPay, and Crypto.com](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/da/ce/daced9d0ddddd343ca4b7ba6784c736c/01864763cf4dca990afe10fbd14ec5f6/AD_4nXf9Td0KTwk4o_J4vaYUrHXoFeogI2A0vq6n02hJvJGBih6-xSj9_ASyFk1Qk--QxGtDwr_3T3-r5nbfyyeqqdS19PyWVkxV01OT9dud6muTh65Q2LQov-V229p22vMiHVxIMae0gdWzn6CyxZOXb8boeyKv.jpeg)

Transactional keywords often include terms like “buy,” “subscribe,” or “for sale,” which can trigger [Google Shopping Ads](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-shopping-ads/), like these:

![Google Shopping Ads for "clever coffee dripper" search](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/bc/6a/bc6a3384ea0d7f2cf1eeedbd24a045ee/a519dcb642323fa5db1a42c73339515d/AD_4nXfXwbgkaNjqohYuqLN3n3yVgO2fUt8LVCxs45kRLYbdPRJFbTLvTirw9Mx-QnNeO01fRSkMxXOgrxzBEsT9nJlGUvQ1CCdZhcPt4JIzXMdGqa51K0GpngFpk6ENbP8BCtliGOS6ujI5_mfjzfp1IEE5L-ob.png)

Because transactional keywords represent the bottom of the funnel, they are highly valuable for SEO and pay-per-click (PPC) strategies.

Generally, more precise keywords (e.g., “neon blue unisex watch”) are transactional, while broader ones (e.g., “men’s watches”) are commercial.

## How to Check Types of Keywords

Determining intent can be tricky. Sometimes a single keyword, like “apple,” could refer to the fruit—or the brand. One way to check is by analyzing what Google displays on the SERP.

In the U.S., for example, typing “apple” into Google gives you information about the tech brand, meaning the search engine recognizes “apple” as a navigational keyword:

![Google’s search results for “apple” in the U.S](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f3/93/f3933badfbff83b68fb5866b52601082/b7c4512481721ad3eabf325ae6accb44/AD_4nXcoziuEVHHaOb8-hqEUTAKVfphjxodRSxmFtMTcQU50zukoS7xcF_ov54_lQxWpQ1kmytO4Nlcu6u1VcjrUo6mPbUhYcighrCxPoyktYb0lq8bZkY81WM-P-E4j5-sPIwEg8pTrfPIFiJJjrnHzyt1BuyXp.png)

Some queries, like “coffee beans,” may return both commercial **and** informational results—indicating **mixed intent**.

![Google’s search results for “coffee beans”](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/f3/40/f3404bee5ecc6327307b02b764b98c35/bddca1a5deccd7b22a6de7f1f7bc733d/AD_4nXc10mRx9FNEIWfGvn8SY5x4OAVH28Uc3N9L7WgOxbl2NqXijlJzgSjdmjkj2lgi8nwU-YWCg9XIykmNHhBVLf0VUDv4KQ__C4vsylRPjzyC2g6rbV9dlWS007lQ08KE0ZM9lkNHE9sAEw8nWkfg7xQ1Da2G.png)

**I** (informational), **N** (navigational), **C** (commercial), or **T** (transactional).

## How to Optimize for Different Types of Keywords

Here’s how to optimize your website content for the different types of keywords.

### Optimizing for Informational Keywords

Optimizing for informational keywords includes producing [high-quality content](https://www.semrush.com/blog/quality-content/) that demonstrates Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness ([E-E-A-T](https://www.semrush.com/blog/eeat/)).

Check which pages rank for your target query, include detailed explanations, address FAQs, cite expert authors, and link to reputable sources.

For example, say you’re trying to target the informational keyword “vegan diet.”

See what kind of content Google wants to return for this term. Take a look at the SERP by Googling your term. See which pages are ranking and if there are any relevant SERP features (like “People Also Ask”).

![Google search results page for "vegan diet," showing results from NHS and WebMD and a "People also ask" section](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/5b/75/5b752f061779744ecb0fce4e0f93324f/2e318b1c9fb858aa67213cbcc50a55f5/AD_4nXcxqTqFqV7pzp4ayc7bEAhR4ozWOi8hTN2xlTNBAUMCLB0lsDXZ9bdzlz1ZEY5IAhMfjsqEaT8Z9mXYbiCtyZSXN4tza6_Lq7S7AeEwaNCshx_IakPZFJUnMHW3ICmV0mG9pS5zclYUcaHxJRVyBjDKUJ3H.jpeg)

To create high-ranking content that demonstrates E-E-A-T:

- Provide plenty of detail and genuinely helpful information
- Answer commonly asked questions
- Use an expert author and cite their qualifications in an author bio
- Display badges for any industry accreditations you have
- Include data and stats from reputable sources, with links
- Provide answers to PAA questions

### Optimizing for Navigational Keywords

Optimizing for navigational keywords **helps searchers find your business more easily**.

- Highlight your brand name on your site, in your homepage’s [H1 tag](https://www.semrush.com/blog/h1-tag/), and across key pages
- Maintain clear menus and an updated [sitemap](https://www.semrush.com/blog/website-sitemap/)
- Create a [Google Business Profile](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-my-business/) to verify your business

These measures increase visibility in navigational queries. For example, when you Google “Semrush,” you’ll see that the Semrush website is the first organic search result:

### Optimizing for Commercial Keywords

Optimizing for commercial keywords can **help guide customers toward a purchase**.

To optimize for commercial keywords, create product or service pages with detailed information and high-quality images. Write the content to address searchers’ needs.

For example, Adidas ranks highly for the commercial keyword “running shoes.” Their “women’s running shoes” page includes helpful content alongside a range of running shoes available for purchase.

This content answers frequently asked questions and provides information that helps shoppers make informed decisions. It also signals to search engines that the website is reputable and helpful.

![Section on Adidas Women's Running Shoes featuring product descriptions and FAQs](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/05/04/05047c054850269372a09af62b4f1e9c/a5d9c0451a4822e7213a1f6678709bc7/AD_4nXcrHQB0UyoDVz8273muXm1rzuDsmgn9deKWBNpZ2jvMj6XhelgOtEdA04jPYwPtjzbJrSp_S9HR8TGNUyr55iJFj0FWdjkQvEXd_IwEaBl22x6uSZuM--8ZoNttlkjZx5mTWABHK7m0xOomOWWTW9ap56U.png)

### Optimizing for Transactional Keywords

Optimizing for transactional keywords focuses on **providing a direct way to buy or convert**.

When people search for transactional keywords, they are ready to buy. To optimize for these keywords, make purchasing quick and easy.

Place the purchase option near the top of the page that targets your chosen keyword.

For example, BitPay ranks highly for the keyword “buy crypto online.” Its high-ranking page is simple and features a form to start buying crypto on the first screen.

![BitPay page for instant cryptocurrency purchases with a UI to select currency amount, cryptocurrency, and payment method](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/be/9d/be9df6763f1caa35c97ffaa1f071fd5c/853a6c0311ac98d63f90d29583f645ff/AD_4nXf2MexRKuJ4aFRp4aj2244OIO-zpj139YlEIBNS5iKw0KULnzhm8AEC65sYhgajkZdTVudfd596NyrMgKTyGH9nxVvuXL5AQ8btv9-huZe3bpc0RWQc3AFtGne863xAp2H42ElnKBovVl_4a2LbjyNkEhH8.png)

Include the target keyword in the title tag, H1 tag, and, if possible, the page URL. Reassure customers with reviews, professional certifications, and secure payment badges.
