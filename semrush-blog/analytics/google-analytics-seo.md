# How to Use Google Analytics for SEO: 6 Best Practices

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) lets you track your content performance. To uncover data you can use to improve your website.

In this guide you’ll learn how to use Google Analytics for SEO. So you can get better results from your SEO efforts.

But first, you need to do a bit of preliminary work.

## Set Up Your GA4 Account to Collect the Right Data

The first step is making sure you’re collecting the right data. With enhanced measurement events and Google Search Console (GSC).

### Start Using Enhanced Measurement Events

Google Analytics records visitor data as “events.” And enhanced measurement events are a type in [Google Analytics 4](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-analytics/) that provides more comprehensive SEO data.

They do this by recording visitor activity like external link clicks, scrolls, and site searches.

Here’s how you can make sure you’re collecting this enhanced data:

First, click on the gear icon in the bottom left of the screen to access the “Admin” section. Then, click on “**Data streams**” under “Data collection and modification.”

![GA4 dashboard with 'Admin', 'Data collection and modification', and 'Data streams' highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/95/a5/95a544837a5225191a5edf8dcf6f0854/f3e6cfdafe047effb83c6b2ea80ae888/vx0Kfjean_rKuu9WxB5nC2_5wG1Exas-U8NEQZmiv-oU2Hnhr8ZHwUN3khxDS2KB5LmRbKQoeqGNFic7p7Kjf4geu6QUhpQj1VqiMcjvhFQM1xbkaPa9ZwxERH8yIaotDOxLzQ5SJJFLb0WCl3MNm9E.png)

And click on the data stream for your website.

![Google Analytics web data stream within the google analytics admin section.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/74/ea/74ea5e65041d91598dca9c99acae8674/fed349bc3bd34ae0a4fd58a0619cd047/6ve5H6Szh6Tvl1eu8269BJP6KLM1ggBwzOrbRDabxgIcEPUh6siwJB8kBBl-feUPiIEaIZaqBli2ip0hVleB17gm250twDbmUhNABRs02oZbHcvJWJazdoUHPt9PFjuTMUW62xPCUy8nEocNYmYS1aQ.png)

In the “Events” settings, click the toggle beside “Enhanced measurement” to turn it on. Then, click on the gear to review your individual settings.

![Google Analytics data stream settings with enhanced measurement toggle highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1f/c0/1fc03aa0092bed9262b07c6b970bf8e3/d6fa0d0d37a19f6aa68d29c7bec88613/y3uHqCzLL0qCI-REIpD7IehAKmuQvl_0I8KoQ5VvtnT9owD-xcN_2PP41e18EJdiTCYdjoSTw0g9H2N_6WiheAY3yGpE9RM7ITq0-8pi96fOxqE-5_jVtB4PaEgjCk82f1S_0pfCLyqeZDl8MrjUaXE.png)

Make sure that “Site search” is toggled on. We’ll use site search data to gather SEO insights later.

![Enhanced measurement events in Google Analytics with 'Site search' row highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/9a/fb/9afba4181f24ce16a393dde43a53f0db/cf0c8c956c82c707497fab8db86e4d8b/SNYlm-GyPj6q6c8Of2i5B8TCfOS7lV1sVObqA0Y03rG302J8CAckSS7OyeSSmLO61VXKG6KV9Zd69TTXGCNULqKyqbIEZYcJz6Nbiv9JDjlmiiBEisxpqNgnSo-4Vgrn7ZXJa9sgbOpjAlYLDkhtDQk.png)

### Connect Your Google Search Console Account

[Connecting GSC to Google Analytics](https://www.semrush.com/blog/connect-google-search-console-analytics/) lets you see which search queries drive visitors to your website. And view your site’s top-ranking landing pages.

To connect GSC to GA4, click the gear icon to go to the “Admin” section.

Then, click on “**Property settings**” > “**Product links**” > “**Search Console links**.”

Click the “**Link**” button.

![Link button highlighted on Search Console Links page.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/1f/86/1f8628e5bf1e736d4bdb36bbbcbb3f0d/d03319becaf7840c8c68febc190ab0a6/zrUP-hSn0jq1MNHvZvrQAXg6MmjCW8gyL9QIulGlrYzyRDcO1jCT2vb2H-dBsg4Gb7KaoX4EdFKR4XPaCqwAxr8gF-UF-XykyenGhzR2OyzBpXpS44LM-VPEFc2Fuy71p7-8S8OSf7hbS6NFr1Wl43M.png)

And then click “**Choose accounts**” to select your Search Console property.

You’ll see a list of Search Console properties that you’re a verified owner for.

Choose the correct property using the checkbox. Then click “**Confirm**.”

![Property selected and Confirm button highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/9b/1e/9b1e61226e0b9727bcbd10f314123762/bfb5fab778dfbbbbdd21f412eed475e8/tiUW2-dJdOK-OQejJK9IMElFFUeUgXdBLcQSa2K2z8T3TkPall8-PzySwLUWwfcExeKC8KbGUFEnjLm2RxJplLDo9N6c6smuo-eIwgOLOSbOOBYexg9P-HmFWu5RNmBGBjNxg0oQIu_6O3b313gtcWk.png)

Click “**Select**” next to Web Streams.

![Web Streams row highligted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/9e/c4/9ec4e57988b26a2f411fbe6757d56b8b/bd7e608d39dc3218111449a9b9c778c0/zcdw6kXRiC3RuP9ROiRE7Z8uhI6-IkT2lNFUxdyWp66DpNDBHUFAmWrO_94klB-TN5-S7S6WgSEsLHnT9PHrpgqoAFjOBu9AbsQlJ05QFBAFeQKLkRISzwmse3AYPvp629Zq4pwf4NDwwi7gzMgSgKI.png)

And finally, click “**Submit**.”

![Review and submit step with Submit button highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e9/b1/e9b1f81db430549dbef6e0a9d0bafa1b/d49ebc311a71a2ef5627650d712a808d/JbGtoy9XJEXJp5cDN-HR_9jz6JFEk95xsjnEjTpDMSXEHg1LNc27aBD23PUG5SWXBMenbaqK7GaGtuoSXPEw2t7T2EsqXHCCFu0-nMS1Ae9SgyJAq26md8jKjip2ibTNvUtfRidLQlLxu_BnE66Z9SU.png)

## 6 Best Practices to Using Google Analytics for SEO

### 1. Discover Keywords Visitors Search For on Your Site

Most websites have search functionality. Like this:

![Semrush blog search page with search button in navigation enlarged and highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/88/aa/88aab1ffe61518578df48dc4d02e9613/e06f3ae9015d20c2521a081cb53529bf/fTbcJQMG3aUdo74QPEuujAaMnHz2OBkcn-_VZXdbN-64mcMZac69VT0ZCcxfX1RjzV9n8agomr85SnQT8ZTYSMAHiPfgFNBBl2xaNFZGui-_zTwvXhyvSd-ckWTrI3cYH_ukEhKSI0Y2Aq03LK6ZKuo.png)

Google Analytics can track what your website visitors are typing into that search bar. So you can optimize or create content to more directly address those queries.

To use Google Analytics for SEO content ideas like this, you need to modify an existing report.

Go to “**Reports**” > “**Engagement**” > “**Events**” within the left-hand navigation.

![Engagement menu expanded and Events options highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/57/04/5704e401f78baaceada6d1756d21289c/a2d616955e6ebe104d097a6e814c7bb0/lH6n8_84wdJOj9DEvy1_rjfdOEGugqkHzCGuT6m45GEWWSC7wHXhwA0toEfTigPsVJqrJA8YIOyVJUEra4D_T8uHdppg1t4MVvyngeo7Pz6YjcrIydmv2d1m-xNJBmjI_rq_8kXVZM8_pCR45mjP06E.png)

You now have a list of keywords your visitors actually use on your site.

 Let’s take it a step further and look into those search terms. To see whether they’re viable content ideas.

Let’s say GA4 shows you that your visitors are searching your site for “looker studio." But you don't have any content on the subject.

You might decide to begin writing about Looker Studio since it's interesting to your visitors.

But what specific topics within that theme have the best keyword potential?

You can find relevant topic ideas in just a couple of clicks with our [keyword research](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordmagic/) tool.

Type in “looker studio” and click “**Search**” to generate a list of keywords.

![Semrush Keyword Magic Tool start with 'looker studio' in input field and Search button highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/d0/18/d0187a208dceef68faa06fbfaa35ac87/71de62dd59b8a52996e2644204334e47/aYvP3ttrND0_UrQc-Bl450c6gtkNntbyBFGDR7od0HmCGbCWPXS36k8byTvGfKOqiUuS1RFqrheko8yTwlK_83bWmpHg_ujkh_i0nZnha7vGS7rngsBxv0ryg4QGnkPTD4R_aP5FrBJ7pBz-KyfyzMw.png)

Look through the options and create your own list with keywords that have the following:

- **Appropriate** [**search intent**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-intent/)**:** Search intent (“Intent”) is the motivation behind any search. Make sure the search intent of the keyword aligns with the goals you have for the content you plan to create.
- **High** [**search volume**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-search-volume/)**:** Search volume (“Volume”) is the estimated number of times a keyword is searched each month. Look for a keyword with a volume of at least 100.
- **Low** [**keyword difficulty**](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-difficulty/)**:** Keyword difficulty (“KD %”) is a percentage that indicates how easy or difficult it is to rank for a keyword. Look for keywords with a score of 49% or below.

![Intent, Volume, and KD columns highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/78/f2/78f2329f1f7e77953d239860927e10ac/b671af1a128dce56de09cd207a7cbcc1/nyKQ0-8TjH-FAAhVtHZ1SzuDPtBSbxGDswnLZZ0aN-bj15sCF-hJqBgRZJAcUEBjTPXzsQ8cunYmpn_S_k30dcH56VzNeM-4jfL8bNH3vH_Ny63MokqNfSYFdJuEPlyU8K0vZpqb29dm2M2DkQveJLg.png)

Use your keyword list to create your new pieces of content.

### 2. Find Declining Content to Refresh

Most websites have content that used to perform well but doesn’t anymore.

And small changes to those pieces—such as updating outdated information or [better optimizing for on-page SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/on-page-seo/)—can drastically improve SEO performance.

They may be underperforming now because they’re outdated, they’ve been outperformed by a competitor article, there was an algorithm update, etc.

For example, our “[What Is SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-seo/)?” article started losing traffic in January of 2022.

So, we updated and optimized it later that year. Which helped the article perform even better than it did before—as you can see in [Organic Rankings](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/organic/).

![Semrush Organic Rankings report for updated article with two arrows pointing to spikes in traffic.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8b/16/8b1642bb4e27caab4feff1f4d4f6676b/aef31a23e5815b7514beeb50f5b9a677/2N4kQ-D6CfeJW1h-dc_tLKE5CUiMtmghiZkAf0ZoXIc08D-Jy-hAFNWlWg4cn93XJX1aZzzXyWfqjXstYZ1qhvu1m_6X-rq4zKRZt1qUiDhwYdB-Q9f3OZlrrnXtR50l7Ox7zzGn38UIiMA1LHJZFB0.png)

Finding these underperforming pieces is a great way to use Google Analytics for SEO.Then, you can plan content updates and get your top-ranking pieces back on track.

Here’s how to do it.

Go to “**Reports**” > “**Search Console**” > “**Google organic search traffic**.”

![Google organic search traffic menu option highlighted within reports navigation.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/00/56/0056bd0953b13d9d9b8cb402240d3593/977250523a47642d5e40ae684b3402c4/rQK-Ygqj5W0rH0UdR3fiiT4Qx655CvT8fGzyyuyKkddZZD5XZLKMJNafOGKcI8h3EECHDVRdeihxp2-H4aTk78RWLVZuJcZwq1y1yG1yU_EszIfjwq3symAmh5aS6oKTsw50o7S7QI19Y0WETye2mg0.png)

Next, look at your content’s performance over a period of time. By clicking on the dates in the upper right corner of your screen and setting your desired range.

![Google organic search traffic report with dates highlighted in the top right corner of the screen.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/6c/96/6c960d0db52e70079a6023eff96c82f5/edc819d9d6026720f6f4ed467254e2d1/F5VaYtItE7PblHTq22tqkIyBeRYkcVwghz_SWoYZT17uOOYzEe53kcJ6lIfa-pfoG4WFyNWMThId4TpiGXRvVjiwDPjwOgpH24ezLj5y6CsuQfgGKTql0BpgPX5gQ0Bqv6u5fA2HQR8FpZhkQ5Mw1-o.png)

We recommend looking at content performance over the last six months. And comparing it with the previous six months by clicking on the toggle beside “Compare.”

To do this, click on “**Custom**” and choose the most recent six-month period. And compared it with the previous six months.

When you’ve selected your dates, click “**Apply**.”

![Google Analytics date range selector with the Compare and Apply buttons highlighted.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/13/d9/13d97192fc4595bf94032e7e59f14de1/6be99ffdc1f84b85ab437cdd1a19e443/Er4__vUqULEHrf28a1V5JFmlji2gllo8GzU5VmKZCy6xRfqT9Dr2-wVi1uYdoKRnjzwf2TV2A5LK0FsP-541ojN_GxQm1B0WA9e41qV_bR609za-yPl5zNtKGpINF0viIvBHodi6QqUr1XRWTMQT7f0.png)

Now, you should see your top-ranking landing pages, stats for both time ranges you selected, and the percentage change between the two.

Look at the “Organic Google Search average position” column to see how your rankings have changed. The report below shows a website with improved rankings—average position went from 18.36 to 15.17.

Sort the table to display pages with the most clicks from search results by clicking the arrow next to “Organic Google Search clicks.”

![Report sorted by Organic Google Search clicks.](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/5e/2c/5e2c7602402e47643187a9c0f45895a5/dd968553fa1d58157ccf964bf5ef5e46/geQm2OrbYguuLz_SksOECFXgNJ9I-7cflVc4xRrUxL83av3sVq-Uz33OonyxjJEp8RPKqfhXP6irfmk-xHpWASfKln4qQGcY_yOwef4jUJHG9BvqsYoXdNSfrcherlOHQ_JvQjeMNe1qplxl3KMXl0w.png)
