# 30 Attention-Grabbing Call to Action Examples

## What Is a Call to Action (CTA)?

A call to action (CTA) is a visual prompt—usually text or a button—that directs a user to take a specific action. Like “Buy Now” or “Subscribe.”

[Calls to action](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-a-call-to-action/) appear all over the web. Like this clothing website that encourages people to shop:

!["Show Now" call to action highlighted](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/2f/c7/2fc7ca169dd58d9d2be4f44299045f72/3446c4b2ed23e733e4dc37684798c4e1/LW8SNj_rAMlOql1ZfFXqGvlLdgWp0SeNHPTTfBkjRBMp7wXODPYlOj2QlLErNtM9_EPeHBsT5yYKdVYaV87r1CLJwdDbB6i_lcn8UMEGPPBpb26yA1hJRz0-0TtGqdlMd78x9Xd3Cl8NYnlgROsR5zQ.png)

And on social media. Like this Facebook ad. It asks people to download a report:

!["Get report," and "Download" CTAs highlighted in Coveo's Facebook ad](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/55/da/55dacf2f4d10e9563c2acf52d2e495c2/9655a1d5333a03677988c788e970acde/-E8Hc-5I634aNtwTuvCMoLTXNJXwzmnTN4_rNTxRY-VxDyt_qJZYD2dT-RTgreWj_5f7-8S_fRH1cF-3H0QzOw7faO-UG1_G95bEYZZ-qVF8ORN0g4biNqCFJgfU7aNXr5eAO4U6w0Dlg65xLq4H_ow.jpeg)

You can even spot CTAs in search ads. Here’s one that asks people to call:

!["Call us" CTA in Summers PHC's search ad](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/ef/05/ef056bd210feb83f4d9de9f5813fec3c/e7361fcef421337b7d84c99cf938c817/LUm7FLfkgrc57UEoXcBdJd0EFfYpFIhdcg7O0QNLawGv0SkUvN2a3du-tfonySW1oq3oTbIwNfp5LegrxyG7kwiAKgC7MHhNoT_2TbwVBMjNuWOhylibDt2m4hkkn_ERbW-tbhJzUa4avUjBtHII56Q.png)

Ready to get inspired? Let’s take a look at 30 incredible call to action examples, sorted by type.

## Informational CTA Examples

Informational CTAs lead to pages that provide additional information on your products, services, or business.

Like a product page. Or an “About Us” page.

They typically include phrases like “Learn More,” “Read More,” or “Explore.”

Let’s dive into some attention-grabbing examples.

### 1. Mailchimp

[Mailchimp’s CTA](https://mailchimp.com/solutions/email-marketing-platform/) gives users a glimpse of what they’ll discover by clicking the link. In this instance, the invitation is to “Explore 300+ Integrations.”

![Mailchimp's "Explore 300+ integrations" CTA](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/48/d6/48d65db2f76137431fcec2874d42bb40/6c4d39b0dd07c54ef617601b5263028e/LvSL6OnhnUuUJU_LjUJyDbha027y9VCY3FXPg8wSk5rc2drlN-UcNyamkD_pEnW8DlFQt_e1kRDoIR2H-U-N4drxhc3t5foB_fLUCWnfC1fSTnVEq2u2-l1KznpHN0gYpphQ3KuJlJTKy2NpYdTE8kw.png)

Using a number emphasizes Mailchimp’s extensive integration capabilities. And reassures users that adding Mailchimp to their tech stack should be a pain-free experience.

So, when writing your informational CTAs, see if you can include numbers. Here are some examples:

- **To emphasize credibility:** Read reviews from # customers
- **To emphasize thought-leadership:** Access # expert guides
- **To emphasize accomplishments:** View # completed projects

### 2. Apple

[Apple](https://www.apple.com/) uses a CTA in their [Google search ad](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-advertising/) that asks people to “Explore the lineup” to learn about the various computers they offer.

![Apple's "Explore the lineup" CTA in Google search ad](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/62/72/6272746c88b28015d587346205304f06/9b1501a4cfd1eef437456c7e4901a482/L-ezSiSpacoSGGUPAdeeUFcXcXTCXoxoxblixo-Wdj3UHafTVMusjvh47IUuDZ6Z9AaQtNHRL36_MoX25M4GOhmu9iXBySv7Wf7M3DJG9EivqZAetXyrgheBDP-wDSrbSfc3VWOhzUoqTdmDElZehRw.png)

You can get plenty of CTA inspiration by reviewing your competitors’ ads.

Here’s how.

Use Semrush’s [Advertising Research](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/adwords/positions) tool to pull all your competitors’ search ads over the years. Enter their URL and click “**Search**.”

!["monday.com" entered into the Advertising Research tool search bar](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c4/e8/c4e816a8fc733d07966c43abb5baafc9/81cdd5cd48dfb347af27e63c9a5c8f7d/_DCe3qSjiizpo1R6vez3AajLxcQA8rv-8OKlzCK8D2QgnCRqp5xgDMeVf3xzfk54MT33znmcYomk5yctsk_ed3gIXFML3YTr7In_whU0h52H3oPyoofbjqQM0B1_2v12ETAYBJVukVGvnmAQIVeWTy0.png)

Then click the “**Ads Copies**” tab to view ads. (Change the date if you want to view past ads.)

!["Ads Copies" tab in Advertising Research tool](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/4c/30/4c306ff816bf4bdbe648f9df3a6ee1f5/557bb6d3104d99b37d0ed97d67e75b9b/A7lr9xaqNP1aBqYoJcdZBY7WmR6dm94LOjpyJ1yz6VdlNC-s825_p_s-0houRhxqsmUxHVHEcpVHrHIXmD6QU7mpa65V0rVrO098nA4KkyzVuTwA-l7tXRMc2w-DWtA3YcnRoc4MXw4ImEi_p7oZ2tM.png)

Go through these and get inspiration for your own CTAs. Consider noting what your competitors are advertising and how they frame their CTA.

For example, if they advertise a free trial, do they mention how long the trial is?

When writing your CTAs, use what you learn from your competitors to come up with a few different options. Then test to see which ones perform best.

### 3. Sakuraco

Subscription box company [Sakuraco](https://www.facebook.com/wearesakuraco/posts/pfbid0XLjDNJWmmMza3eGsu1L249dGX9wW5Px5CCTWusTEyFwWAjPvhgZDn72KbPrBgScAl) has multiple CTAs in their Facebook ad:

1. Learn more (button)
2. Find out more (link)
3. Discover Japan’s Authentic Sweets

![Sakuraco's "Find out more," "Discover Japan's Authentic Sweets," "Explore new authentic Japanese snacks every month with Sakuraco," and "Learn more" CTAs highlighted in an Facebook ad](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/26/ee/26eefa00acf0dd95bfe56fd62553b884/d4692f0278208d2b5e581396c3f04465/B-uUCY0WLG21LUD68A9Rs-tic1_k9MLK333OzStDBrp6Iuj0i6uWweuU2nnI16AMKGPtxDrLnuza82Jpora1assfK1FGoP4jcC2alJN4Y0SNmCcGeO_Cdf5T9ZaDCxpB57tENF0-o73bAZEw1pVcZ4Q.jpeg)

Although Sakuraco has multiple CTAs, they all follow a similar pattern. And that pattern encourages users to click for more information.

Experiment with multiple CTAs when writing ads for Facebook. Like a CTA in the headline and in the primary text.

But like Sakuraco, keep all your CTAs related to the action you want the user to take. Otherwise, they may get confused. And they won’t know what you’re asking them to do.

### 4. Tiffany & Co

Jewelry company [Tiffany & Co](https://www.tiffany.com/) prompts visitors to follow their diamond’s journey with their CTA.

!["Follow Your Diamond's Journey" CTA highlighted on Tiffany & Co's website](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/3b/6a/3b6a42759b6cfb9de133d71c51525de8/f374c4de568f24afe8bf34b7448ff3d5/GK_S8_ookzKZ2z67LMZ2hX8WQ7jRlomiVD3aS2SLJ__NPDTykbPEwQWZtcryd06ECooHjCliInkVv6i7qCLOG_zrArJMRBNwAjzdnWgzgI2oiQeDAPjq1qM5tMuW1YcHkEPl3ok8IvJY6Kwkem3pxjs.png)

This CTA may work better than a standard “Learn More” as it tells users exactly what they’ll learn by clicking the link. And it’s directly related to the products they sell.

To write a CTA like this, ask yourself, “Why should someone click this link?”

Then, mold the reason into a CTA.

### 5. Warby Parker

Eyeglass supplier [Warby Parker](https://www.warbyparker.com/) infuses their personality into their CTA. Instead of instructing users to “Learn More,” they’ve taken a different approach and asked users to “Peruse the palette.”

![“Peruse the palette" CTA highlighted on Warby Parker's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0b/03/0b03d90cc8ebc94352c59d345bdecfce/d7471cb6180f299c5f98fa9310675945/YzjcwSe8eID8iKWrsUOKstDeFH3tgV8gpB9DtvPkmG2vjOcDQmyNq0oH58vW4xnvBSwjlLfBiUcs8PzpIF-VOzepySqNH0zuQHWj7cNEUy96fw9Nc0foM-SdWtRohdltx7l8s8b_BxwIAJzKxmV1KIU.png)

When writing your CTA, think of your [brand’s voice](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-to-define-your-tone-of-voice/) (the personality you use to communicate with your audience).

Then, like Warby Parker, dress up your CTA with words and phrases unique to your brand.

### 6. Starbucks

Coffee company [Starbucks](https://www.starbucks.com/responsibility/people/) uses a descriptive CTA that tells users where they’ll go after they click. Which is a page with examples of inclusion and diversity.

!["Find examples" CTA highlighted on Starbucks's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/8c/fa/8cfa854b6ee13c4b418fc42d31706b04/4a92c3f44134b7483914cc023484e1a9/Cwjyt8h4rVHxxxb-z1Qf-QYWlX-qS04btVQkUizHygfqSg8NG9Hfy-1WP_XoqMmn5JNlszRwSNoMVT9PZ0QzKezYtYPgFQqoLqEfqmfQMKaJQDtTsqxOqekd7iiZ5LElNGNFkv1eYV3Tm4Ygmglu9K4.jpeg)

This type of CTA can drive traffic to a page that compiles resources. Like blog posts, case studies, testimonials, or examples.

To drive traffic to these types of pages, try CTAs like:

- Discover what our customers say
- Read our case studies
- Browse our blog

## Lead Generation CTA Examples

Lead generation CTAs collect leads from your website by asking users for their email. These CTAs usually say something like “Subscribe” or “Sign Up.”

Here are some examples of lead generation CTAs to inspire your own:

### 7. Reformation

Clothing company [Reformation](https://www.thereformation.com/dresses) has two CTAs in their opt-in form:

1. “Sign up to see for yourself”
2. “Give us your email address. Promise we won’t spam.”

!["Sign up to see for yourself," and "Give us your email address. Promise we won't spam" CTAs highlighted in Reformation's opt-in form](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/2c/f3/2cf30c885996c2a821c401f7f1f1dcf2/2100bd3d90fe8a09296e4a2c630ece60/gc0EmwU5l3wU913e9B5udoQYubX8Um9YGqqJIx45w-4YGbTDMQkP93fZ-M2jtAZrjdpW75sTh7cX0KLdOADWWlbOLR1_biHcjbrgoYXWctwJwIFCTXpEKqtvj_YmUG1QvCKB-tgmBqtFnaRo4CU2Q2s.png)

The first CTA piques the user’s curiosity.

You can incorporate a CTA like this in your lead generation forms by telling the user why they should sign up for your email list.

The second CTA tries to eliminate any doubts the user might have. In this case, the doubt is that the emails will be spammy.

Get to know your subscribers and identify their initial reservations when subscribing to your list. Then, create a CTA that addresses their concern to put future leads at ease.

### 8. Boom! By Cindy Joseph

Makeup brand [Boom! By Cindy Joseph](https://www.boombycindyjoseph.com/products/boomstick-trio) uses the CTA “Join The Club” to ask people to sign up for their email list.

The last line is also a simple but effective pun. This playfully adds a sense of urgency, suggesting that “joining the club” is long overdue.

![“Join The Club” CTA on Boom! By Cindy Joseph website](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/b5/7d/b57d8c76de18992c1c6aa63f0f1899b1/84ddf34023b0c7819099a531e026cf84/CGAKOgQDr4o0PbLMdrS--4ztgen_B98hSmD9tx9OXaCvmW2Fed8SGClBPMLhxjxWDT3EnN6JipW0yoHFhL90XqhVvJac37J2v4ElcgOGAW-Os-OARy1dTU4kUnYL-IQbk6FS70znJ6s8hMlWPGEs1AA.jpeg)

This CTA fosters a sense of community. If you want to give your email list a community-feel, try a CTA like this:

- Join the inner circle
- Be part of our network
- Become an insider
- Join our community

### 9. Brian Kurtz

Copywriter [Brian Kurtz](https://www.briankurtz.net/) takes a different approach to the usual “Subscribe” CTA. His CTA button explicitly tells subscribers what they get in return for giving him their email address.

In this case, they get a document with direct marketing secrets.

!["Send me the secrets" CTA on Brian Kurtz's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/5b/48/5b489117ecb2c317cc6607bf65f5b26a/e77a6aafd58dce22b3159a9e844eb985/6F_fSZj_aSRv6MvyLXY3pGWvkF8aM1YGtONpABUutIYwVfQc9sLgLU6N6FH2ZT7qFp7q7ZRWEOLeBvfdKMjx3FaUyqb38S0U7y5kxOTg_stOM-Trd3oE3moFU7z-iMz_gUIHMa-JRZkLNg6TqFRE0hw.jpeg)

Consider replacing your “Subscribe” CTAs with something like Brian’s CTA. And tell the user what they get in return.

Try something like:

- Send me the [item]
- Give me the [item]
- I want the [item]
- Get the [item]

## Form Submission CTA Examples

Form submission CTAs require users to fill out a form—usually to contact you or book a meeting. These CTAs might read “Contact” or “Submit.”

But you can get more creative with it.

Here are some examples of effective form submission CTAs:

### 10. Impact

Marketing training company [Impact](https://www.impactplus.com/services/schedule-now) offers free coaching sessions for those who fill out their form.

Rather than a generic CTA like “Submit,” they use “Book Your Free Coaching Session.”

![“Book Your Free Coaching Session" CTA on Impact's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/d7/72/d772531ba4cb72288de4b2a241f25576/09308114776456b188b54867d486bef4/CE1Za9wbZn1sehf9cfKRTpJ_3xmDhYIMc18GizGujRoB3pbSqW7_TOhI1skCnGmdqJLTcrnWxSEZIpsOxaUnS1SYZSAvD-4mkdo6icBSSTYbT0cUv8oyQVlAwobchMaXrC1RY0wnV-_TWTRIpBpHq-E.jpeg)

This makes the purpose of the form clear. And users know this form isn’t for contacting and asking questions.

You can use a similar approach by writing a CTA that tells users what happens if they fill out a form. Like “schedule a consultation” or “book a meeting.”

### 11. Choice Screening

Background screening company [Choice Screening](https://www.choicescreening.com/contact-choice-screening) uses a CTA that tells users that filling out the form puts them in touch with a person. As opposed to an automated reply or a support chatbot.

!["Talk to a human" CTA on Choice Screening's contact form](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/17/c9/17c9b9db2f15798d435cb843e6ac0414/94d7d66d2f9855577d2d2cedd132c086/maIQZhiV01BGW8b2N65OAWSfG7FdGRaSrKtpqAigoG-MXOwWOMjLwGqSWpWv69BC8f9kW6GZhulZY5KNWGgq9IZK6AA8sQinZLgUKDR2YEDvZBqGeXAXVpty9z44DDxfSTtV2MWNGu-2KL8yVHmt6Ko.png)

Use your form submission CTA to highlight who users can expect to speak with after they submit your form.

Try CTAs like “speak to a specialist” or “talk with our team.”

### 12. Zendesk

This call to action button example from support company [Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/contact/) reaffirms who the user is contacting.

!["Contact Sales" CTA on Zendesk's contact form](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/0e/a7/0ea788b765b77a5ff5946000d4331156/fb35a090e8b21a0435b6953e0fe49918/Iuk9PxmhfMz66VXcNZDVqT_31VL7cv--NmhlxIOjsR4twRcR78s_fqIsySzoNYK-HxuTpitvwA4UIxG5hCuZjBAQqIqg96e4KDtVSvp7EhooxCQC0OGldOAUPmvdZI7MFMVc8a2dw0fh2GaAVc-W9aE.png)

If this form was titled “Contact Form” and the button said “Send” rather than “Contact Sales,” someone might fill it out hoping to reach support.

However, this CTA eliminates confusion. And ensures people send their messages to the right place.

Try this approach if your site has multiple forms that connect to different teams.

## Engagement CTA Examples

Engagement CTAs aim to engage and interact with the user. They often involve actions like “Take a Tour,” “Watch Video,” or “Start Free Trial.”

Here’s how brands write attention-grabbing engagement CTAs:

### 13. Biteable

This CTA from video software solution [Biteable](https://biteable.com/explainer/) reads “Make an explainer video” instead of something like “Sign Up.”

This way, people know what they can achieve by clicking the button.

![“Make an explainer video” CTA on Biteable's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/36/ff/36ff5683a2613292e80862eb1f53b81a/4461a58540fe1e90c7ed9a986efd40d6/GjtBUJlBKcV_8f6AWAZT8Z5tbCu7xu3tg0XTQUSKlNNRA0ooIWifaE_IfzpTGJPhU4-nfdTB9-puOGIe2t0e4XBI3SLNDnS_AuvG9TM1TqOdXUO44scfZlrXoYTneV_L1Ntw8wEMdRA_KWSM2B_ADDg.png)

To create a CTA like this, tell people what they can do with your product or service if they click the button or link.

### 14. EventBrite

Event management website [EventBrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/l/sell-tickets/) highlights the benefits of their service with their CTA.

!["Grow your events" CTA on EventBrite's social sharing section](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/c9/c9/c9c9aa56fd059f6c8f83089e504746ed/10354bd83208c6c10c4bd69eea8929a4/r9AHqoz1yciWSQDW49e1tG6Bori0CDIETzfFZ0ylDk4WVVpu4l2_RGWLtms5eOyu8gnn4ypRio-njBqYFGTIiuGviI5RUed1332GAClXRa8_l9PEuf2Kp0OJEa7d0A8d_6We4EX4iUEKXmFmcodZxEs.png)

This CTA tells users that they can grow their events by signing up with EventBrite.

If you want to write a CTA like this, ask yourself what benefits users can realize by following through with your CTA.

Will they get more confidence? Grow their business?

After defining the benefit, use it as your CTA.

### 15. Duolingo

Here’s another benefit-focused CTA from the learning app, [Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/).

!["Get better at math" CTA on Duolingo's math site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/dd/b7/ddb76992974898f82e599aa7a5d31c42/5dca338d661b9998d1af32fdee17b663/AMp9EdPIJR2lSY8v5lUTnD8W316VgFbLENK6oR592OxFkOwpMJ3kGZblFEbaRgJe5Fak3XTLG5Pi5Vj7zBsEBDN7qYEVa_JGDx_oTG2thMY0cki9EpXp1xxY1haiBqoovO-EaaPm7kMMrOu7EFtK_vk.png)

Their CTA tells people that clicking the link will help them improve in math.

A benefit-focused CTA can be effective if the landing page aligns with the highlighted benefit.

In the CTA example from Duolingo, the button leads users to a page to download the Duolingo Math app.

### 16. Nike

This QR code call to action example from [Nike](https://www.nike.com/ntc-app) demonstrates how you can use CTAs in unique ways.

!["Scan this code to download the Nike Training Club App" CTA on Nike's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/e0/60/e0607c2f226def195163b8c5b67b60b2/971ae1170cb6b9dcb093bed08a3fa962/TLrBDeogkr6V2KnwvYpvlSRUXICOWq1Hypsg_xya_TbOqlrAOV-SEJsyagpwaTorGidHIxrcx53hmtrfzyT-ayjRh56VE1HmMIye6qUCm7Kye5hlTQsVIvdjXDchUKdKvQqVpG4vDhdWrMAPh9n9rkU.jpeg)

If you use QR codes on things like posters or menus, add a CTA near the code. Nike adds theirs underneath the QR code.

You could also experiment with adding your CTA above or beside.

Either way, your CTA should describe what happens when someone clicks the link attached to the code.

In this case, the QR code directs people to download an app (as Nike describes).

### 17. Kiva

[Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/) is a platform that connects lenders with borrowers. Their CTA tells readers they can “find a borrower” (people who need money for projects).

!["Find a borrower” CTA on Kiva's website](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/89/da/89dac8eaf8b32029b7a50acc3957595c/284af696099c4974b6b1a297ed37459a/KI9pHm2TGYKS4GY3yvbppWy1YxRFfIJnLF2Vm8ynt2dbgFsXmv5uhzarL3KLfCAK8dmTI1FuKvlLKNQDVWK9QHbTAkxTlnf1DQFXvL6Eo1_hOpzzkelI9yxuwy2CJYczq1SYBPylZBh-gDnGh-1Lb2c.jpeg)

To write a CTA like this, clearly and concisely describe what people can do after they click the link.

## Sales CTA Examples

Sales CTAs drive purchases and use phrases like “Shop” or “Buy Now.”

Let’s look at a few other options.

### 18. Crate&Barrel

Home decor company [Crate&Barrel](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/christmas/) replaces “Shop Now” with “Bring home joy” for their CTA.

![“Bring home joy” CTA on Crate&Barrel's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/11/89/118966c995f37d05df2c9eb8a0642f67/17d3d53f2b516fcf9a1f0323030bd704/WJ-ouN2eanyylFy5CjTAxQ6gjQeoWYX_9lc6GCI_xwLdoFZXBQaRPf4e1oeIOgYLj72Vwl-wZLmHCRm2JDV_yLkaRmwsp5iPJihZMljalAP9ifwXgcOU4HArMnt_iSofSLZcDX7FYnle_oTfgy4Fpj0.png)

Try replacing your “Shop Now” CTAs with something more descriptive. And tell users what they can get if they buy your product.

In Crate&Barrel’s example, shoppers can add joy to their homes with their decor. This is more appealing than simply saying something like “Go to shop.”

### 19. Wool and the Gang

Knitting company [Wool and the Gang](https://www.woolandthegang.com/) sells knitting kits. Instead of a CTA like “Shop,” they instruct users to “Choose a Kit.”

![“Choose a Kit” CTA on Wool and the Gang's site](https://static.semrush.com/blog/uploads/media/68/46/684647b01f5d79b13dbde8a3c488ca65/e89d5acc2947373827f0719a2e1e2d46/hp2VBcg7Tme8ldYCbFF6JJOSvZ1bYiQ78EBKDD_z0LeIN1oj47_aJxtJUKH5Y94aIvk1SOKh1NyOkOkodbyfQizYeFoqyMtEYNlkt3e_j7vy11vlW5BTmUWrBdz8786IV5i-sPHtQDKv1DeUztr76lU.png)

Adding “choose” to your CTA tells users they have various options. And they can find a product that suits their needs.

Try a similar CTA when directing users to pages with multiple products or various pricing packages.

### 20. James Wedmore

## How to Write Effective Calls to Action

The best way to write CTAs is to ask yourself what users get if they follow through on your CTA.

For example, if you sell language courses, your CTA could be something like “Become Fluent in French.”

You can also see what your competitors have used for their CTAs. Browse their websites (including their homepage, landing pages, and sales pages) and their ads to get inspiration for your own CTA.

To view your competitors’ ads, use the [Advertising Research](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/adwords/positions) tool.
